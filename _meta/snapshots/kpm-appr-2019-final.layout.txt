   How Are We Doing
   For the 2019 tax season, the department processed nearly two million tax returns; about 65 percent (1.3 million) were refund claims. This
   measurement is the average amount of time it takes from when the agency received a return until a refund was issued. The agency averaged 16
   days to refund in 2019, eight days longer than the target. In response to a desire to balance timeliness with fraud reduction, the Oregon
   Legislature modified the target to 16 days beginning in 2020.

   The primary factor that impacts return processing is whether or not a return is suspended. Suspended returns are diverted from automatic
   processing for manual review because errors exist or additional information is needed to process the return; either may indicate a potential for
   fraud. The following table provides information on average processing timelines overall, and for non-suspended and suspended returns.

                                                                      Average Days to Process Returns

                          Type of Return              Overall           Non-Suspended               Suspended                 Percentage of
                                                                                                                          Returns Suspended
                                  All                   16.3                    13.8                     66.2                       4.0%
                                 e-file                 15.3                    12.9                     68.2                       4.4%
                                 2-D                    20.2                    17.2                     54.2                       8.3%
                                Paper                   35.1                    32.1                     62.0                       9.8%
   This table illustrates that electronically filed returns that don’t suspend have the shortest average processing time of 13 days. E-filed returns are
   the most common filing method.

   Factors Affecting Results
   Aside from suspended returns, other factors within the agency’s control that impact return processing are:

           Business edits/rules on processing returns—the agency has over 200 rules that: incorporate statutory limits, correct math errors, match
           against third-party information, and allows the agency to request additional documentation. Some rules are system processed (math
           errors), while others require a manual review. Annually, business rules are improved to close known gaps and stop fewer legitimate returns.
           The fraud module in the agency's tax processing system uses analytics to identify potentially fraudulent refunds. The attributes for
           fraudulent returns have become so similar to legitimate returns that it’s difficult to differentiate between the two. Each year, the agency
           reviews fraud triggers to address known gaps and to make improvements. There will always be a need for manual review to combat fraud,
           but we are working to better identify those that the system can sort through.
           Early season refund hold—the agency does not issue refunds prior to February 15. This allows the agency time to load information
           submitted by employers (due January 31) and match it to the information reported by the taxpayer on their tax return.
           The number of seasonal staff hired to open mail, key paper returns, process correspondence, etc.

   How Are We Doing
   The agency fell short of the 90 percent target for tax returns filed electronically for the reporting period. A rate of growth of about one percent has
   become the norm for the past few years. The table that follows summarizes filing methods as a percentage of total returns filed for the 2019 tax
   season.

               Filing Method                 Count                  Percent
          Electronic                             1,765,490                 88.5%
          2-D Barcode                                 158,566                8.0%
          Paper                                         71,379               3.5%
                                                   1,995,435
   E-filed returns are generated by software and transmitted electronically so they are more accurate, less costly and quicker to process, and require
   less handling and storage than paper returns.

   The agency anticipates making continued incremental progress towards the 90% target during the 2019 –2021 biennium.

   Factors Affecting Results
   The agency takes several steps that are designed to encourage e-filing:

           Promotion—like the IRS, the agency discusses e-file in our publications and on our website, promoting its accuracy and typically quicker
           refunds.
           Ease of filing—the agency has a direct e-file system allowing any full-year-resident taxpayer to enter their return information into an online
           form and file the return directly with us for free, regardless of income level or return type.
           The agency also participates in the Free File Alliance. When taxpayers meeting certain income, age, veteran status, and return-type criteria
           they can e-file for free. The agency posts a list on our website of software companies offering free services.
           The agency has an e-file mandate for tax practitioners that matches the IRS mandate. There is no penalty for noncompliance, but the
           agency sends a reminder letter each year to those practitioners who didn’t file their client’s previous-year returns electronically.

   How Are We Doing
   The agency exceeded its performance goals for this measure in years 2015 to 2017, reflective of the intensive training required of nearly all staff
   as a part of the Core Systems Replacement (CSR) Project. The agency missed the increased target set by the legislature in 2018, in part due to
   the limited scope of the final implementation stage of the CSR Project. The figures for 2019 reflect the first year there was no required training
   related to the CSR Project as it’s now complete. Given this, the agency expected to miss the target.

   This measure allows the agency to prioritize supporting employees to keep pace with changing technology, policies, and processes. It also honors
   building a respectful and professional workplace, increases understanding for equity, inclusion, and diversity, and ensures adequate knowledge
   transfer and succession planning required for seamless function of mission critical operations. Training has also been identified as a driver for
   employee engagement in the last several engagement surveys. These factors make it an important internal measure; however, it is not an optimal
   measure of agency performance. Given this, the agency will recommend deletion of this measure in the future with the understanding that this will
   remain an internal measure maintained by the Human Resources Section and available upon request.

   Factors Affecting Results
   In the previous four years, the majority of agency staff were required to complete training on our new core system. This training requirement was
   critical to successfully implementing the system, so, funding for training was included in the project budget. Now that this project is complete, the
   agency's performance dropped to 37 percent.

   In an effort to ensure employee access to appropriate professional training, the agency purchased licenses for unlimited training on a number of
   topics. This maximized available training dollars. The agency also worked with local training providers to offer popular trainings at a discounted
   price. In addition, the agency focused on capturing training records in iLearn and encouraged employees and managers to create professional
   growth goals involving training to enhance knowledge, skills, and/or abilities.

   How Are We Doing
   This measure provides information on customers’ assessments of agency performance related to key drivers of customer satisfaction. While the
   agency recognizes that paying taxes may not make taxpayers happy, it’s important they rate their experiences with the agency as satisfactory.
   The agency’s customer service ratings increased in each category in 2019, marking the third straight year of improved performance.  Four of the
   categories had a 9 percent increase, with two (helpfulness and expertise) having the highest score of 94 percent. Availability of information lagged
   slightly at 89 percent, potentially requiring additional focus as it’s an indicator of self service.

   The data was collected from a survey. Taxpayers took the survey by telephone or online. About 98 percent of the surveys were by telephone. The
   responses were from taxpayers inquiring about several different tax programs, but predominantly from the personal income tax program.

   Factors Affecting Results
   The agency standardized the data collection processes for this measure. As a result, sample sized jumped from 1,875 in 2016 to more than
   23,000 in 2019. The agency also created a cross-agency customer service review group to support implementing improvements identified through
   this enhanced data collection process. The agency believes there is a relationship between timely processing of returns and satisfaction. Despite
   not meeting the target in number of days to process a refund, customer satisfaction has increased. The agency believes this is attributable to:

           Improvements made by the cross-agency workgroup.
           The agency increased the number of phone representatives available during processing season through opening the Fossil office and
           converting several positions to phone representatives in the main call center.
           Addition of new, separate, telephone lines for tax professionals and for corporations.
           Staff encouraging taxpayers to take the survey, increasing the sample size.

   How Are We Doing
   Our overall score of 81 is an increase over the 2018 score of 79. While performance increased from the previous year, it fell short of the target of
   90 set in the 2017 budget process.

   This measure aligns with the agency’s strategic priority to enhance taxpayer assistance. The goal is to provide self-help access for those who
   want it and provide effective one-on-one assistance where necessary (or desired). In practice, the agency believes this measure is too complex
   and weighted too heavily on timeliness rather than actually measuring the taxpayer’s ability to access the support they want or need. The agency
   is exploring alternatives with the hope of proposing an improved measure of taxpayer assistance.

   Factors Affecting Results
   The results of this measure are impacted by the performance of three components that form this composite measure. Each component is
   weighted:

           Call wait time. Calls with a wait time shorter than five-minutes made up 77 percent of total calls, up from 71 percent in 2018. Staffing
           increases were the primary driver of this improvement (weight: .4).
           “Where’s My Refund” inquiries. The percentage of “successful” inquiries made through phone or web applications was 85 percent, which
           reflects a 1 percent increase from 2018. Successful inquiries are defined as any response other than "not found,” which means the return is
           not in the system when the taxpayer asks. Typically, it’s because taxpayers didn't wait the suggested two weeks from when they file before
           requesting a status update (weight: .5).
           Customer service rating. The percentage of customers rating our service as "good" or "excellent" was 91 percent, up from 81 percent in
           2018 (weight: .1).

   While these three components are all important indicators of customer service performance, aggregating the results into a composite
   measure does not allow for flexibility or provide true insight related to taxpayer assistance expectations. Additionally, it is heavily skewed towards
   personal income tax (Where’s My Refund) rather than being agency focused.

    Appraisal Value Uniformity
    Actual                                                     28%                       29%                       18%                        20%                       15%
    Target                                                     20%                       20%                       20%                        30%                       32%

   How Are We Doing
   The reported metric is the measure of variance between the values from our site-specific appraisals and the values from our annual mass-
   appraisal program of processed taxpayer-filed industrial property returns. Performance for 2019 was 15 percent, well below the adjusted target of
   32 percent.

   Measuring the coefficient of dispersion (COD) is an appraisal industry standard metric for uniformity. The metric is reliant on market sale
   transactions. In the last five years, the agency hasn’t had a large enough sampling of usable sale transactions, so, the agency used site-specific
   appraisals to calculate results. The agency would ultimately like to eliminate this measure due to the small number of transactions. Substituting
   site-specific appraisals makes the measure performance appear more volatile than may be the case. Given this, the agency has reached out to
   our county clients through the Executive Committee of the Oregon State Association of County Assessors for input on a more meaningful
   replacement measure. The agency anticipates proposing a replacement measure once the results of this solicitation process have been received.

   Factors Affecting Results
   The agency is required to appraise Oregon’s highly complex industrial properties engaged in processing and manufacturing activities. These
   properties don’t sell often. When they do, there are many factors that make it difficult to compare the sales price to assessment roll values.
   Therefore, the limited number of sales transactions has decreased the reliability of the measure.

   Some properties have valuation issues including omitted property, taxpayer reporting errors (duplicates, age of assets, failure to report), and
   reclassification of assets. These issues create a greater separation between the value determined via our mass appraisal process and the value
   determined via our site-specific appraisal process, resulting in higher COD and greater deviation between RMV and the roll value.

   In 2017, the agency refined its business strategy and set a goal to appraise industrial sites on an eight-year cycle. Once our first regular appraisal
   cycle is complete, a more consistent COD trend should emerge. Through the implementation of our eight-year appraisal cycle, we should
   continue to see a reduction in the deviation between roll values and RMV; however, this cycle won’t be complete for another four years. Looking
   ahead, the agency anticipates proposing a more informative outcome measure for this program.

   The agency’s cost of funds for direct enforcement for fiscal year 2019 was $0.24. For this measure, lower performance is better. This represents a

   outcomes.

           difference in the measure’s performance. Corporation enforcement revenue dollars increased this fiscal year which caused the largest
           impact to the measure. This increase is directly tied to relatively few audit payments.

   How Are We Doing
   In 2018, our cost of assessments increased to $0.17. For this measure, lower performance is better. This represents an increase from the fiscal
   year 2017 cost of $0.15. This was due to decreased assessments in both the Corporation and Withholding sections. Assessed dollars decreased
   from $241.9 million in 2018 to $211.7 million in 2019. The audit and filing enforcement funding in our Legislatively Adopted Budget remained
   constant at $35.6 million in fiscal year 2018 and 2019.

   This measure is calculated using both audit and filing enforcement data. The filing enforcement portion includes “failure-to-file” data, which is a
   department estimate of owed revenues. This estimate is typically quite different than actual monies owed, which is determined when the taxpayer
   actually files. When the agency overestimates the amounts owed, performance on this measure will look more favorable. Sophisticated reviewers
   of this information could make this assertion, which makes this a less reliable measure. The agency anticipates completing a comprehensive
   assessment of its KPMs during the 2019–21 biennium. The goal is to find more suitable enforcement measures.

   Factors Affecting Results
   There are many factors that impact the cost of assessments. The Corporation Section has a lot of volatility in its assessment dollars in particular,
   which can cause a large amount of fluctuation.

   How Are We Doing
   Improving employee engagement has been a long-standing priority for the agency. In 2013, the agency contracted with the Department of
   Administrative Services to access an employee engagement measurement tool used by several other state agencies. This tool was used to report
   results from 2013 to 2018. In 2018, the Department of Administrative Services stopped offering access to the tool and the contract with the vendor
   wasn’t renewed. DOR was given a one-time extension to have access to the tool with reduced features: closed ended questions without key
   reporting capability. In 2019, the agency did not have access to this tool so an employee engagement survey was not completed. Given this, the
   agency would like to delete this measure and undertake an internal review to determine an appropriate replacement measure for this KPM.

   Factors Affecting Results
   At the request of the legislature, the Oregon Secretary of State (SOS) conducted an organizational culture audit in 2018. This audit provided
   agency leadership additional insights into employee engagement and overall morale. The agency has implemented actions based on the findings
   of the SOS audit with a focus towards increasing morale, engagement, and improving the overall culture of Revenue. The agency also hired a
   consultant to evaluate the current state of outcome-based management practices and policies within the agency. An outcome-based management
   approach is built on a foundation that looks to engage and empower employees at all levels of the organization. Given this, the agency
   will explore new ways to measure organizational health beyond employee engagement with the understanding that this information will be
   maintained as an internal measure by the Human Resources Section and available upon request.
