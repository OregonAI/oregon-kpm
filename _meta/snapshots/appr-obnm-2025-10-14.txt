How Are We Doing
The Board of Naturopathic Medicine has three full time staff - the Executive Director, an Investigator 2, and an Administrative Specialist 2.  The Board investigates every complaint received -
approximately 40 investigations per year.  The Board investigator is responsible for all aspects of the investigation - administrative tasks such as complaint intake, sending acknowledgement letters,
serving subpoenas for all relevant medical records, organizing and reviewing hundreds of pages of complex medical records, and then preparing, scheduling and conducting interviews of all licensees
and witnesses. The Board investigator is also responsible for summarizing the medical records, licensee and witness interviews, in an investigative report, which often involves creating multiple
exhibits (which are often 50+ pages of additional documentation) for Board review and deliberation. The Board investigator is also primarily responsible for ensuring licensees comply with their
probation conditions, which involves time consuming tasks such as, coordinating and reviewing licensee chart reviews, pulling quarterly PDMP records, and tracking compliance with other probation
conditions.  In January 2025, ED Baptista transferred some probation duties and tracking responsibilities to the AS2 to assist the Board investigator with his work load, allowing him more time to focus
on investigative work.  The ED also developed a probation case tracking system to more efficiently track both probation cases and on-going investigations; specifically the length of time an
investigation is open and tasks to be completed so that the ED may more effectively track the progress and assist with the completion of older and more complex investigations.  The average length of
time from complaint to completion of the investigation in calendar year 2024, was an average of 9.6 months, up from an average of 8.7 months the previous calendar year.
Factors Affecting Results
The Board has a small staff of three, including one Investigator 2.  However, as noted above, the one full time investigator is responsible for all aspects of the investigation - including the time
consuming administrative work of sending acknowledgement letters and subpoenas, organizing hundreds of pages of medical records, scheduling interviews with licensees and witnesses.   Each task
takes a considerable amount of time; up to four weeks from subpoena to receiving the medical records, reviewing hundreds of pages of complex medical charts and records to prepare to interview all
witnesses and every licensee under investigation.  Furthermore, Licensees under investigation are frequently represented by counsel, which often results in 2-3 week delays to coordinate schedules
actual target
for an interview date and time when all three can be present.  The Board investigator is also responsible for summarizing all medical records and licensee and witness interviews, in an investigative
report, which often involves creating multiple exhibits (which are customarily 50+ pages of additional documentation) for Board review and deliberation.   In 2024, eleven (11) investigations presented
to the Board resulted in discipline, up from five (5) in 2023.   The Board investigator also manages most aspects of licensee probation conditions, which involves time consuming tasks such as
coordinating and reviewing licensee chart reviews, pulling quarterly PDMP records, and tracking compliance with other probation conditions.  The uptick in Licensees on probation added to an already
considerable work load for the sole Board Investigator.  With the recent delegation of some administrative tasks and the Jan. 2025, implementation of a new probation and case tracking system, we
expect increased efficiencies, resulting in reduced time from complaint to investigation completion and achieving an actual average from complaint to completion closer to / on target in 2026.
