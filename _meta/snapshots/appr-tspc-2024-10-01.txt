How Are We Doing

Receiving a seventy-six percent (76%) satisfaction rating in this area is outstanding. The three (3) public service representatives,
one of whom is serving in a limited duration position through June 30, 2025, and the four and a half (4.5) license evaluators track
their cases closely and make every effort to respond quickly and with accurate information. In the same survey, sixty-nine percent
(69%) of licensees responded favorably when asked about the ability of licensing staff to provide services correctly the first
time.  Given the number of complex challenges related to licensing, this percentage is seen as positive progress for the agency.
Factors Affecting Results
The agency has reviewed reports on this metric from past years and discovered an inaccuracy in reported data since 2019. Since
2019, the agency has been unable to track this metric utilizing the system currently in place. As a result, the agency and
Commission must begin the process of identifying a replacement metric for customer service response.
The licensing department's high customer satisfaction rating of 76% is attributable to both the experience and dedication of the
Licensing Team. Under the supervision of a director who delves into the work alongside the team, they continue to see improved
responses from customers. The continued dissatisfaction, however, of just under 25% of those surveyed may largely be attributed
to the impact of the current eLicensing system. The staff have had to create workarounds for multiple repeated tasks due to the
system's shortcomings. Users also experience frustration in using the system which will not be replaced until November 2025 (the
agency is currently preparing to release an RFP for the system's replacement).

This metric is one that TSPC has been unable to report confidently and accurately in the past due to limitations of our current

measure would meet future reporting needs. The reporting of the aligned metric below is hoped to meet the need for reporting for

Beyond meeting the target for 2024, this is outstanding improvement for the agency and reflects the Licensing Team's continued
focus on well-serving customers in a timely manner.
Factors Affecting Results
These results are the outcome of additional limited duration staffing approved during the 2024 legislative session.  One (1)
additional limited duration public service representative began shortly after the session, which helped the agency improve over last
year. The agency struggled to meet the target of 35 days or fewer for processing applications yet was able to do so despite a
dysfunctional eLicensing system that is scheduled to be replaced in November 2025. The current system creates challenges which
lengthen time dramatically for both applicants and for TSPC Licensing staff. The successful besting of the target number of days is
directly attributable to the outstanding efforts of the Licensing public service representatives, evaluators, and leadership.
Oregon Teacher Standards and Practices is the regulatory agency for over 60,000 individuals holding over 70,000 licenses among
them. When we evaluate the customer service ratings, we cannot ignore the limitations created by having only three (3) licensing
public service representatives (one of which is a limited duration position which will end in June 2025) and four and a half (4.5)
license evaluators on staff to respond to questions and approve initial and renewal license applications for the State of Oregon. Put
simply, the agency ratios of 1:20,000 (public service representatives to licensed educators) and 1:13,333 (license evaluators to
licensed educators) are insufficient to meet the licensing needs of Oregon educators.  Looking more closely at the time period of
the customer service survey, we see that we had 1,624 license applications for every one (1) evaluator in the months from January
to April.  Expecting continued improvement of customer service targets may not be realistic, yet the team continues to dedicate
itself to doing so. As we seek to increase the number of licensed educators actively serving in Oregon, these ratios will need
consideration as well.

Factors Affecting Results
Although during the 2024 legislative session the agency received resources for limited duration (LD) employees to help with
investigations, the training of each LD is extensive. The impact of adding these positions will be better seen in next year's KPM
report.
The number of complaints received by Teacher Standards and Practices has increased this year, leading to over 400 cases
currently in the queue for investigation.  These complaints are categorized into the following areas:
• Abuse
• Assault
• Boundary Violations (Grooming of minor students)
• Classroom Management
• Conduct with Adult
• Drugs and Alcohol
• Failure to Report
• Fraud/Document Falsification
• Internet Pornography
• Licensure Violation
• Mental Fitness
• Sexual Conduct
• Other
The unpredictability of the sheer number of cases and the severity of cases is an important factor in this KPM. The time necessary
for each investigation varies, depending on cooperation of complainants, witnesses and the educators themselves. To investigate
these complaints and negotiate with educators and their legal counsel after Commission decisions, Teacher Standards and
Practices currently has three (3) legal liaisons and five (5) investigators. Of these eight (8) positions, only six (6) are permanent
employees. Without the limited duration employees who will complete their service term on July 31, 2025, and if we continue to
receive cases at the current rate, the investigator to case ratio will be 1:100+.  This ratio leads to extremely long wait periods for
less severe cases to be investigated while we prioritize sex abuse (SB 155) and other physical safety concerns for students. Each
of these prioritized cases take extensive investigation and contribute to the challenge of meeting the current KPM as written.
With a consistent history of greater than 50% of received complaints being dismissed by the agency, TSPC continues to evaluate
how to best communicate with the field regarding offenses that would lead to a sanction from the agency. In addition, TSPC is
evaluating how to best ensure that complaints submitted are tied to an actionable offense by statute and rule. By ensuring that
cases reported to TSPC tie to statute and rule violations under the agency's authority and reducing the number of reported cases
which do not, staff will have more time available to investigate relevant misconduct reports.

How Are We Doing
For three years in a row, the agency has processed for approval within 30 days 100% of our new program and major modification
proposals. The Educator Preparation & Pathways Unit is responsible to process EPP proposals for educator licensure of all Oregon
educator licensure including teacher, principal, professional administrator, school counselor, school psychologist, and school social
worker. Any of these programs in private or public state-approved institutions that lead to Oregon licensure must be approved by
the Teacher Standards and Practices Commission prior to completion of candidate programs. Further, the timelines for approval
are quite restrictive. Proposals must be reviewed and recommended for approval to the Commission according to the following
timeline:
• Submission 30 days before Commission Meeting
• Recommendation for approval at Program Approval Meeting of Commission 14 days prior to Commission Meeting
• Consideration for approval at Commission Meeting
Factors Affecting Results
Though the agency has not considered an alternative KPM as anticipated, the changes required to meet the new early literacy
education preparation standards will have an impact across all Oregon Educator Preparation Programs. Staff anticipate an increase
in the number of new program and major modification proposals and, thus, recommend this measure stay in place for the next
biennium.

How Are We Doing
The TSPC Customer Service Survey was sent to the 7,308 individuals who applied for a license between the dates of 01/01/2024
and 04/30/2024.  In addition, it was sent to all education service and school district superintendents, the TSPC human resources
list-serv, and all of our Oregon college and university educator preparation programs.  The survey focused on agency services from
January 1, 2024, through June 30, 2024.  Of the initials 331 respondents, 231 had worked with TSPC during the focused time
period.  This time period was selected due to leadership changes in the agency and the desire to measure services under current
leadership.
Outcomes indicate a continued dramatic increase in customer satisfaction falling just shy of our 80% target satisfaction rate.  When
we disaggregate by unit, we see differentiation in customer satisfaction.  One hundred percent (100%) of education preparation
providers rated the agency as either “good” (3) or “outstanding” (4) in overall service, whereas 68% of licensed respondents
responded similarly. Given that in 2023 seventy-five percent (75%) of licensed respondents rated the agency as either “below
average” or “poor”, we see dramatic improvement in this year's data. Though improvement continues to need to be made within this
area, the agency has made an important shift within customer service overall and specifically within our work with licensed
educators.
Factors Affecting Results
Oregon Teacher Standards and Practices is the regulatory agency for approximately 60,000 individuals holding approximately
70,000 licenses among them. When we evaluate the customer service ratings, we cannot ignore the limitations created by having
only three (3) licensing public service representatives and four and a half (4.5) license evaluators on staff to respond to questions
and approve initial and renewal license applications for the State of Oregon. Put simply, the agency ratios of 1:20,000 (public
service representatives to licensed educators) and 1:13,333 (license evaluators to licensed educators) are insufficient to meet the
licensing needs of Oregon educators.  Looking more closely at the time period of the customer service survey, we see that we had
1,624 license applications for every one (1) evaluator in the months from January to April.
TSPC has seen improvement over the last biennium, even with fewer staff to address customer needs. This may be attributable to
a team with deep experience who continues to dedicate itself to customer service despite workload challenges.  The sustainability
of improvement is a question, as the agency will continue to operate with the current licensing data system until at least November
2025; the system requires workarounds that add steps to the evaluator process for each considered application. As we seek to
increase the number of licensed educators actively serving in Oregon, the ratios within the Licensing Team will need consideration
as well.
