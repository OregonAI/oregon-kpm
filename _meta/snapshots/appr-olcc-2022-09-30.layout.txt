How Are We Doing
The commission conducted 10 minor decoy operations in May 2022.  7 locations passed, 3 sold.  The commission hadn't run an alcohol minor decoy operation in the prior 23 months because of the

Factors Affecting Results
One of the majpor deterrence for alcohol licenses is the liklihood of detection.  A consistent operational tempo has been missing the past two years as commission focus has been supporting the
public health objectives of the state government.  The resumption of operations will serve as a reminder for licensees to ensure that their employees are confirming that customers are eligible to make
alcohol purchases.

The commission has implemented a new business process for tracking the issuance and resolution of violations.  The rate of premises experiencing a second violation within two years of a first is 4%.
The KPM result indicates that 4% of alcohol premises which had a violation referral recieved by the administrative hearings unit received a second violation referral (that was resolved) from a seperate

licensees.  The likely result of a higher rate of processed cases, is a higher rate of second violations to be anticipated over the next several years before there is stabilization in the flow of referred and
resolved cases.

How Are We Doing
The FY 2022 average licensing time statewide was 98 days. This is above the legislatively set target of 75, and below last year’s licensing time of 109 days. The Commission continues to adapt to
changes caused by the COVID-19 pandemic and the changes in licensee privileges extended by the legislature in the 2021 session.

The commission is focused on restoring business processes and modernizing the license application process in order provide prompt service to alcohol licensees.

Factors Affecting Results
There are many factors affecting the number of days it takes to issue a liquor license; some internal and some external.

Internal factors continue to be identified and streamlined through process improvements and technological solutions. The COVID-19 emergency caused the OLCC to pivot licensing rules and allow for
accelerated applications for off-premises licenses for bars and restaurants, license investigators were tasked with helping licensees adapt to new rules and public health guidelines.  The legislature
changed licensee privileges during the 2021 session, changing which license types that applicants may select, and making hundreds of licensees eligible for refunds.  Additionally, several alcohol
license investigators were re-assigned to help with processing the backlog of marijuana license renewal investigations.  Monthly break-downs of days to license show substantial slowing in process
times beginning in March.

External factors are difficult to control. A primary external factor affecting how quickly a liquor license can be issued is the license application review by the local governing body (city or county). Statute
gives local governments up to 90 days (45 days plus an additional 45 day extension – if requested) to review a license application within their jurisdiction and provide a recommendation (positive,
negative, or neutral). The OLCC cannot complete the processing of an application until the local government review is completed. Lengthy application review by local governments usually occurs in
the larger metropolitan areas, such as Portland. These areas also have higher numbers of license applications, in absolute terms, which influence the overall statewide average licensing times.
Additionally, the timeliness of the applicant in providing materials necessary to the application investigation can impact overall processing time. Applicants not prepared for or committed to the process
may have longer processing times.  A 2011 analysis found that staff processing time totaled to an average of only 32.9 days per license; only 36% of the total time to issue a license. The remaining
64% of the time was driven be external factors described above and are outside the control of the agency.

How Are We Doing
The overall agency rating was 71 percent (rated as good or excellent) compared to 65 percent in 2021. The OLCC missed the 85 percent target in all five areas when averages were taken from five
stakeholder groups.

The OLCC met or exceeded the 85 percent average target for surveys from liquor agents and alcohol licensees for helpfulness and knowledge of staff.  Marijuana licensees provided an evaluation of
59 percent excellent or very good service- driven by complaints of poor timeliness and poor evaluation of information about changing rules and laws.

The lower average scores were driven by the much less favorable responses from the general public compared to prior years. Respondents who self-identified as interacting with OLCC related to
distilled spirits an average satisfaction score of 40 percent and those interacting around alcohol licensees 33%.

The random selection of staff for responses provided feedback that evaluated agency timeliness of services and information availability as the lowest areas of customer service...consistent with the
reports from other stakeholder groups.

Factors Affecting Results
Surveys were conducted on 6 stakeholder groups: the public, marijuana licensees, alcohol licensees, liquor agents, staff and worker and service permit holders.

Respondants were selected by stratified random samples for the licensee groups, simple random samples for the staff and permit holder groups, canvenience sample for public stakeholders
(interacting with the commission's website or spocial media accounts), or a census of liquor agents.

There were a total of 364 respondents from the five survey stakeholder groups. There was a significant difference in responses between the general public and marijuana licencees versus alcohol
licensees, worker permitees and agents.  Marijuana licensees continue to be frustrated at perceived slow license processing times.

Respondants from the general public varied significantly based on the area of interaction with the commission.  Those seeking information about the bottle bill expressed the highest satisfaction with
service, while those connect about alcohol licensees reported the lowest.

Survey respondant rates for sample were low, with less than half of sample members responding for the alcohol and marijuana licensee groups, and fewer than 20 percent response for the
permitees.  The low response rate may have sorted out less motivated respondents resulting in an unkown bias on the results.

How Are We Doing
The OLCC rate of return in FY 2022 was $2.30 for each dollar spent.  This is substantially lower than the 2.65 rate of return last fiscal year.  The principal difference year-over-year has been the 20
percent increase in payments to liquor agents.  Without the legislatively required increase in rate for agents, the OLCC would have a 2.61 rate of return for FY 2022.

Factors Affecting Results
Because the commission’s largest cost driver, agent compensation, is part of a rate-based formula pegged to liquor sales, the commission will need to dramatically increase revenue rates above
increases in regulatory and administrative expenditures to attain the target rate of return of 2.5.

The commission will also begin to service bond obligations for the new warehouse project starting the 2022 fiscal year, placing the achievement of the target rate even further out of reach.

How Are We Doing
The FY 2022 mean marijuana licensing time statewide was 83 days for the 469 cases for which a new recreational marijuana license was issued during fiscal year 2022. The median time to license
was 73 days indicating that there were a relatively small number of cases that disproportionately raised the average days to issuance. The commission experienced a short period of slowed license
issuance as a high volume of applications were submitted between the end of the commission’s ‘pause’ on new applications and the effective date of HB 4016, which froze all new marijuana
applications.

Laboratory licenses took the shortest period to process, 63 days on average, while processors were issued in an average of 90 days.

Factors Affecting Results
There are many factors affecting the number of days it takes to issue a recreational marijuana license; some internal and some external. Internal factors continue to be identified as business
processes are developed to implement newer marijuana law and rules.

External factors are difficult to control. A primary external factor affecting how quickly a recreational marijuana license can be issued is the license application review by the local governing body (city
or county). The issuance of Land Use Compatibility Statement by the local governing unit is the triggering event that allows the Oregon Liquor Control Commission to assign an investigator to begin
actively processing a marijuana application.

Applicants have also struggled to complete applications correctly and in a timely manner. The commission adopted a policy of side-tracking applications from non-responsive or unprepared applicants,
in order to expedite processing engaged and prepared applicants.
