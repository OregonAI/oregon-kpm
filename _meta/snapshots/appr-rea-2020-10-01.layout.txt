How Are We Doing
For the 2020 reporting period 100% of property managers and principal brokers who completed the compliance review process came into compliance within 45 days of the review completion.

Factors Affecting Results
This key performance measure was introduced in reporting period 2014 and has performed well historically by consistently exceeding the target over the last six years. However, since implementation
the compliance review has been optional. This results in extremely minimal engagement - despite incentivizing the effort with continuing education credit and marketing the program in the Oregon
Real Estate News Journal. Due to low participation this program fails to reach the licensees that are most likely to benefit, and even though has a successful KPM associated, does not provide the
Agency with meaningful data to drive educational efforts.

This KPM has the potential to be more volatile than others in that if any number of licensees fail to comply, the overall compliance rate will suffer dramatically. The Agency often has two or fewer
participants in a month. If just a few in a period do not, or cannot, reach compliance in 45 days then compliance issues may appear outsized.

Secondly, again due to having few participants, the existing data is not likely to be reflective of the entire pool of licensees. As participation is self-selected those that volunteer appear to be more likely
to be in compliance and unconcerned about having files examined. This is demonstrated when looking at mandatory clients’ trust account reconciliation reviews as a comparator. In the reconciliation
review program where review volume is substantially higher, the noncompliance rates are considerably higher and outcomes have clearly established patterns. This allows the Agency to observe
where failures are recurring, execute broader outreach and monitor performance through subsequent reviews and case related data.
In response to the missed opportunities inherent in the self-administered compliance review the Agency intends to modify this program in the next biennium. The Agency will introduce a mandatory
compliance review for principal brokers and property managers. With a new automated process the Agency will be able to collect valuable information related to noncompliance at a much higher
volume without requiring an increase to staffing resources and expanding the reach of this program.

How Are We Doing
For the 2020 reporting period 74% of cases were completed within 150 days of receipt of the complaint, falling short of the 80% benchmark. The Agency has exceeded the 80% standard in this
category since 2017, so reliably that it was raised from 60% in the 2019 Legislative Session. While the rate falls below the goal and marks a downturn in historical data, the Agency has undergone
some significant yet necessary changes under extraordinary circumstances, and this is a result to be proud of especially given that this represents an increase in case closure volume of 36% over the
prior year. The Agency has implemented meaningful change in the Regulation program igniting the current rebuilding period in this Division. Reorganization efforts are producing good results and sub-
data indicates a return to above 80% case completion rate within 150 days again in the next biennium.

Factors Affecting Results
This most recent reporting period has presented the Agency with a significant number of challenges. The Division has had the vacancy of a long term manager and the position has been held open for
an extended period. Also, COVID-19 forced staff offsite to telework fulltime and required rapid change in business practices, including converting paper files to an electronic format to maintain
effectiveness and security in a distanced environment. Investigators have also had to adjust to new and less familiar tools for interviews and file inspection, like Zoom and Teams rather than in person
field investigations at the site of business. However, the most significant contributor to the change in this KPM is the discovery and resolution of a considerable amount of old outstanding open cases.
This situation has led to substantial changes to roles and responsibilities in this Division as well as an appreciable but temporary increase to the overall case load handled by regulatory staff.

Early in his tenure, Commissioner Strode ordered an internal review of the business operations of the Regulation Division. At a high level, the purpose of this audit was to identify the depth and cause
of informally observed case delays. Though more specifically, Executive Management detected a distinct need to optimize business processes, coupled with more rigorous and transparent reporting
and accountability. The findings of this review, identified that case related workflow effectively channeled all work-product through the former Division Manager exclusively, often at repeated stages in
the case lifecycle. Not only did this create redundancy and substantial workflow inefficiencies but also established authority and knowledge isolation -thus shrouding production administration and the
true backlog.

The findings revealed that starting in 2013, cases requiring final review by the Division Manager began accumulating - and by the Fall of 2019 had reached 100 cases with two or more years of
inactivity. Former leadership failed to distinguish this problem as performance data for closed cases remained at satisfactory volume and closure rates. These cases were not distinctly identified in
other internal metrics at the time. Upon discovery of these issues reorganization efforts commenced immediately.

The Commissioner approached this issue with the intent to clear the backlog, while simultaneously implementing process improvements for ongoing case management. In that effort, administrative
and compliance review work supporting the intake of a complaint, formerly handled by a single support staff in Regulations, was shifted to the Compliance team in the Education Division. This work is
now shared amongst four staff and is less vulnerable to delays in periods of high volume, employee time off and position vacancy. Pending queues for investigations were eliminated and investigators
are now given cases upon their opening rather than having a manager hold a case until it is ready to be worked. This allows investigators to understand and continually evaluate their entire case load,
prioritizing and infilling as needed. The Agency also already employed two Lead Workers in the Regulation Division whose role was intended to guide the administrative review and settlement
process. They are now being utilized as their positions were created, have been empowered to close non-disciplinary cases independently, and obtain direct approval from Commissioner on all
disciplinary actions.

Since implementing changes in the Division, days in administrative review have reduced by 82% and days in complaint review have decreased by 22%. These early results provide very strong
indicators to future success in this measure, continued increases in productivity, and that the process redesign has been effective.

The Agency chose to undertake the large case backlog in a large single grouping, knowing that its performance rating in this measure would be temporarily diminished However, resolving this issue
and preventing it from reoccurring in the future is a great accomplishment and one that the new leadership is eager to share. The Agency has been fully transparent with its industry stakeholders -
from reporting the findings in the OREN-J, and providing ongoing updates to the Real Estate Board. Overall, Executive Leadership is heavily engaged in performance and establishing a culture of
empowerment and shared accountability while setting clear expectations for management and staff.

How Are We Doing
Now arriving at 96%, FY 20 is the first time that the Agency has met or exceeded KPM #3 since 2014.

Factors Affecting Results
The Agency has taken a new approach in the settlement process. Historically the Agency has taken the position of withholding the specifics of the settlement offer and using the meeting as an
opportunity to present and further negotiate details. However new leadership has consciously modified this approach due to concerns over unnecessarily delays and opaque communication. The
Agency now presents a final written settlement offer as the first settlement offer. The settlement process continues to allow for respondents to produce new information to allow for a changes in the
settlement when appropriate. Respondents now have a clearer understanding of the Agency’s intention and the process is less subject to miscommunication. However, a respondent may always
decline a settlement agreement and prefer to take the case to hearing.

How Are We Doing
The current KPM falls short of the target by 1%, coming in at 74%.

Factors Affecting Results
In the 2017 legislative session the Agency requested the approval of the legislature to change the engagement point with which this KPM was evaluated by the new licensee. Prior to the approved
change, the Agency would contact the licensee six months following licensure. However the comments related to the KPM were heavily focused on the failure of the examination to prepare a new
licensee for success in real estate sales. By inquiring with the new licensee one month following licensure comments have become more relevant to pre-license education and the effectiveness of the
examination.

The Agency observes that many of the comments associated with lower ratings suggest confusion with the question itself. In real estate, most licensees view competency to follow some sort of formal
training program at their brokerage. Pre-licensing exams are not intended to teach sales and marketing. Moreover, “ethics” is often associated with “Code of Ethics” of the Realtor Association,
whereas Oregon statute only refers to “duties and responsibilities” of a licensee.

Within this reporting period, the Agency hosted two work groups comprised of staff and industry leaders from the licensee community, to update the examination content. The updated examinations
will be put into place in October 2020 and is expected to improve this KPM further.

Also beginning in October, the Agency's exam vendor PSI will begin conducting online proctoring for real estate pre-license examinations as a measure to increase safety and reduce spread of
COVID-19 as well as provide greater equity for applicants that have lesser availability to travel to an examination site.

The Agency will continue to closely monitor the comments associated with this KPM following these changes and will be offering clarifying language to the question so that respondents understand the
role of occupational licensure exams as a measure of competent knowledge of statute and rules.

How Are We Doing
The Agency continues to exceed targets in all areas of customer service.

Factors Affecting Results
The Agency strives to provide outstanding customer service to licensees and the public. Agency staff are available by telephone, email and appointment at the office location in downtown Salem,
Monday through Friday 8:00am to 5:00pm. All applications are available through the Agency's eLicense system and conducted online. License applications are processed through automated
workflows, based on business rules, in real time as requirements are met and do not require staff intervention outside of the background review. Business applications are completed within three
business days.

The Agency handles approximately 2,000 phone calls per month with an average hold time of 24.5 seconds. Agency email is responded to within the same day. Comments from the customer service
survey are monitored on a monthly basis and are overwhelmingly positive, heaping praise on the well-deserving front line customer service staff. A very small snapshot representative of typical
comments on customer service include,
       Elizabeth is a keeper. She cared about my problem.
       Everyone I have ever spoken to offers FANTASTIC Service.
       The agency employee who helped me was excellent.
       Great job on Jennifer on doing a walk thru with me.
       Roger is amazing and very helpful.
       Generally very timeline responses.
       Tami was very helpful & kind.

The Agency’s eLicense system is considered a foundational element of the Agency’s overall customer service. This system is beginning to age beyond user expectations in functionality and the
Agency has observed an increase in negative comments regarding its user-interface and features. The Agency intends to request funding for replacement in the 23-25 biennium as well as conduct
updates to improve the user experience in the 21-23 biennium.
