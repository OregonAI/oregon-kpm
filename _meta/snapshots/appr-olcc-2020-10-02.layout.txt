How Are We Doing
The FY 2020 results reveal an 84 percent compliance rate of refusing sales to minors which is 6 percent less than the legislative target of 90 percent compliance.  The compliance rate was the same
as FY 2019.

The Portland and Salem metropolitan regions experienced 85 and 82 percent compliance rates respectively, while the Eugene compliance rate was 73 percent and Bend licensees passed 83 percent
of the time. Medford region conducted fewer operations but still logged an 80% compliance rate for visited locations.  Metro Portland and Salem provided 60 percent of the cases.

The Oregon Liquor Control Commission conducted 554 alcohol minor decoy operations in FY 2020, down from 741 in FY 2019.  Due to the COVID-19 emergency, no minor decoy operations were
carried out from March 16 to June 30.

Factors Affecting Results
The Oregon Liquor Control Commission was on pace to conduct approximately 750 minor decoy operations.  The COVID-19 emergency required compliance staff to pivot toward supporting the
adaptation of the hospitality industry to Oregon’s on-going health emergency.  Minor decoy operations halted and compliance staff were directed to assist in ensuring that licensees fulfilled their public
health obligations as re-opening progressed.

A related factor affecting results is the random sampling of minor decoy operations.   With the exception of some targeted premises that have committed a prior offense, the majority of operations are
performed on a different group of licensee each year. This can result in some variation from past years because now fewer establishments with a recent citation are revisited within a short time period.

Fiscal year 2020 was a period of re-establishing regular operations in alcohol enforcement for the Oregon Liquor Control Commission.  This is reflected in the decline in a rebound minor decoy
operations observed over prior years.

Recruiting minor decoys is always a challenge and inspectors spend a considerable amount of time recruiting qualified volunteer decoys.   Decoys are instructed not to deceive the licensee during a
decoy operation. Decoys are expected to “appear” younger than 21 and use their actual IDs when asked.  IDs were checked in over half of the instances where the decoys were sold alcohol indicating
that servers and store clerks are not properly noting the age on the ID. This may be a training issue for licensees.

The OLCC has leveraged its experience in conducting minor decoy operations by consulting with and training local law enforcement agencies to effectively conduct their own operations. The creation
of these synergistic partnerships bring together the OLCC's knowledge base with the personnel resources of other law enforcement agencies so more operations can be conducted around the state.  It
should also be noted that an “inspection gap" continues to form as the number of licensed businesses is growing with respect to the number of OLCC inspection/public safety personnel.   This gap
results in a general decrease in the number of minor decoy operations conducted only by the OLCC and the need for local law enforcement partnerships.

The Commission is actively engaged in a process to increase the frequency and media prominence of minor decoy operations; a way to keep the problem of youth alcohol access foremost in the
minds of Oregon alcohol licensees.

How Are We Doing

The FY 2020 second violation rate is 4.4 percent, slightly less than last year’s second violation rate of 9.2 percent.  The second violation rate for the last five fiscal years has averaged 8.1 percent.

During FY 2020, there were 159 alcohol premises with at least one serious violation.  This represents less than 2 percent of total licensed premises for the year

Factors Affecting Results

The rate of repeat violations declined again joining observed decreases for the three of the prior four years.  FY 2020 saw an increase in overall compliance actions, but a decrease in alcohol
compliance charges processed by our administrative processes unit. Fewer compliance actions reduces the likelihood of any licensee receiving multiple violations during the period. OLCC
administrative hearings unit has focused on the massive influx of marijuana violations received in FY 2020, it has reduced the number of charge letters issued to alcohol licensees.

During FY 2020, the Oregon Liquor Control Commission has requested budget limitiation for more case presenters to try to catch up to the case load that has more than doubled.The Commission has
filled empty Liquor Regulatory Staff positions left vacant by Inspectors who had switched to recreational or medical marijuana inspector positions. However, the commission has not expanded the
number of case presenters available to issue and prosecute charges against violating premises.

Compliance staff have also continued implementing a strategy of using resources to engage with businesses proactively and reserve compliance actions for the more serious violations such as sales
to minors.

How Are We Doing
The FY 2020 average licensing time statewide was 85 days. This is above the legislatively set target of 75. The average time to license for FY 2020 is 6 days higher than the FY 2019 alcohol days to
license of 79 days. The Commission continues to adapt in order provide prompt service to alcohol licensees.

Factors Affecting Results
There are many factors affecting the number of days it takes to issue a liquor license; some internal and some external.

Internal factors continue to be identified and streamlined through process improvements and technological solutions. The COVID-19 emergency caused the OLCC to pivot licensing rules and allow for
accelerated applications for off-premises licenses for bars and restaurants, license investigators were tasked with helping licensees adapt to new rules and public health guidelines.  Additionally,
several alcohol license investigators were re-assigned to help with processing the backlog of marijuana license renewal investigations.  Monthly break-downs of days to license show substantial
slowing in process times beginning in March.

External factors are difficult to control. A primary external factor affecting how quickly a liquor license can be issued is the license application review by the local governing body (city or county). Statute
gives local governments up to 90 days (45 days plus an additional 45 day extension – if requested) to review a license application within their jurisdiction and provide a recommendation (positive,
negative, or neutral). The OLCC cannot complete the processing of an application until the local government review is completed. Lengthy application review by local governments usually occurs in
the larger metropolitan areas, such as Portland. These areas also have higher numbers of license applications, in absolute terms, which influence the overall statewide average licensing times.
Additionally, the timeliness of the applicant in providing materials necessary to the application investigation can impact overall processing time. Applicants not prepared for or committed to the process
may have longer processing times.  A 2011 analysis found that staff processing time totaled to an average of only 32.9 days per license; only 36% of the total time to issue a license. The remaining
64% of the time was driven be external factors described above and are outside the control of the agency.

The 2020 results of this measure indicate that the OLCC is above the current target set by the legislature and is continuing to implement staffing and business process strategies for resuming
improvement.  The OLCC is pursuing other solutions to its business needs that include regulatory innovations, the implementation of streamlining measures, and the development of an enterprise-
level licensing system that will unite many disconnected processes and increase the agency’s online service capacity

The license investigation process is largely manual, involving many phone calls between investigators and applicants in order to guide them to their license.  In addition, financial investigations of
liquor licenses can also become labor intensive for complex business structures. The agency has budgeted more alcohol license investigator positions to increase staff dedicated to liquor license
investigations and providing more timely service to our industry partners.

How Are We Doing
The overall agency rating was 74 percent (rated as good or excellent) compared to 72 percent in 2019. The OLCC missed the 85 percent target in all five areas when averages were taken from five
stakeholder groups. However, the OLCC met or exceeded the 85 percent average target for surveys from liquor agents and alcohol licensees with 85 percent excellent or good service.  Marijuana
licensees provided an evaluation of 65 percent excellent or very good service- driven by complaints of poor timeliness and changing rules.

The lower average scores were driven by the much less favorable responses from the general public compared to prior years, and increased frustration expressed by marijuana licensees about
timeliness of service.

Public respondents who self-identified as interacting with OLCC related to recreational marijuana provided an average satisfaction score of 80 percent

The agency continues to make efforts to increase information availability through Gov Alerts and posting updates on the agency website. The Commission is endeavoring to improve licensing times for
marijuana licensees by hiring more staff.

Factors Affecting Results
There were a total of 351 respondents from the five survey stakeholder groups. There was a significant difference in responses between the general public, alcohol and marijuana licensees or agents.
Those with business before commission generally provided a much higher evaluation of the Commission than did the public survey.

Marijuana licensee stakeholders have grown substantially less satisfied with the timeliness of service and the availability of information provided.  The commission has been working to streamline our
license process and continues to hire licensing staff as allowed by the commission budget.  We expect that information availability will improve next year because of two importnat changes.  1) there
has been less major legislative changes affecting the marijuana industry, 2) the commission has updated its website, and several licensees mentioned that the new website is an improvement over the
older one, especially the ease of searching for information.

The increase of general public attention to the Oregon Liquor Control Commission and the widespread confusion that the Commission is responsible for legislation and rules implementing all aspects
of marijuana (medical and recreational) as well as cigarettes may have contributed to the low evaluations from the public stakeholder group.

How Are We Doing

The OLCC rate of return in FY 2020 was $2.61 for every dollar spent. The rate of return is below the target of $2.70 per dollar spent that was missed in FY 2016 and exceeded the past three years.
During FY 2020 the surcharge generated $ 18.9 million in additional revenue. Without the surcharge the ratio would have fallen from $2.61 to $2.42, indicating that the surcharge continues to be
instrumental for generating targeted revenue.

Factors Affecting Results
There are many factors that affect the agency's rate of return. Gross revenue from liquor sales increased 8 percent during FY 2020 over FY 2019.

The increase in liquor sales can be attributed increased economic growth in Oregon for most of the period, as well as an substantial increase in sales during the COVID-19 emergency.

The largest expenditure, after the landed cost of product, is agent compensation. Agent Compensation in FY 2020 rose 8 percent because of increased sales and legislative changes.  Additionally, the
shift in sales from sales to bars and restaurants to consumer sales during the emergency triggered a large increase in credit card fees because licensees purchased using cash or check while liquor
agents required customers to use credit cards for public health reasons during the COVID crisis.

How Are We Doing
The FY 2020 results reveal a 90 percent compliance rate of refusing sales to minors which almost matches the updated legislative target of 90 percent compliance.  The compliance rate was the same
as alcohol compliance. Despite stopping minor decoy operations during the Covid-19 emergency, Regulatory Specialists conducted 247 marijuana minor decoy operations in fiscal year 2020, 112
more than in FY 2019

The Salem region experienced a 92 percent compliance rate, while the Medford’s compliance rate was 94 percent.  Bend regions compliance rate was 87 percent.  Eugene region passed 77 percent
of locations visited locations.

Fiscal Year 2020 was the third year that the Recreational Marijuana Program conducted minor decoy operations.

Factors Affecting Results
A key factor driving compliance results is the likelihood of detection. The "perception of detection" is a significant motivator to comply with marijuana laws for licensees and their staff.  When the
number of operations decreases, a licensee may not perceive the risk of detection as likely and choose to make decisions that do not comply with the public safety laws.

The first minor decoy operations conducted in the Portland metro region, during December of 2017, resulted in a 70% fail rate.  Medical marijuana dispensaries had never experienced minor decoy
operations before, and these were the first such operations conducted on recreational retailers. Widespread publicity of results, and the Commission’s decision to increase penalties associated with
sales to minors corresponded with a substantial decline in subsequent violations.
Marijuana compliance efforts pivoted toward more minor decoy operations in FY 2020. Attention was still paid to compliance during harvests, as with “Operation Good Harvest” in 2019, but licensees
have acclimated to maintaining a compliant posture.  As a consequence operational activity for minor decoys increased with an additional 112 operations conducted despite the COVID-19
emergency.  With the onset of the emergency, compliance resources flexed to help implement changes to manage safe commercial activities in the sector

How Are We Doing
The FY 2020 mean marijuana licensing time statewide was 195 days for the 237 cases for which a new recreational marijuana license was issued during fiscal year 2020. The median time to license
is 147 days indicating that there are a relatively small number of cases that disproportionately raise the average. However, even the median time of 147 days is well above the target of 70 days to
license. The Commission continues to try to increase staff and to adapt in order to implement the recreational marijuana program and provide prompt service to applicants.

Laboratory licenses took the shortest period to process, on 157 days on average, while processors were processed in an average of 226 days.

Factors Affecting Results
There are many factors affecting the number of days it takes to issue a recreational marijuana license; some internal and some external. Internal factors continue to be identified as business
processes are developed to implement newer marijuana law and rules.

During the second half of FY 2020 the Commission redeployed some alcohol license investigators to help focus on marijuana licensing activities, as the marijuana program has experienced a large
number of complicated license sales in addition to working through the backlog of applications that had been submitted prior to June 15, 2018.

 The Commission continues to hire licensing staff, implement process improvements and technological solutions (automation). The recreational marijuana program continued to fill a number of
vacancies in license investigator positions during fiscal year 2020.  The renewal process also saw a large proportion of applicants with complicated renewals containing site and/or ownership
changes.  Renewal applications, location changes or ownership changes of less than 50% are not included in this metric, but do represent a licensing burden.
External factors are difficult to control. A primary external factor affecting how quickly a recreational marijuana license can be issued is the license application review by the local governing body (city
or county). The issuance of Land Use Compatibility Statement by the local governing unit is the triggering event that allows the Oregon Liquor Control Commission to assign an investigator to begin
actively processing a marijuana application.

Applicants have also struggled to complete applications correctly and in a timely manner. The newness of the program and changes in law and rules guiding the application process have posed a
challenge for applicants who are navigating the process.  In order to help applicants, the Oregon Liquor Control Commission has produced a business guide for applicants and licensees, and
continues to provide education and information through the website, emails, and frequent communication with license investigators.

An analysis of some of the longer duration applications show applicants that fail to respond for 365 days or more, before resuming the application process.  The Commission has initiated a process of
side-tracking applications for applicants who are not actively engaged in the licensing process.
