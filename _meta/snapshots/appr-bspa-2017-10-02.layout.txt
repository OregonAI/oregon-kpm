How Are We Doing
The annual supervision audit continues to show that there are issues with speech-language pathology assistant supervision in Oregon school districts. While some errors are related to the failure to
notify the Board of supervisory relationships, there are several instances of people receiving no supervision in a given month in direct violation of Board rules. There is a persistent confusion in
some districts on the roles and responsibilities of speech-language pathology assistants and their requirement to work under the supervision of a licensed speech-language pathologist for all
students on that SLP's caseload.
Factors Affecting Results
This year's audit was twice as large as previous audits due to the persistent issues stated above.

Contracted telepractice supervision from remote locations is an emerging issue with SLPA supervision. The Board has concerns that the lack of guidance in this newly emerging modality has blurred
the roles and responsibilities of SLPs and SLPAs.

Some school districts facing budgetary choices have tried to move towards a heavier reliance on SLPAs to provide clinical services, sometimes without proper safeguards to ensure they are in
compliance with Board's rules.

How Are We Doing
The Board met on August 11, 2017 and discussed review of Board best practices and strategic planning. Their goal is to finalize their review in November of 2017.  The Board has had some long
time Board members turn over for newer members which has necessitated training and orientation to the Board's ongoing issues. Several large and complicated investigations have caused
considerable workloads for the director. Without a staff investigator the executive director is left to balance the investigations and case management with all other duties and expectations. The
agency continues to find administrative efficiencies by working with other health-related licensing boards that share the office space. A shared in-house accountant has given the agency greater
responsiveness and accountability.

Factors Affecting Results
All new Board members now take the State's orientation course.
