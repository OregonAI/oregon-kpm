How Are We Doing
Alcohol licensees recorded a 74.4% pass rate on 1,004 alcohol minor decoy operations.  This is below the target of a 90% pass rate.

The Oregon Liquar and Cannabis Commission resumed full-scale minor decoy operations in FY 2023 after a two and a half year hiatus for the COVID-19 emergency.

Licensee pass-rates for the first three months of FY 2023 were 66%, with pass rates climbing to 77% for the final six months of the fiscal year.

Factors Affecting Results
The Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission resumed minor decoy operations in FY 2023.  Licensees and thair employees seem to have developed a low-perception of detection as initial
operations resulted in very low licensee pass rates.

Commission missions were constrained by the limited number of minor decoys that the agency has been able to hire and retain.  Regions have had to share decoys, and the limited availability of the
decoys has prevented more operations from being conducted.

Licensees participating the Responsible Vendor Program have consistently outperformed non-participant licensees, with a 12% improvement in pass-rates for RVP members.

The commission is continuing to hire minor decoys in order to increase the supply of decoys in each of the regions to increase availabity and improve operational output.

Rate of Second Violation
Actual                                                                            7.20%                              4.40%                                                                    4%                                  7%
Target                                                                             12%                                12%                                 12%                                12%                                 12%

How Are We Doing
During FY 2021 there were 89 premises with serious violations received by the hearings unit, that persisted to final dispostion.  Of those, 6 premises received a second serious violation within the
following two years; a 7% rate of second violation.  This is below the pre-measure 91 rate of about 13%.

Only 89 premises that were charged in 2021 made it to final disposition by the end of FY 2023.  This is lower than the historical rate of final dispositions from before the COVID-19 emergency, and the
pre-measure 91 rate of charges from the commission's Liquor Regulatory Specialists.

Factors Affecting Results
The rate of second violation is dependent on the number of charges forwarded to the administrative hearings division by Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission compliance specialists during a
particular year.  The rate is also dependent on the ability of the Administrative Hearings unit to process violations and bring charges to status of final disposition.

Since the end of the COVID-19 emergency, the commission has been increasing capacity to process violations in a timely manner, and compliance has resumed full-scale minor decoy operations,
and other alcohol compliance missions.

There were 159 serious violations charged in 2023 that were brought to final disposition the same year.  This is compared to 89 charges in 2021.  The commission anticipates an increased rate of
repeat violations as the fraction of premises charged with an initial violation will increase dramatically over the current rate of about 1.5%.

How Are We Doing
The FY 2023 average licensing time statewide was 137 days. This is far above the legislatively set target of 75, and above last year’s licensing time of 98 days. The Commission continues to adapt to
changes caused by the COVID-19 pandemic and the changes in licensee privileges extended by the legislature in the 2021 session.

The commission is focused on restoring business processes and modernizing the license application process in order provide prompt service to alcohol licensees. The commission will be
implementing a new online licensing system over the course of the next biennium.  It is anticipated that the new system will help applicants file correct applications, and also streamline commission
management of licensing workflows.

Factors Affecting Results
There are many factors affecting the number of days it takes to issue a liquor license; both internal and some external.

Internal factors continue to be identified and streamlined through process improvements and technological solutions.  The legislature changed licensee privileges during the 2021 session.
Additionally, several alcohol license investigators were re-assigned to help with processing the backlog of marijuana license renewal investigations.  Monthly break-downs of days to license show
substantial slowing in process times during the first half of the fiscal year, with processing times reverting back toward the 90 day mark for the earlier.

External factors affecting processing times are applicant responsiveness to the process and the license application review by the local governing body (city or county). Statute gives local governments
up to 90 days (45 days plus an additional 45 day extension – if requested) to review a license application within their jurisdiction and provide a recommendation (positive, negative, or neutral). The
OLCC cannot complete the processing of an application until the local government review is completed

How Are We Doing
The overall agency rating was 69 percent (rated as good or excellent) compared to 71 percent in 2022. The OLCC missed the 85 percent target in all five areas when averages were taken from six
stakeholder groups.

The OLCC met or exceeded the 85 percent average target for surveys from  alcohol licensees for helpfulness, knowledge and overall service of staff.

Marijuana licensees provided an evaluation of 73 percent excellent or very good service- a 14 point improvement over 2022, but still below target and driven by complaints of poor timeliness and poor
evaluation of information about changing rules and laws.

The lower average scores were driven by the much less favorable responses from the general public compared to prior years. Respondents who self-identified as interacting with OLCC related to
distilled spirits an average satisfaction score of 36 percent versus 47% for public respondants overall.  These findings are certainly related to the revelation of unethical behavior by OLCC executives.

The random selection of staff for responses provided feedback that evaluated agency timeliness of services and information availability as the lowest areas of customer service...consistent with the
reports from other stakeholder groups.

Factors Affecting Results
Surveys were conducted on 6 stakeholder groups: the public, marijuana licensees, alcohol licensees, liquor agents, staff and worker and service permit holders.

Respondants were selected by stratified random samples for the licensee groups, simple random samples for the staff and permit holder groups, canvenience sample for public stakeholders
(interacting with the commission's website or spocial media accounts), or a census of liquor agents.

There were a total of 1807 respondents from the six survey stakeholder groups. There was a significant difference in responses between the general public and worker permitees versus licensees,
employees and agents.  Worker and server permitees continue to be frustrated at perceived slow license processing times.

Respondants from the general public varied significantly based on the area of interaction with the commission.  Those seeking information about the bottle bill expressed the highest satisfaction with
service, while those connect about liquor sales reported the lowest, with frequent comments about corruption at the commission.

Survey respondant rates for sample were low, with less than half of sample members responding for the alcohol and marijuana licensee groups, and fewer than 20 percent response for the
permitees.  The low response rate may have sorted out less motivated respondents resulting in an unkown bias on the results.

How Are We Doing
The OLCC rate of return in FY 2023 was $2.35 for each dollar spent.  This is substantially lower than the 2.65 rate of return of 2021, but better than the 2.30 of FY 2022.  The principal difference since
2021 has been the 14 percent increase in payments to liquor agents over the 2021 rates.  Without the legislatively required increase in rate for agents, the OLCC would have a 2.4 rate of return for FY
2023.

Factors Affecting Results
Because the commission’s largest cost driver, agent compensation, is part of a rate-based formula pegged to liquor sales, even with increased sales from retail expansion, costs will rise
commensurately.  The commission will need to dramatically increase revenue levels with retail expansion and increased sales well above increases in regulatory and administrative expenditures to
attain the target rate of return of 2.5.

The commission will also experience an increased service bond obligations for the new warehouse project starting the 2024 fiscal year, the adjusted target of 2.25 may be attainable with increased
throughput from retail expansion and the new distribution center.

How Are We Doing
Marijuana Licensees recorded an 83.8% pass rate on 702 minor decoy operations during FY 2023.  This is below the target rate of 90%.

The Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission resumed regular minor decoy operations on marijuana licensees during FY 2023.

Factors Affecting Results
The commissions ability to conduct minor decoy operations at full scale is constrained by limited availability of minor decoys.  The commission continues hire decoys, but at some points during the

We anticipate that compliance will improve as retailers develop a greater perception of detection from consistent minor decoy operations.

How Are We Doing
The FY 2023 mean marijuana licensing time statewide was 96 days for the 352 cases for which a new recreational marijuana license was issued during fiscal year 2023. The median time to license
was 84 days indicating that a small number of cases disproportionately raised the average days to issuance.

Laboratory licenses took the shortest period to process, 62 days on average, while processors were issued in an average of 102 days.

Factors Affecting Results
Factors continue to be identified as business processes are developed to implement newer marijuana law and rules.  One internal factor has been the imposition of a ‘pause’ on accepting new
applications, a brief respite from the pause, and then the implementation of HB 4016.  The changes resulted in, first a stabilization in application flows, and brief surge, and then stabilization.  New
applications are often the product of license sales to new owners.

The speed of processing a change of ownership is affected by the ability of the new and existing owners to complete their business transition in a timely manner.

The commission adopted a policy of side-tracking applications from non-responsive or unprepared applicants, in order to expedite processing engaged and prepared applicants.
