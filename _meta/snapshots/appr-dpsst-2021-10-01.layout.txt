                Data Collection Period: Jul 01 - Jun 30

* Upward Trend = positive result

  Report Year                                                                     2017                               2018                               2019                                2020                               2021
Average increase in Police Officer Trainee test scores based on assessments at entry and completion of Police Basic Training.
Actual                                                                          50.99%                                54%                              30.50%                             25.05%                                21%
Target                                                                            30%                                 40%                                40%                                40%                                 40%

How Are We Doing
The average student improvement during the current reporting period was 21%. The change in test scores from entry (pre-test) to completion (final test) of students enrolled in the Basic Police
program continues to reflect an increase in knowledge as a result of the training.

Factors Affecting Results
The Basic Police (BP) curriculum continues to demonstrate a positive impact on the level of knowledge of the students. However, like 2020, the scores for this reporting period are markedly different
from previous periods. There are five notable factors affecting these results, the individual effects of which cannot be quantified.

Factor #1: The COVID pandemic continues to impact the delivery of the BP curriculum significantly, both in the form of staffing and – most significantly – scheduling. In the early stages, the two-month
academy shutdown from March to May 2020 impacted three of the classes (BP398 - 400). Once classes resumed, COVID impacted training for numerous individual students in all classes this fiscal
year. Most recently, entire classes were paused for at least two weeks at a time, including BP407.

Factor #2: The fires in September and October 2020 disrupted academy schedules for three weeks. While the academy sent no classes home, schedules were significantly modified, classes were
canceled, and the learning environment operated under hazardous air conditions. (BP402 - 403)

Factor #3: Over at least the last three years, the average pre-test scores have continued to increase (54% in 2018 to 62% in 2021), while the average final test score remains constant (90%). Factors
outside the academy, such as changing recruitment standards at individual agencies, are the most likely contributors to the jump in pre-test scores.
Factor #4: The ongoing BP curriculum revision continues to affect classes over multiple fiscal years. The first class to have all three phases of the curriculum was BP410, which will be in the 2022
report.

Factor #5: Due to a change in data collection software, the data reported in 2021 is slightly different from previous years. Moving forward, the number reported is the difference between the average
pre-test score and average final score for all BP classes in that fiscal year

How Are We Doing
The percentage of attendees ranking the usefulness of DPSST’s regional training courses above a “6” remains only slightly under the established target of 95%. Nevertheless, 2021’s 94% reflects a
continued increase since 2017.

Factors Affecting Results
Much like many areas, the ongoing pandemic continues to impact regional training in the form of smaller class sizes, limited locations to perform training, and general overall exhausted attitudes with
the events of 2020/2021.  Despite this, Regional training continues its upward trend of attendees ranking the usefulness of DPSST’s regional criminal justice training courses at a “6” or above. In 2021,
DPSST offered 114 regional criminal justice classes to 2,166 students.

Continued communication with constituents revealed that many Oregon agencies are dealing with staffing shortages and as a result, are opting to forgo training in order to maintain minimum staffing
levels in their communities. The regional team remains committed to offering the best training available and working with our constituent agencies to identify the types of training most needed.

The percentage of attendees who ranked the usefulness of DPSST fire service regional training courses at or above "6" during this reporting period was 94%. While this remains only slightly below the

communication with constituents.

Additionally, the ongoing pandemic continues to impact the regional fire training program’s ability to deliver training. However, Regional Training Coordinators and Trainers continue to work diligently
to provide training to their highest capacity and quality.  The program is continually evaluating and implementing innovative training procedures that follow COVID-19 safety guidelines to ensure the
safety of our staff and the fire service.

How Are We Doing
DPSST takes its responsibilities of enforcing the Board on Public Safety Standards and Training's (Board) certification/licensure standards for Oregon's public and private safety providers very
seriously. The agency understands the importance of the professional standards processes on maintaining the public's trust in each of the disciplines it regulates.

The agency's target is that the Oregon Court of Appeals upholds 100% of any denial, suspension, or revocation decisions appealed to them. This target reflects the seriousness with which the DPSST
and the Board approach certification/licensure denial, suspension, or revocation actions. This reporting period saw two cases sent to the Court of Appeals, but no cases overturned.

Factors Affecting Results
As stated above, the DPSST and the Board take their enforcement of professional standards very seriously. Staff, discipline-specific policy committees, and the Board evaluate and review cases with
great care. Staff ensure any denial/suspension/revocation cases have a well-developed record of the conduct involved, provide clear outlines of the particular standards against which the conduct is
measured.

For cases in which the denial, revocation, or suspension action is discretionary, a process has been developed to allow any affected public or private safety professional to provide mitigation for
consideration. This further ensures a fair and thorough process and relevant decision-making within the framework of laws and administrative rules.

How Are We Doing
For the 2021 reporting period, 470 (25% of armed PS certifications) armed private security professionals/instructors failed to successfully complete the required annual training by the due date. All 470
certifications were suspended. Of those, 469 of the certifications were timely, resulting in a rate of 99.8% timely suspensions. The remaining suspension was administratively missed and suspended
late.

Of note, 47 of the 470 suspensions were reinstated after successful completion of firearms certification.

This is a new KPM starting in this 2021-2023 biennium.

Factors Affecting Results
Current standards require armed providers to complete the firearms marksmanship qualification and armed refresher course annually to demonstrate proof of their current knowledge and skills with a
firearm. The due date for qualification and training is determined by the last successfully completed course per OAR 259-060-0120 and 0135.

This qualification and training requirement must be successfully completed, submitted to, and accepted by DPSST by the due date. Individuals who do not submit proof of successful completion of
training are emergency suspended and may no longer provide armed private security services.
The high rate highlights the effectiveness of the Board’s established training standards for Oregon armed private security providers and the DPSST’s ability to enforce these established standards.
Both of which contribute to the professionalism, public trust, public safety, and confidence in Oregon’s private security providers

How Are We Doing
The SLA and OLM courses continue to improve students’ assessment of their own confidence to apply learned knowledge and/or skills in identified areas.  The 38% increase in assessment scores is
up from the 36% increase in 2020 (see note below). In this reporting period, the percentage of improvement increased in all critical areas connected with CPE’s legislative expectations – problem-
solving, use of research, and addressing future challenges. The percent increases in these specific areas were 30%, 40%, and 49% respectively. On average, students arrive needing assistance or
practice in identified areas and complete training with increased confidence that they can apply learned knowledge and/or skills on their own.

Factors Affecting Results
Factors affecting these results include students’ various backgrounds, educational levels, and years of related experience prior to participating in the SLA or OLM courses. Additionally, during the
reporting period, CPE switched one and a half classes to a virtual format due to the ongoing pandemic. Nevertheless, results indicate positive improvements in all students following training.
