Actual                                                                           $10.93                            $36.40                             $20.62                            $12.18                             $18.56
Target                                                                           $25.00                            $25.00                             $25.00                            $28.00                             $28.00

How Are We Doing
The results were below the target level for the state fiscal year ending June 30, 2019. DOJ recovered $18.56 for every $1.00 spent, rather than the $28.00 target.
Factors Affecting Results
Very large claims can skew results and the $28.00 target was established based on years that included very large punitive damages recoveries. For example, in 2006, DOJ helped recover $25
million from parties responsible for leaving the New Carissa's rusting hulk on a south coast beach; some of the recovery actually accrued to the state in 2007. In 2012 DOJ received a punitive
damages award  of $56 million and in 2016 DOJ received a punitive damages award of $11 million, both of which significantly skewed the results in the respective years. Punitive damage awards of
this nature are rare and to a certain extent out of DOJ's control.  In addition, changes to the law have made plaintiff's lawyers less likely to include punitive damages requests in their litigation or to
settle matters outside of litigation; thereby reducing the amount of punitive damages DOJ is likely to receive in the future.

                                                                                actual     target

  Re port Ye ar                                                                 2015                              2016                              2017                              2018                              2019
% OF SEXUAL ASSAULT EXAM S CONDUCTED BY SPECIALLY TRAINED SANEs
Actual                                                                           72%                               69%                               69%                              67%                               64%
Target                                                                           85%                               85%                               85%                              75%                               75%

How Are We Doing
The results for state fiscal year ending June 30, 2019 were below the target level but consistent with prior years results.
Factors Affecting Results
DOJ administers the Sexual Assault Victims Emergency Medical Response (SAVE) Fund. The SAVE Fund helps offset costs arising from SANE training and from the examination of victims of sexual
assault by trained SANEs. The SANEs are employed by health care providers; they are not DOJ personnel. The result measured by KPM 15 is, therefore, affected directly by personnel who do not
serve under the Attorney General's direction or control. The availability of SANEs is still an issue in some areas of the state, due to both geographic challenges and lack of funding for 24/7 hour
coverage. The ongoing training provided by the Attorney General's Sexual Assault Task Force to certify more SANEs is a critical element contributing to this measure. There are approximately 135
trained SANEs in Oregon. There will always be a need for ongoing training as SANE certifications expire after 3 years.

How Are We Doing
The results for the state fiscal year ending June 30, 2019, did not meet the target.
Factors Affecting Results
This KPM represents how efficiently we are briefing cases and keeping up with the number of cases coming in. We categorize cases in terms of difficulty and then set a target time for attorneys to
spend briefing cases in each of the categories. We have no control over the number of cases that we respond to, but we can control our productivity by adjusting the time we devote to each case.
This year, three factors contributed to a decrease in efficiency: (1) We continued to have some vacancies and turnover among our support staff responsible for processing these cases, which led to
a backlog of cases not being closed, which skewed the data upon which this KPM is based. (2) For most of this fiscal year, we did not have an Honors Attorney (which we typically have). (3) One
particularly significant event (the Supreme Court allowing the cert petition in Ramos v. Louisianna) resulted in the Appellate Division diverting resources away from regular briefing duties.
 Depending on how the Supreme Court ultimately rules in Ramos, this may have a continuing effect on this Performance Measure.
