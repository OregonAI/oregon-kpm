How Are We Doing
This metric measures the percentage of the Department’s home loans (in units) that are 90 or more days delinquent or in foreclosure. For fiscal year 2025, the Department successfully achieved better
results than the Key Performance Measure target. This figure remains better than the adjusted KPM metric reset for the 25-27 biennium.

Factors Affecting Results
Oregon’s economy remained strong through the fiscal year despite changes in the federal government, with home values increasing. This contributed to a low percentage of mortgage delinquency,
especially when compared to the Federal VA loan program delinquency in Oregon. ODVA has not experienced any foreclosure activity that resulted in a State-Owned Property (SOP) during the past 6
years.

How Are We Doing
In 2022, 2023 and 2024 fiscal years, the Department enjoyed a high level of new mortgage loan origination. This was attributed to ODVA offering better then prime mortgage rates through QVM
Bonds. 2025 experienced a minor slow down. Continued high prime rate and concerns in the economy given recent federal changes could have lowered buyer confidence. ODVA will monitor if future
target adjustment is required.

Factors Affecting Results
The high level of private sector home loan rates provided ODVA with a highly competitive rate for the past few years. The increase in loan production had a positive effect on the loan servicing
portfolio, resulting in the high levels of loan dollar amounts. Recent concerns on the national economy could have had an impact on the portfolio but loan origination still remains high. Future inclusion
of a refinance program is anticipated to increase loan origination.

How Are We Doing
The Oregon Veterans' Homes averaged 83% occupancy for the fiscal year 2025. Individually, the Veterans' Home in The Dalles averaged 70% occupancy and the Lebanon Veterans' Home averaged
95% occupancy.

Factors Affecting Results
Average occupancy levels at Oregon Veterans' Homes has been steadily increasing over the last several years. Some challenges impacting census at the Oregon Veterans' Home in The Dalles
include lack of provider resources, location, and continued staffing challenges.  The largest factor affecting admissions to The Dalles Home is the inability to serve veterans with behavioral and mental
health needs.  This is due in part to the specialized training needed by staff to care for these veterans and lack of payor sources as well as facility adaptations required for behavioral health residents.

How Are We Doing
The two Oregon Veteran Homes have historically offered, and will continue to offer, private pay rates for veterans below the median rates charged by private skilled nursing facilities in Oregon. ODVA
wants to ensure all veterans who require skilled nursing care have access to the high quality, veteran specific care offered in our two Homes. We accomplish this by offering private pay rates that are
lower than those charged by private nursing facilities across Oregon.

Factors Affecting Results
The factors influencing the veteran private pay rates generally include traditional cost drivers in the long-term industry including the needs of the veteran requiring care and the critical costs of
recruiting, training, and maintaining a qualified nursing workforce who provide care to residents in the Veterans' Homes. Each year direct care nursing costs increase at the Homes.  Reimbursement
rates from the USDVA and Medicaid increase as well, although these increases have not historically been sufficient to offset the increased cost of care and operations.

How Are We Doing
Women veteran engagement in Oregon continues to grow year over year. A key metric that is important to monitor is the number of new women veterans who are engaging with ODVA and seeking

Factors Affecting Results
Strong drivers of this engagement includes outreach events such as Women Veterans Day, partnering with the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs Women Veterans programs, health care events,
and the ODVA Women Veterans Conference which happens once every two years. In FY25, ODVA, in conjunction with County and Tribal Veterans Service Officer partners engaged with 583 new
women veterans.

How Are We Doing
This is the first iteration of this KPM, and the Agency will continue to look for ways to modify this performance measure.   The original goal was set to the performance of a single grant, but the
adjusted goal is based on engagement across several ODVA programs to match the KPM narrative. The goal is the total number of veterans with housing instability who engage with the agency for
benefits and service. This number includes veteran engagement through the Veterans Services Grant (VSG), The Oregon Emergency Financial Assistance Program (OVEFAP), The Campus
Veterans Resource Center Grant (CVRCG), and ODVA’s Homeless Coordinator (HVC) casework.

VSG - 1,785

OVEFAP - 144

CVRCG - 10

HVC - 10

Total of 2,153

Factors Affecting Results
Results are directly related to agency advocacy efforts and those of our partners administered through the Veterans Services Grant. As ODVA and the US Department of Veterans Affairs continues to
prioritize efforts and resourcing to curb the tide of housing instability and homelessness, we expect engagement to continue to rise.
