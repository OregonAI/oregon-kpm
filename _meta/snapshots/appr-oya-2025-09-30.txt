How Are We Doing
(5a)
The agency met the target for 2025. While there were ten incidents of suicidal behavior in OYA facilities, this is a decrease from FY 2024 and is the lowest number of incidents in the last five
years.
(5b)
The agency did not meet the 2025 target. Suicidal behavior incidents decreased from 3 in FY 2024 to 2 in FY 2025; however, we exceeded the target of 1 or fewer incidents.
Factors Affecting Results
(5A)
Youth placed in Close Custody facilities are at an increased risk for suicidal behavior. Risk of suicidal behavior increases for youth who have a history of substance abuse, mental illness, trauma,
suicidal behavior, and who are placed in a restrictive environment and separated from their community support systems.
OYA assesses for suicidality at intake and a reassessment cadence is determined based on the risk level identified in the initial assessment. Risk level is also evaluated anytime there is suicidal
ideation or behavior. OYA has a policy that aligns supervision and intervention with each youth’s risk level and sets communication expectations for the living unit
Actions:
No action items are identified because this goal has been met.
(5B)
Policy outlines requirements for assessing suicidality throughout a youth’s commitment to OYA. These requirements include determining when a mental health evaluation and suicide risk
assessment should occur, consulting with mental health staff to identify appropriate safety interventions and accessing crisis services when there is an immediate risk of lethal self-harm/suicidal
behavior. In addition, suicide risk levels are reassessed during transitions to community or secure placements.
In addition, contracted programs are required to have suicide prevention policies and procedures including how the program will respond when a youth exhibits self-injurious/self-harm suicidal
behavior.  OYA certified foster care providers are required to implement suicide prevention techniques and additional reporting requirements per Oregon Administrative Rule.
Actions:
OYA remains focused on youth safety and suicide prevention, including a recent review of its “Suicide Prevention in the Community” policy with staff. OYA provides Question, Persuade, and
Refer (QPR) training every two years to teach professionals how to identify situational and behavioral warning signs of suicide and connect youth to appropriate resources. In addition, OYA is pursuing
a new training program that incorporates cultural awareness into understanding suicide risk. Additionally, OYA has also increased its efforts to connect all youth with the Oregon Health Plan and other
services when they are eligible, so the youths' care and treatment are not disrupted due to service coverage lapses.

How Are We Doing
The agency did not meet the 2025 target, although there was a slight increase compared to last year.
Factors Affecting Results
Timely access to education records can be inconsistent due to the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA), which grants parents and students rights to access and control the disclosure
of records. Under current law, families retain educational rights even when the court designates OYA as the legal guardian of a youth. As a result, schools require a signed release of information from
a parent before educational documents can be shared delaying timely response to youth’s individual educational needs.
Actions:
OYA wants to begin exploring the ability to develop a standard FERPA-compliant release of information form and pursue policy changes that would make this document a routine part of the
intake process when a youth enters OYA custody.

How Are We Doing
The agency did not meet its 2025 target. However, performance showed a slight increase from 35.6% in FY 2024 to 36% in FY 2025.
Factors Affecting Results
This KPM relies on JJIS for reporting, although the Oregon Judicial Department’s (OJD) Odyssey data system is the official record.  A factor that continues to impact this measure is that it only reflects
information on restitution payments when the condition is closed in the JJIS system. Additionally, it only includes restitution orders of $10,000 or less; larger orders are excluded as outliers. As a result,
a relatively small number of youth with combined, large restitution orders can significantly affect payment rates.
In addition, youth in Close Custody facilities have limited opportunities to earn money. Those in community placements can seek employment or participate in work programs to make restitution
payments, however employment options are often limited due to education and behavioral health service requirements.
Actions:
Administrative staff in field offices track restitution payments through the Odyssey system, and restitution goals are monitored during case audits to support accurate records.  OYA will
continue discussions with OJD on a potential data interface between the modernized JJIS and their system.

How Are We Doing
The agency did not meet the 2025 target; satisfaction levels increased on four of the six components but decreased on two.
Factors Affecting Results
This measure reflects responses from 72 family members of youth served by OYA.
To improve response rates, OYA implemented a series of targeted strategies to improve our customer service
metrics. Key improvements included simplifying survey content, broadening our communication outreach, and leveraging mobile platforms to enhance accessibility and engagement.
Additionally, factors such as recent lawsuits involving OYA, questions regarding Professional Standards Office practices, media attention, and ongoing investigations may have impacted satisfaction
scores.
Actions:
OYA continues to look for ways to better serve families and provide them beneficial services and information. The Office of Inclusion and Intercultural Relations oversees the agency’s family
engagement services and is actively seeking ways to strengthen collaboration with families. This year, the office is developing an orientation video for families with youth in OYA facilities expanding
access by providing families with additional opportunities to receive the material. The video will be available in English and Spanish, with the potential to add other languages as needed. To further
strengthen communication, OYA will continue to provides families with regular email updates on relevant news, activities and initiatives. OYA’s Family Advisory Council also serves an additional
resource to identify how we can better serve families.
In addition, Governor Kotek has appointed KO Berger as OYA’s first-ever Youth & Family Advocate; an action we believe will meaningfully improve our results on the family customer-service survey.
