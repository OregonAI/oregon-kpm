How Are We Doing
The Oregon Racing Commission has realized a downward trend in equine catastrophic injuries over the past five years, despite a small uptick in 2017.

The target is 1.5 horses euthanized per 1,000 starters.

The totals are shown in percentages.

1.5 = 100% of goal

1.82 = 82.4% of goal for 2017

1.35 = 135.8% of goal for 2013-17

For the past year, Oregon is slightly above the national average in the number of horses euthanized per 1,000 starters.  The national average on dirt racing-surfaces is 1.7 horses euthanized per
1,000 starters; Oregon’s average for 2017 was 1.82.   However, over the past five years, the local trend has been downward, with an average of 1.35, better than the national average and below
Oregon’s more aggressive target of 1.5.
Factors Affecting Results
Looking back 5 years provides a more accurate statistical picture than a single-year snapshot because of the limited number of races run in Oregon.  Annually, Oregon sees 60 days of racing; for
comparison, California has 642 race days. A variety of factors influence catastrophic injuries in race horses, including: condition of the horse, training methods, race track surface, medications,
jockey skill and racing luck.

How Are We Doing
The ORC Licensing Office is meeting its target turnaround time for processing a license.
Factors Affecting Results
Two full-time employees provide licensing functions.  Staff workload changes depending on racing schedules, and applicants requiring extensive background checks take more time.

Efficiencies were made in allocating staff time to allow additional, dedicated staff hours to assist with processing licenses.  Cross-training of staff has provided better coverage of the office for
licensees.

How Are We Doing
No news is good news: A lack of data on customer satisfaction can be construed to mean customer satisfaction is high.  If licensees are unhappy with the Commission, they let us know.

The Commission’s success hinges upon collaboration with our stakeholders.  We reach out to, and have a good working relationship with, the Oregon Horsemen’s Benevolent & Protective
Association, the Oregon Thoroughbred Owners & Breeders Association, the Oregon Quarter Horse Racing Association, and the Advanced Deposit Wagering (ADW) companies.  Our efforts include
setting policy through the administrative rules process. Cross-training staff for better coverage and accepting credit cards continue to have a positive effect.
Factors Affecting Results
As a regulatory agency, the Commission's possible barriers could include: negative feedback form a licensee because the Commission was unable to issue a license due to the applicant's criminal
background; or negative feedback because the Commission imposed fines or sanctions because of rule violations.
