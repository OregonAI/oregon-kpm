How Are We Doing
Licensees are required to complete 30 hours of continuing education annually, including two hours of ethics or professional conduct. The Board audits approximately 20% of licensees annually
to verify that these continuing education requirements are met. In 2017, the Board conducted 612 continuing education audits. Of those, twenty-nine were found to be out of compliance. The
resulting percentage of licensees audited who were found to be in compliance was 95.3%.
Factors Affecting Results
The Board strives for 100% compliance with continuing education requirements. Licensees who do not satisfy the continuing education requirements are subject to disciplinary action, including but
not limited to a civil penalty and/or license suspension or revocation.

How Are We Doing
The Board takes its consumer protection role very seriously. Many complaints are acted upon within one day of receipt. Complaints involving return of documents, unsatisfactory service or similar
issues can often be resolved to the consumer's satisfaction within a few business days. Twenty-two of the 25 consumer complaints received in 2016 were responded to within three days of receipt.
All were responded to within five days of receipt.
Factors Affecting Results
The Board strives to contact a complainant within 24 hours to confirm receipt of the complaint. However, the Board only has two full-time staff, which makes it difficult to follow-up on some complaints
immediately if the Compliance Specialilst is out of the office.

How Are We Doing
This is a new measure for 2017. Last year's pass rate on the tax preparer exam was 63.9. This is a significant increase from the prior year's average of 36.1%. The Board is working with instructors
of basic tax courses, (the basic tax course is a pre-requisite for the exam) to promote effective teaching and classroom practices and ensure the courses adequately and appropriately cover the
required curriculum.
Factors Affecting Results
The IRS requires all tax forms and publications to be in English only. Therefore, the Board's licensing exams are only offered in English. Some non-native English speakers have difficulty translating
and understanding somewhat complex tax issues, which affects their exam results. The Board also conducted a thorough review of all exam questions and question categories in 2016. This resulted
in significant changes to the composition and length of the exam.
