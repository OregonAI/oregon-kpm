How Are We Doing
Alcohol licensees recorded a 78% pass rate on 1327 alcohol minor decoy operations.  This is below the target of a 90% pass rate.
The Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission resumed full-scale minor decoy operations in FY 2023 after a two and a half year hiatus for the COVID-19 emergency.  FY 2024 represented the first full
year of  target tempo of operations.
FY 2024 saw the first minor decoy operations on home-delivery services.  The first round of delivery MDOs had a 33% pass rate on three operations.
Factors Affecting Results
The Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission resumed minor decoy operations in FY 2023.  Licensees and their employees seem to have developed a low-perception for likelihood of detection as
initial operations resulted in very low initial licensee pass rates.  As the rate of inspections has increased to full tempo in FY 2024, licensee pass rates have returned to historical averages, which are
still well below the target pass rate of 90%.
Licensees participating the Responsible Vendor Program outperformed non-participant licensees with a 5% improvement in pass-rates for RVP members.
The commission is continuing to hire minor decoys in order to increase the supply of decoys in each of the regions to improve operational output.

How Are We Doing
The rate of second violations for FY 2024 was 13%, with 58 premises out 445 receiving violations for two separate cases over the prior 3 years.  That is a 4% increase over the prior reported rate, but
a return to the pre-COVID-19 average.
Factors Affecting Results
The increased rate over the prior fiscal year results is driven by several factors. The resumption of full-scale minor decoy operations starting in FY 2022, many more violations have been detected and
referred for adjudication.  The way data is managed has also allowed dis-aggregation of cases with multiple separate charge events rolled into a single settlement.  In the past, those were treated as a
single case event.  The rate of violations is also being calculated this year to include any repeated violations by premises within 3-years of the reporting year.
The administrative hearings unit has also had several case presenters added to staff and has established a higher rate of processing cases providing more possible adjudications for the increased
flow of cases sent from compliance officers.

How Are We Doing
The FY 2024 mean licensing time statewide was 134 days from receipt of the application, slight reduction from last year’s time-to-license of 137 days. This is above the target of 75 days. The
Commission continues to adapt to changes in licensee privileges extended by the legislature in the 2021 session.
The commission is focused on restoring business processes and modernizing the license application process in order provide prompt service to alcohol licensees. Commission staff have been busy
transitioning to new online licensing system this fiscal year:  It is anticipated that the new system will help applicants file correct applications and streamline commission management of licensing
workflows.
Factors Affecting Results
There are many factors affecting the number of days it takes to issue a liquor license; both internal and some external.
Internal factors continue to be identified and streamlined through process improvements and technological solutions.  The legislature changed licensee privileges during the 2021 session.
Additionally, several alcohol license investigators were re-assigned to help with processing the backlog of marijuana license renewal investigations.  Monthly break-down of days to license show
substantial slowing in process times during the second half of the fiscal year when the workload for implementation of the new software system was at its highest.
External factors affecting processing times are applicant responsiveness to the process and the license application review by the local governing body (city or county). Statute gives local governments
up to 90 days (45 days plus an additional 45 day extension â€“ if requested) to review a license application within their jurisdiction and provide a recommendation (positive, negative, or neutral). The
OLCC cannot complete the processing of an application until the local government review is completed

How Are We Doing
This year the Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission customer service survey resulted in an average rating of 73% across all 6 survey questions and the 6 stakeholder groups that are surveyed by
the commission.  The commission missed the 85% evaluating service as excellent or good in all categories.  Alcohol licensees and worker and server permit holders responded with average
satisfaction that exceeded targets in all categories except for timeliness and availability of information.  The lowest ratings came from the employee stakeholder sample.  Frustrations with timeliness
and accuracy marked the lowest average scores for that group (43 and 63% respectively).  The marijuana licensee stakeholder group was also most frustrated with timeliness and information, with
many comments noting that staff were helpful, but processes slowed by implementing new software and confusing legal and regulatory environment.
Factors Affecting Results
Implementation of new licensing software systems impacted the perception of timeliness and information availability for marijuana and alcohol licensees.  Alcohol and marijuana licensees expressed
frustration with software vendor support for licensees trying to navigate all three of OLCC's vendor-run online interfaces (CAMP, OPTO and METRC).  The public expressed suspicion of liquor store
agent practices regarding rare liquors, and related comment on the past rare liquor scandal.  Liquor store agents respected their DM's but want more education and face time with staff.  THey also
expressed frustration with POS and returning order mistakes.

How Are We Doing
The OLCC rate of return in FY 2024 was $2.06 for each dollar spent.  With lower revenue and higher operating costs, the ROI for FY 2024 was lower than 2023 and below the reduced target of $2.25
per dollar spent.
Factors Affecting Results
The reduction in the rate of return is driven by the drop in revenue from reduced case sales and flat prices combined with higher operating costs related to cost-of-living adjustments for staff and the
debt service payments for the warehouse and technology projects.
With less revenue and higher operating costs, the ROI for FY 2024 was lower than 2023 and below the reduced target of $2.25 per dollar spent.
Inflation indexing of agent compensation also ensures that agent compensation (the largest ongoing merchandising cost) will continue to grow as a share of revenue.
The adjusted target of 2.25 may be attainable in the future with a return to sales growth plus increased throughput from retail expansion and the new distribution center.

How Are We Doing
The average time to license was 95 days for the 260 cases for which a new recreational marijuana license was issued during fiscal year 2023. The median time to license was 86 days indicating that a
small number of cases disproportionately raised the average days to issuance.
Application processing time dipped slightly from last year, despite the activation of a new online processing system during FY 2024. License investigators were able to implement the new system
without losing time over last year.
Factors Affecting Results
There are many factors affecting the number of days it takes to issue a recreational marijuana license; some internal and some external.
Internal factors continue to be identified as business processes are developed to implement newer marijuana law and rules.  The commission has also implemented a new online licensing software
system.  Implementation has taken staff time for testing, training and refining business practices.  It has also been a learning process for licensees.  We anticipate that the new portal and licensing
management system will ultimately reduce redundant processes and unify management of the licensing process to improve times and customer experience.
Another internal factor helping reduce the load of novel applicants has been the moratorium on accepting new applications.  New applications now are the product of license sales to new owners.
The issuance of Land Use Compatibility Statement by the local governing unit is the triggering event that allows the Oregon Liquor Control Commission to assign an investigator to begin actively
processing a marijuana application.  That is why assignation of an investigator is the start of the count for the KPM.
Applicants have also struggled to complete applications correctly and in a timely manner. The commission adopted a policy of side-tracking applications from non-responsive or unprepared applicants,
in order to expedite processing engaged and prepared applicants.
