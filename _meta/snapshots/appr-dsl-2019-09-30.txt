How Are We Doing
The Department did not meet either target. Revenues for forest lands have remained stagnant and costs have increased as a result of the cessation of harvest on the Elliott State Forest and the
Department’s extensive efforts investigating creation of a Elliott State Research Forest with Oregon State University and to decouple the Forest from the Common School Fund.  Costs also
increased in FY2019 for non-forest land management.
Factors Affecting Results
Measure 2a is driven by the Elliott State Forest (ESF). The ESF was historically a major revenue source for the Common School Fund. However since 2013 ownership and management of the ESF
has resulted a net cost.  At the direction of the State Land Board the Department is currently collaborating with Oregon State University to develop a plan to transfer the Elliott State Forest to the
University for use as a research forest. This process resulted in significant costs – primarily consultant contracts for facilitation and development of a Habitat Conservation Plan needed for
Endangered Species Act coverage.  Another factor affecting this performance measure is the on-site management of the Elliott through a professional forest management company as well as the
Department’s hiring of a forester to oversee all forest land activities.
Operations costs on the non-forest lands increased as a result of significant fire protection costs on rangeland properties in southeastern Oregon. Another factor affecting this increase is the work
associated with development of the Department’s South Redmond tract as a Large Lot Industrial site with the City of Redmond.  Through a professional services contract, the Department has made
significant progress to annex the property into the city limits and better position the property for future development and sale. The Department also hired two staff - a property technician and
ownership specialist.  These positions are necessary for improved management of trust lands and to ensure agency ownership documentation and records are current and accurate.

How Are We Doing
We received $69.8 million and returned $33.8 million. Our refund rate was 48.5%, which is 5% higher than in FY 2018. We returned and additional $1.35 million to owners compared to FY 2018.
We continue to improve our owner location efforts. During FY2019, we returned $10.3 million to owners as a result of our direct efforts. Skip-tracing tools are used to obtain improved addresses,
and letters are sent to all living owner accounts at $250 or more. We currently perform a manual owner location attempt on any account with stock valued at $1,000 or above before any sales. As
time allows, staff also conduct a 2nd attempt on larger accounts including those where the apparent owner is deceased per external research.
During FY 2019 claim requests began to regularly exceed our capacity to review and approve claims within the required 120 days. Our request for a new claims examiner position to address the
increase in claims volume was denied during the legislative process.  To operate at a more effective level and provide timely return of funds to the rightful owners, the unit could use three new
positions to process claims, focus on owner location efforts and address the exponential growth in garnishment requests.
We will ask for additional resources, while continuing to analyze our processes and leverage technology to maximize our efficiency.
During FY 2018, we planned to upgrade our technology to improve the customer experience and operational efficiency. Our goals were to have paperless claims and an automated verification and
approval for simple claims. Such technology already is in use in more than 30 states, and users are very satisfied with the impact on their operations. During FY 2019, we negotiated and proceeded
with implementing KAPS, the most innovative system currently available on the market. The system went live on September 8, 2019. We are optimistic that we will see an enhancement of all unit
functions, including simplifying report processing, securities management, audit tracking, and streamlining of claims processing. Efficiencies from the new system will be captured in the FY 2020
reporting.
This is the second year for this measure, but data exist to show a longer trend. In the last 10 years, we returned $265 million. Our 10-year average return rate is almost 42%. FY 2019 is our best
year to date.
The Unclaimed Property Professionals Organization surveys states on their unclaimed property rate of return the last survey covers the 2013-2017 period. During that time frame, Oregon ranked
18 of 51 states and had a return rate of 42%. Only three states returned more than 60% of receipts to their owners.
Factors Affecting Results
There is increased demand on claims staff’s time. They must process claims, arrange time to conduct owner location activities and process garnishments, which have grown in volume to account for
almost 20% of their work. Any publicity or outreach effort results in more claims, which extends our claims workload well beyond our capacities.
During FY 2019, some staff time was dedicated to legislative work, administrative work and planning for the implementation of the new system. This affected the number of hours dedicated to claims
work. In addition, the unit manager, who regularly worked claims to relieve workload pressures, retired; that further affected productivity.
We are also directing attention to reporting non-compliance and having increasing success in receiving more unclaimed property each year.  While our total claim refunds have been agency
records in the last two years, our receipts have also broken records during the same period. During FY 2019, we received $2.2 million more unclaimed property than in FY 2018, which corresponds
to a 3% increase.

Staff continue to apply for grants and seek other funding opportunities to support Reserve operations and projects. Opportunities over the past year and successful applications for grants and

All programs are currently using some form of structured needs assessment and/or evaluation surveys identify audience-driven needs for program development, information and services. During
