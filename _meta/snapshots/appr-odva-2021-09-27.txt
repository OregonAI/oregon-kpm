How Are We Doing
This metric measures the percentage of the Department’s home loans (in units) that are 90 or more days delinquent or in foreclosure. For fiscal year 2021, the Department successfully achieved better
results than the Key Performance Measure target.
Factors Affecting Results
The Department has historically had a lower percentage of loan accounts that were 90 or more days delinquent or in foreclosure as compared to other lenders. This lower percentage of serious
delinquent accounts is a factor of prudent loan underwriting and timely contact with borrowers experiencing payment difficulties. The reason for the slight increase from the previous year was directly
related to the reduction in the number of loans in the servicing portfolio.

How Are We Doing
Prior to 2020, the Department experienced five consecutive years of increases in loan origination volume. In 2020 and 2021, loan production decreased due to a variety of factors including: historically
low mortgage interest rates for conventional loans; shortage of housing inventory on the market; and the impact of COVID-19. The combination of those factors have contributed to a decrease in loan
origination volume for 2021
Factors Affecting Results
The open market interest rates remained at historic lows during 2021. Generally, ODVA has higher loan origination production when there is greater differential between the rates ODVA is able to
provide and rates in the conventional market. Additionally, the volume of houses available for sale was very low - limiting the opportunities for buyers to purchase a home.

How Are We Doing
The two Oregon Veterans' Homes averaged 78% occupancy for fiscal year 2021. Individually, the Veterans' Home in The Dalles averaged 71% occupancy and the Lebanon Veterans' Home
averaged 86% occupancy.
Factors Affecting Results
Over the past 5 years, other private Skilled Nursing Facilities (SNFs) in Oregon have averaged occupancy rates of approximately 65%. Historically, the occupancy levels at the Oregon Veterans'
Homes have been significantly higher the private SNFs. Average occupancy levels at the Oregon Veterans' Homes decreased in 2021 due to the COVID-19 pandemic. Factors for the decrease
include impact of COVID-19 in the Veterans' Homes (since March 2020 through June 30, 2020, 33 residents had tested positive for COVID-19, including 13 residents who passed away) and the
suspension of admissions of new residents at the Veterans' Homes during the past fiscal year.
The Veterans' Homes are limited to admitting only eligible veterans, spouses and parents that medically require skilled-nursing care. Due to federal regulation, 75% of the occupancy in each
Veterans' Home must be veterans. Both Veterans' Homes provide high-quality care that honors veteran service and is sensitive to military culture and aging issues. Both Veterans' Homes also have
endorsed memory care communities, which is very rare for nursing homes in Oregon.

How Are We Doing
The two Oregon Veteran Homes have historically, and will continue to, offer private pay rates for veterans that are below the median charged by Oregon skilled nursing facilities. By offering this
reduced rate, veterans who require skilled nursing care can have the same access to veteran-specific care in our two Homes at a rate lower than the private market in civilian nursing facilities across
Oregon.
Factors Affecting Results
The factors influencing the veteran private pay rates generally include traditional cost drivers in the long-term industry including the needs of the veteran requiring care and reimbursement from the
Federal VA/Medicare/Medicaid sources. Inherent in these factors are the critical costs of recruiting, training and maintaining a qualified nursing workforce who provide care to residents in the
Veterans’ Homes. Due to the COVID-19 Pandemic, private pay rates in the future may continue to increase to cover the additional costs of care during these unprecedented times.

How Are We Doing
The Oregon Department of Veterans’ Affairs (ODVA) strives to provide the highest quality of claims representation to Oregon’s veterans.  ODVA is responsible for accrediting and training County,
Tribal, and State Veteran Service Officers to ensure veterans and families are served by skilled, certified professionals.  These Veteran Service Officers are assisted by a strong ODVA Appeals team
which represent veterans and families in USDVA Board Hearings, and Virtual Hearings. This performance measure compares the average annual Oregon disability compensation and pension benefit
payment made to an individual veteran (actual) to the national average (target).  The initial Federal Fiscal Year 2020 target of $16,916 was established based upon historical average rate of growth.
The target for 2020 has been adjusted to $17,771.96 to reflect the actual national average for 2020. Oregon’s average disability and pension compensation amount is approximately 3.5% less than the
national average.
Actual 2021 data is anticipated to be released by the USDVA in September 2022.
Factors Affecting Results
The factors influencing the dollar amount of a disability compensation and pension payment include the quality of representation (reflected in the knowledge and expertise of the Veteran Service
Officer and board hearings officer) and the type of claim (disability compensation or pension) presented to the USDVA.  Other factors include the age and overall health of veterans.  For instance,
older veterans may receive a pension benefit, which is designed to support them by augmenting fixed incomes burdened by exceptionally high out of pocket medical costs such as assisted living
facilities, or to prevent homelessness for those veterans with an extremely low income.

How Are We Doing
Partnerships between the Oregon Department of Veterans’ Affairs (ODVA), Tribes, and Counties providing services to veterans and families have never been stronger. Coordinated efforts to reach
and engage with veterans from all walks of life and historically underserved populations continue to grow. The number of new Powers of Attorney (POA)/Appointments of Representation provided by
veterans to ODVA, Tribes, and Counties is generally a reflection of new claims being filed on behalf of veterans and their family members. For fiscal year 2021, there was a decrease in the total
number of new POAs from the prior year.
The impact of the COVID-19 pandemic cannot be overstated for this KPM. The traditional methods of reaching underserved, houseless, or under resourced veterans and families through outreach
and events known as “Standdowns” were largely cancelled or postponed due to social distancing requirements.
Further, the shift to online advocacy meant that communities without stable or solid internet were highly impacted. Agency efforts to tackle these challenges have come from the hiring of specialized
coordinators such as the Houseless Veteran Coordinator, Women Veteran Coordinator, and very shortly, the LGBTQ Veteran Coordinator. Additionally, ODVA has now entered into MOUs with three
of the nine federally recognized tribes in Oregon to provide training and accredited claim representation from Tribal Veteran Service Officers.
Factors Affecting Results
The primary factor affecting recent results was a 2015 directive from the USDVA mandating that all historical POA’s older than five years old be renewed. These POA renewals resulted in substantial
increases to the total number of POAs filed between 2015-2016 as established clients had to be refreshed. Starting in 2017 and continuing into 2020, the number of new POA filings began to average
to totals typically seen prior to 2015. Due to the leveling off of POA’s, after the required refresh from USDVA, the Oregon state legislature has adopted new targets for this KPM beginning in FY22. In
FY22 the new target goal will be 7,100 POAs, and in FY23 the new target goal will be 7,650.
Despite the reduction in total POA’s and decrease in overall population of veterans as Oregon’s veteran population age, outreach efforts to underserved veteran’s population has improved. For
example, over 1,200 new claims submitted in FY21 for houseless, elderly, and extremely low income veterans as well as surviving spouses. Additionally, there were over 3,300 new appeals and
supplemental claims filed to assist veterans and claimants in ensuring that they are receiving the maximum benefit they are entitled, at the earliest possible effective date.
Note: Beginning in March 2020, County and Tribal Veteran Service Offices, and ODVA experienced an immediate decrease in monthly POA’s obtained. The noticeable decline in new POA’s is
attributed to the reduced ability for in-person meetings and more limited outreach activities due to the COVID-19 Pandemic. This decrease continued through the completion of FY21. Additionally,
ODVA was able to run its own POA reports that provide verifiable and repeatable data, resulting in a restatement of actual POAs dating back to 2015.

Factors Affecting Results

How Are We Doing
From July 1, 2020 through June 30, 2021, 6 months after exiting to a permanent destination, 80% of Veteran households who were contacted after receiving OHCS homeless assistance
funds had retained their permanent housing.
This is in-line with the target of 80%.
Factors Affecting Results
It is important to note that the denominator used in this calculation is the number of people successfully contacted after six months, not everyone who was due for a follow-up. The KPM therefore
reflects the percentage of those contacted who are still in permanent housing after 6 months and does not include those who were not contacted.
There are several possible reasons some clients were not contacted after exiting homeless assistance programs. Due to the way the past KPM report is written in the data system, not everyone due
for a follow-up was being entered into the system. Therefore, it is probable that some people due for follow-up were not contacted. In response, OHCS staff are working to update the report across a
wider range of homeless services programs to automatically prompt service providers to conduct the correct number of follow-ups. This has resulted in improved information and data accuracy in
several areas of reporting and the goal is to expand it to additional programs. OHCS recognizes that improving data quality and reports is an iterative process and is continuing to work on addressing
these issues over time.
As with all programs this last year, COVID-19 impacted program delivery. While traditional Homeless Services Programs at OHCS have continued, there have been multiple new COVID related
emergency rental assistance programs. These programs have been larger in scale than other previous rental assistance programs. Due to the emergency nature of these programs a more limited set
of data is collected—and in particular the six month follow up is not required. Therefore, these programs do not factor into the KPM, but it is important to note the history investments in rental
assistance over the last year.
Obstacles to continue meeting the 80 percent goal include: difficult economic circumstances, high unemployment rates, a shortage of affordable housing units, low rental vacancy rates, impacts from
COVID -19, and poor data quality and collection over the past several years. Many of these challenges are systemic and OHCS and partners will continue to increase the rates of housed Veterans.
Lastly, the KPM is only a limited snapshot of the services provided to Veterans and their families. OHCS housing and shelter programs provided services such as emergency shelter, rapid rehousing,
case management and more to Veterans throughout the state. In addition, the OHCS Veteran Leadership Team helped convene our State counterparts, Federal partners, and other service providers
to discuss best practices and build collective knowledge through peer-to-peer sharing across regions. Through increased collaboration and improved data, we are hopeful in continuing to make
progress towards placing more Veterans in permanent housing.
