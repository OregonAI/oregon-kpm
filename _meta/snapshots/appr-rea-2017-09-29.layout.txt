How Are We Doing
For 2017, 90% of those property managers and principal brokers who completed the compliance review process, including the follow-up survey, came into compliance within 45 days of the review
completion.
Factors Affecting Results
Since clarifying the compliance review process in the initial invitation, beginning in 2015, participation has doubled on average.  Participants are now forewarned that they won't complete the review
until a follow-up survey is submitted.  With that information participants are no longer inadvertently abandoning the process.

As participation has increased the sample size has become less vulnerable to skewing based on the outcome of a single review.

How Are We Doing
For the fiscal year ending June 30, 2017 85% of cases were completed within 150 days of receipt of the complaint which is considerably above the 60% target.
Factors Affecting Results
Retaining senior staff and recruiting employees with the education and expertise to investigate cases with the necessary understanding of the complexity of real estate and financial accounting

Since FY 2014 the Agency has mostly had a fully staffed Regulations division with minimal turnover. The most junior investigators have gained experience in the field and most investigative staff
have had advanced training, aiding in the Agency's ability to exceed our targets.

Some factors  that may affect the outcome of the length of a case will be outside of the Agency's control, such as, unavailable witnesses or information that contribute to lengthier investigations.

How Are We Doing
In FY 2017 the Agency had a decrease in resolving cases through informal settlement resolution. In FY 2017 the Agency underperformed in this measure by resolving 73% of cases, 22% below the
95% target.
Factors Affecting Results
The Agency continues to value the importance of the settlement process in the resolution of disciplinary actions. The respondent has the opportunity to meet with the Regulations Division Manager
after each investigation is complete to go through the settlement process. This allows each party to better understand each other, thus improving the number of cases settled without a formal
administrative hearing. A higher number of cases that are taken to the formal hearing process would require training additional staff in the preparation and administrative functions necessary to
represent the Agency. This would also increase costs to the Agency significantly for each hearing as well as increased workload for the Office of Administrative Hearings. However, it is not
uncommon for respondents to decline a settlement agreement because they want the opportunity to present their position in a formal administrative hearing.

How Are We Doing
For 2017, 67% of new licensees rated the examination as "good" or "excellent" as an effective screen for competent and ethical professionals.
Factors Affecting Results
Licensees new to the real estate business find that the examination material is not related to performance or success in the industry and may provide negative responses as a reaction.

In the 2017 Legislative session the Agency proposed a modification to the data gathering process which was supported by the Joint Committee. Prior to July 1, 2017 the Agency sent the survey to
licensees that had been licensed for exactly 6 months. At this stage in their new profession, the licensee's recollection of the exam is distant and instead tends to reflect on their preparation for
success in the industry, which the exam is not meant to assess. Starting on July 1, 2017 the Agency began to send the survey to licensees that have attained the new license within the last month.
By collecting information from licensee earlier, the Agency is able to gather a response that is nearer to the exam process and the licensee can more accurately reflect on the information the
measure is aiming to assess.

The Agency continues to monitor pre-license education course content. The Agency meets periodically with the examination vendor and Board to improve examinations by reviewing the test items
for both accuracy and relevance.

How Are We Doing
The Agency continues to exceed all targets related to customer service.
Factors Affecting Results
The Agency has prioritized customer service for applicants and licensees through the efficiency and ease of use of the online licensing system as well as by striving to provide "live-answer" support
by phone and "real-time" email response during business hours.

In June 2016 the Agency transitioned to the new statewide IBM telephone system. Prior to this transition the Agency had a receptionist that answered all calls live and routed those calls to available
staff members. While this supported our live answer mission the Agency did not have data to verify that calls were being handled efficiently. The Agency configured the new system to route calls
equitably among staff while emulating the live answer model, having all callers greeted by a live staff member quickly. Over the last year since implementing the system, Agency management has
monitored performance and made adjustments as needed to maintain high performance in customer service.
