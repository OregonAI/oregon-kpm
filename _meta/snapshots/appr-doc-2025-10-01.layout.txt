How Are We Doing
The Department did not meet the 80% compliance target for the 2024–2025 reporting period. The percentage of Ballot Measure 17-compliant Adults in Custody (AICs) remained at 69%, consistent
with the previous year. While this indicates stability, it also highlights persistent challenges in achieving the constitutional 40-hour work/education requirement. A majority of AICs continue to engage in
meaningful work or educational activities, but systemic and operational constraints remain barriers to full compliance.

Factors Affecting Results
Custody and Classification Constraints: Only AICs classified as minimum custody and meeting strict public safety criteria are eligible for outside work crews, limiting the pool of participants. Program
Prioritization and Scheduling Conflicts: Education and treatment programs, especially for substance use, take precedence over work assignments. Limited provider availability often leads to
scheduling conflicts. Stable Population, Shifting Exemptions: While the overall AIC population remained stable, a slight increase in exempt individuals and a marginal decrease in compliant AICs
affected the compliance rate. Resource Limitations: Funding constraints continue to restrict the expansion of work programs and staff support, particularly for outside work crews. Restrictions on
certain populations (e.g., sex offenders) further reduce eligible participants. OCE Program Participation: Oregon Corrections Enterprises (OCE) continues to provide critical work opportunities. Over
2,300 AICs completed more than 120 hours of OCE training, with approximately 1,400 participating at any given time. However, institutional transfers and limited job availability in minimum institutions
hinder broader participation.

The Department remains committed to improving compliance with Measure 17 through strategic and operational initiatives: Workforce Development: We are aligning programming with projected job
market trends to better prepare AICs for post-release employment. Community Engagement: Staff are expanding partnerships with local agencies to create more host agency crews and community-
based work opportunities. Root Cause Analysis: Institutional teams are actively identifying barriers to compliance and recommending targeted improvements. OCE Expansion: OCE is growing its
Business Strategy Team and Advisory Council to address labor shortages and expand fair chance employment partnerships. The Reentry and Pre-Release Hiring Program continues to show promise,
with 29 AICs receiving job offers prior to release in its first year. Future Focus: We aim to increase the number of qualifying work assignments and reentry partnerships to improve compliance and
support successful reintegration.

How Are We Doing
After several years of steady decline, this period shows a small increase in arrests, though conviction and incarceration rates continue to fall. The supervision cohorts reviewed here were the first since
the COVID-19 recovery and the rollout of BM110—two major shifts that likely influenced the system. These changes may have led to higher arrests as processes adjusted, while lower conviction and
incarceration rates can be partly explained by courts struggling to keep pace and by the ongoing defense crisis, which continues to slow case resolution today. Because so many factors affect re-
arrest, conviction, and incarceration, it’s difficult to point to just one reason for the shifts in this years supervision cohorts.

Factors Affecting Results
The Department and county community corrections agencies put considerable effort into assessing AICs and developing a comprehensive corrections plan that will address identified risks and needs.
This includes use of the Level of Service/Case Management Inventory (LS/CMI), a measure of risk and need factors, as well as a fully functional case management tool.   Additionally, the department
and county community corrections agencies utilize the Women’s Risk Need Assessment (WRNA) tool which targets identification of risks and needs correlated with the female population.  Ongoing
system improvements have been made to better target in-prison interventions and to conduct more careful and coordinated release planning. In addition, community corrections agencies statewide
continue to work collaboratively with the department to enhance the effective and efficient transition of AICs from prison to our communities. Such efforts are centered on a commitment to implement
and enhance evidence-based practices throughout Oregon’s criminal justice system.

The department continues to target interventions and resources on those at highest risk to commit new crimes as well as focus on the preparation and transition of AICs back into the community with
the goal of reducing future criminal behavior. However, it is recognized that in addition to our efforts, there are many factors impacting recidivism, to include individual local county practices.

KPM #10          Leave Programs - The percentage of Adults in Custody (AIC) who complete transitional leave and non-prison leave (AIP)
                 Data Collection Period: Jan 01 - Dec 31

* Upward Trend = positive result

                                                                            actual     target

 Report Year                                                               2021                             2022                            2023                             2024                            2025
Leave Programs
Actual                                                                    87.80%                          86.63%                           91.60%                          84.08%                           84.36%
Target                                                                      85%                             85%                              85%                             85%                              80%

How Are We Doing
                                                  This KPM reflects the performance of two varieties of DOC transitional leave programs: Non-Prison Leave associated
                                                  with the Alternative Incarceration Program (AIP), and Short-Term Transitional Leave (STTL).

                                                  The Oregon Department of Corrections offers AIP to inmates who have been sentenced by a judge as eligible for AIP,
                                                  and who apply to participate and meet other statutory and department policy requirements to participate. The
                                                  programs are based on intensive interventions targeted at criminogenic risks, rigorous personal responsibility and
                                                  accountability, physical labor, and service to the community. Additionally, AIP consists of an institutional phase for a
                                                  minimum of 180 days, followed by a period of non-prison leave of up to 90 days, for a total minimum program length
                                                  of 270 days.   During the institutional phase, these programs provide 14 hours of structured and regimented routine
                                                  every day for a minimum of 180 days. Adults in Custody (AIC) who are eligible to participate in AIP are screened
                                                  through the multidisciplinary Treatment Assignment Screening Committee to determine the most appropriate program
                                                  to meet the AICs’ needs.

                                                  The Department also offers STTL of up to 120 days to AICs who are not enrolled in an Alternative Incarceration
                                                  Program. As with AIP Non-Prison Leave, AICs must meet the statutory and department policy requirements to be
                                                  approved for Short-Term Transitional Leave.

                                                  Regardless of whether Non-Prison Leave or Short-Term Transitional Leave, participants are closely monitored for
                                                  compliance with their transition plans while in the community. The higher the percentage of successful completions,
                                                  the greater the success.

                                                  For the 2024-2025 reporting period, the transitional leave completion rate for STTL remained consistent (82.5%) when
                                                  compared with the 2023-2024 reporting period (82.5%). The completion rate for AIP Non-Prison Leave during 2024-
                                                  2025 increased (90.5%) when compared with the completion rate during the 2023-2024 reporting period (89%).

                                                  Short-Term Transitional Leave (STTL) participation decreased during the 2024–2025 reporting period, averaging 93.2
                                                  individuals per month, down from 103.6 in 2023–2024. This decline is primarily attributed to a continued decrease in
                                                  prison admissions over the past 3–4 years, along with a shortage of suitable housing options. Additionally, a
                                                  persistent barrier to expanding participation in AIP transitional leave is the limited availability of treatment program bed
                                                  space, which serves both AIP and non-AIP populations.

                                                  DOC remains committed to refining internal processes to better identify and support individuals most suited for
                                                  transitional leave, particularly STTL. Ongoing automation efforts and a continued emphasis on robust, individualized
                                                  transition planning are central to enhancing reintegration outcomes and ensuring public safety.

Factors Affecting Results
As noted, overall participation in transitional leave programs has declined, primarily due to reduced prison admissions over the past 3–4 years and a shortage of available housing. While there was a
slight increase in successful completion rates for AIP during the reporting period, the improvement was marginal and cannot be attributed to a single identifiable factor.

High standards continue to govern the approval process for both Non-Prison Leave and Short-Term Transitional Leave. While statutory eligibility is a prerequisite, AICs must also meet all applicable
Department policy requirements to be granted leave. This rigorous screening process is a key factor contributing to the consistently high success rates among program participants. When AICs
complete the in-custody portion of their programs and have access to suitable private or county-approved housing upon release, the core objectives of the transitional leave programs—successful
reintegration and reduced recidivism—are more effectively achieved.

How Are We Doing
Factors Affecting Results
       Law enforcement agencies have no legal obligation to report employee arrests or charges to DOC
       While it is probable that most agencies would inform DOC of charges against our employees, there is no formal process or requirement to do so
       Communication occurs on an ad hoc basis, creating potential gaps in information
Similarly, courts do not bear any responsibility to notify DOC when employees are charged with or convicted of criminal offenses, including assault. Without proactive monitoring or a formal notification
system, DOC may not become aware of all relevant cases.

For employees certified by the Department of Public Safety Standards and Training (DPSST), we explored whether DPSST might maintain relevant records. However, upon contacting DPSST, we
learned that they also do not have a systematic method of tracking charges or convictions for assault offenses among certified staff.

At present, there is no reliable source of data that would allow DOC to accurately respond to this request. Providing incomplete or potentially inaccurate information would be misleading. Developing a
comprehensive tracking system for this type of information would require establishing formal reporting relationships with law enforcement agencies, courts, and other relevant entities—infrastructure
that does not currently exist.

Every Use of Force is thoroughly reviewed and graded.  Only those Use of Force incidents which are outside of the Use of Force Rule (OAR 291-013) or where an injury has occurred require a Full
