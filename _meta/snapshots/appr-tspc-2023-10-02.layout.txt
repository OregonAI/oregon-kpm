How Are We Doing
The COVID pandemic continued to affect TSPC staff ability to respond timely to emails and messages. At the beginning of this reporting period, the number of emails and messages pending was over
2,000. Given that the number of questions continued to increase during the first few months of the period, the agency fell even further behind. However, with the Licensure Unit at full strength the
agency was able to begin reducing the backlog. By April 11, 2023, the team was able to reduce the pending emails to under 100 and the number of messages to under 90. With the coming of late
spring, the number of emails and messages from educators moving to new positions and new teachers applying for licensure increased, thereby increasing the total number of messages pending. For
the entire reporting period, 19.2 % of the days saw emails and messages responded to within 3 days.

Clearly, this continues to be unacceptable; so much so, the commission included improvement in these measures within its new 2022-2025 Strategic Plan. Though the agency saw improvement by
the end of the reporting period, the unit was reduced by 2 positions in the 2023-2025 biennium budget. This will further challenge the agency succeeding in its customer service goals.

Factors Affecting Results
The effects of the COVID pandemic continued through much of 2022. This resulted in a continued strain on the email and message response time due to increased inquiries related to restricted and
substitute licensing and emergency substitute licenses. Additionally, it will be another two years before a new license processing system will be fully in place. Thus, the agency continues to receive
numerous emails and requests for assistance related to the accessibility of the licensing system. Once the agency added and trained two limited duration customer service position, the number of
pending emails and messages began to decline. This was good news; however, it has been short lived given the elimination of these positions in the current biennium budget.

How Are We Doing
Prior to the COVID pandemic, the agency’s evaluator staff remained at 4.5 FTE, and with the operational improvements made by agency and little staff turnover during that, backlogs continued to
drop. In 2021, the agency reached a high of 47% of applications being processed within 30 days. However, during 2021-22 COVID resulted in a huge increase in workload, with no change in staffing.
The increase in customer questions, ongoing challenges with the licensing system, and increased applications due to educator turnover and new licensing types contributed to the agency having no
applications processed within 30 days for the 2022 reporting period.

For the 2023 reporting period, the agency has begun to rebound. Eleven percent (11%) of applications were processed within 30 days. This number is not near the 47% high of 2021, let alone the 60%
target. However, analysis of monthly data helps explain why this slight improvement is nonetheless significant. This is explained in more detail in the following section.

Factors Affecting Results
In an average processing year, the decrease in monthly applications received during November through March allows staff to catch up on applications. Most applications during those months are
processed within 30 days. However, In January-February 2023, the agency was still receiving some of the highest monthly applications due to continued impacts from the pandemic, including the
continued availability of emergency substitute teaching licenses. January 2023 saw an average of 70 days processing time. In March through May 2023, the agency caught up, with average
processing time of 33, 29, and 35 days, respectively. During the summer months of 2023, the agency experienced the gradual increase of applications received due to new teacher applications. The
agency kept processing times to within 6 weeks for much of this period – a number which is generally good for that time of year.

How Are We Doing
The Professional Practices Division is dedicated to reducing the number of cases which have been pending investigation for ten or more months. The Key Performance Measure (KPM) established for
the Professional Practices Division sets a lofty goal of having 75% of TSPC investigations which have not pended in Other Venue completed in six months or less. The TSPC Professional Practices
Division has always prioritized student safety cases over all other case types, regardless of the date received. The passage of Senate Bill 155 (2019) has gone a step further by making Educator
Sexual Conduct cases the top priority for TSPC investigations upon reception of the complaint.

Due to our commitment to investigate Senate Bill 155 cases and other existing prioritizations, TSPC cannot devote 100% of agency investigation personnel to addressing the backlog of cases 100% of
the time. We are required to shift limited agency personnel and resources between tackling Educator Sexual Conduct and other student safety cases immediately, and addressing a backlog of lower
priority cases when time and circumstances allow us to do so. This is why the Division must address meeting the KPM strategically, and over an extended timeframe. But when the Division achieves
our strategic annual goals of bringing the backlog of cases to within ten months or less of the present date, logic dictates that the Division’s KPM numbers will reflect dramatic improvement.
Unfortunately, for the 2023 reporting period, only 30% of the case investigations brought before the commission were completed within 180 days.

Factors Affecting Results
In order to meet this KPM target, staff will need to eliminate, through completion of investigation, existing cases which have been awaiting TSPC investigation for extended periods of time (ten or more
months). Only by systematically eliminating the back-log of cases to be investigated, and by bringing the oldest case pending TSPC Investigation to within ten months since the case was received by
TSPC, will the Professional Practices Division be able to legitimately have a discussion about coming close to meeting the established KPM. To that end, the Professional Practices Division has
established annual goals which commenced in 2020 and will run through 2025. These goals are purposed with reducing and eliminating the TSPC cases pending investigation for longer than ten
months.

Unfortunately, the 2023 reporting period has been impacted by the agency being without one of its four investigators. One of the agency's investigators was on approved leave from late 2022 through
mid-spring 2023, and again from late spring 2023 until September 30. With a 25% reduction in investigative capacity, the agency saw significant reductions in the number of cases being completed
within 6 months. The drop in production was particularly evident in the first quarter of 2023, with only 10% of the cases before the commission being investigated within the 6-month target. The agency
has since rebounded, but without additional staff support, it will be difficult to make up for this loss.

How Are We Doing
The agency received a total of 14 new program proposals or major modification requests for the period June 7, 2022 through June 6, 2023. Of these, 100% were processed for approval within 30
days. The Educator Preparation & Pathways Unit is responsible to process EPP proposals for educator licensure of all Oregon educator licensure including teacher, principal, professional
administrator, school counselor, school psychologist, and school social worker. Any of these programs in private or public state-approved institutions that lead to Oregon licensure must be approved
by the Teacher Standards and Practices Commission prior to completion of candidate programs.

Further, the timelines for approval are quite restrictive.  Proposals must be reviewed and recommended for approval to the Commission according to the following timeline:
       Submission 30 days before Commission Meeting
       Recommendation for approval at Program Approval Meeting of Commission 14 days prior to Commission Meeting
       Consideration for approval at Commission Meeting
Factors Affecting Results
As educator preparation programs continue to move forward post-pandemic, many have begun review and modification of their licensure programs. Many are looking at innovative instructional
delivery models or updating course or clinical experiences. Each of these requires either a minor or major modification request to be approved by the agency. The agency continues to be successful
on this KPM. Per the legislative KPM report from 2023, and given the continued success of this measure, the agency will begin considering a new performance measure to work toward.

How Are We Doing
For this report the agency conducted a month-long survey blast, where agency units and social media reached out to customers to respond to customer service surveys. While the overall agency
customer service was generally positive at 63.5% of respondents rating overall service as excellent or good, the data shows there are two stories depending on the relationship the respondent has
with the agency. One hundred percent of respondents who were school district or education service district customers rated overall service as excellent (83%) or good (17%). For respondents from
education preparation programs or others involved with program approval and educator pathways, 90% rated customer service as excellent (60%) or good (30%).

However, for respondents to the general licensure survey (primarily licensure applicants), no respondent rated the customer service as excellent or good. Seventy-five percent (75%) rated the
customer service as either below average or poor. Comments from respondents overwhelmingly reflected dissatisfaction with the difficulty in actually talking to a customer service representative.

Factors Affecting Results
Capacity is the most critical factor affecting customer service. The agency has increased staff in the Educator Preparation & Pathways unit from 1.2 FTE five years ago to 6 FTE currently. The number
and range of expertise of staff has increased the ability for customers to receive timely and knowledgeable service. Likewise, license evaluators have established relationships with school district
human resources staff, which involves responding to staffing and licensure questions. These relationships naturally support a more timely, knowledgeable response to inquiries.

Clearly, the customer service challenge is in responding timely to license applicant and educator questions, as well as having the staff to respond via phone to inquiries. This indicator is affected by
two primary concerns. First, the agency continues its process of replacing the license application system. The current system requires staff to develop workarounds to many processing tasks, which
take time away from providing customer service. The agency emphasis on processing applications within 30 days further reduces the time that license evaluators can contribute to customer inquiries.
This leaves much of the applicant customer service to the agency's two customer service representatives. In the previous biennium, the agency was afforded three customer service representatives
and one licensure office assistant. During this time, TSPC saw improved timeliness to inquiry response and more personalized follow-up with customers. The 2023-2025 biennium budget eliminated
the office assistant and one public service representative positions, as well as eliminating the agency's reciprocity specialist, who spent much of the time responding to inquiries from out-of-state or
foreign customers.
