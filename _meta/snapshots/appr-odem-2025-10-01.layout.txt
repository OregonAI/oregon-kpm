In Fiscal Year 2024 (FY24), the Oregon Department of Emergency Management (OEM) Exercise Program strengthened statewide preparedness through the delivery and support of 13 exercises.
Statewide there were 118 exercises tracked by OEM coming from state, local and Tribal governments in FY24. The largest exercise in 2024 was IronOR 24, a major statewide functional exercise
involving 122 public and private sector agencies, 17 State Emergency Coordination Center (ECC) Emergency Support Functions (ESFs), with a total of nearly 700 participants. OEM also conducted
quarterly ECC exercises, technical workshops, and two functional area summits to build capabilities and promote coordination across local, Tribal, state, and federal partners. Emergency
management exercise reporting for both Emergency Management Performance Grant (EMPG) and Oregon Revised Statute (ORS) 401 requirements improved by more than 10 percent. This indicates
both the success of and the ongoing need for exercise outreach and support. Several key initiatives were launched or expanded this year. The Exercise Program received EMPG dollars to reduce
exercise event participation barriers and released a revised Exercise Technical Assistance Program (ExTAP) Guide to provide support to organizations. OEM also improved its Emergency
Management Exercise Reporting (EMER) process through an online platform and refined tools for data collection and analysis. A new prioritization matrix guided exercise assistance and contributed
to a high success rate in FEMA’s National Exercise Program (NEP), with four of five supported applications awarded. This is more NEP exercise support than previous years combined. Looking
ahead, OEM will focus on preparing for the 2026 state-level exercise, expanding internal drills and Continuity of Operations (COOP) exercises, developing a digital resource library, and formalizing an
exercise cadre. These efforts will build statewide capability, improve accessibility to training and resources, and ensure a more resilient and coordinated emergency management system across
Oregon.
Factors Affecting Results
In 2024, 39% of CEMP agencies participated in exercises, consistent with 2023. While 94 percent of the Emergency Support Function (ESFs) and 100 percent of State Recovery Function (SRFs)
were represented in exercises, not all organizations with an identified role in those functions participated. OEM has no authority to require agencies to participate in OEM coordinated exercises. The
CEMP includes a wide array of partner organizations in addition to governmental organizations. Exercises and the CEMP are developed with the whole community in mind. Although there was a high
level of participation in the IO24 State Level Exercise, many of the participating organizations are not listed in the CEMP. This indicates a need for targeted outreach and engagement with CEMP
organizations.

The results of the 2024 Maturity Model self-assessment indicate a foundational level of preparedness across state agencies’ baseline capability in supporting Emergency Support Functions (ESFs)
and State Recovery Functions (SRFs) responsibilities with both groups primarily at the initial stages of the Maturity Model. This Year 1 assessment reflects the early stages of the new assessment
iteration, setting an updated baseline for continued development over the next five years. The ESF assessment results indicate that most agencies reporting out to ESFs are at foundational maturity
stages, with 57% of participants at either Initial or Repeatable stages. This highlights the need for targeted efforts in process documentation, cross-training, and resource allocation to advance ESFs
toward greater preparedness and resilience. The SRF assessment results reflect significant initial-stage engagement from state agencies, with 80% of respondents at Stage 1. This baseline
underscores the need for immediate support in process development and training to improve SRF preparedness and establish a structured approach to recovery functions. Overall, the 2024 self-
assessment establishes a critical starting point for the new iteration of the Maturity Model Program. The results highlight the foundational nature of both ESF and SRF capacities, with a clear need for
focused development in standardized processes, role definition, and sustained training efforts. This baseline will serve as a benchmark for measuring progress toward optimized emergency response
and recovery capabilities over the next five years.

Factors Affecting Results
In 2024, 29% of Emergency Support Function (ESF) agencies reached Stage 3 and 20% of State Recovery Function (SRF) agencies reached Stage 2 of the Maturity Model. However, this data is
likely not representative as only 68% of ESF agencies and 28% of SRF agencies participated in the assessment. OEM has no authority to require agencies to complete the assessment. The Maturity
Model is a five-year initiative to assess and enhance the capabilities of state agencies that respond as ESFs and/or SRFs within Oregon's Comprehensive Emergency Management Plan (CEMP).
Annually, ESF and SRF agencies are asked to complete a self-assessment of their progress along five stages of maturity. 2024 was the first year of a new five-year framework that incorporated
updates and lessons from the previous assessment initiated in 2019.

The mission of the State 9-1-1 Program is to administer the legislative mandate for the statewide 9-1-1 emergency communications system. The emergency communications system provides uniform,
prompt and efficient access to public and private safety services for the citizens of, and visitors to, the State of Oregon.​ Part of that work is the collection and distribution of 911 and Enhanced 911
(E911) fees and charges by the states, the District of Columbia, and U.S. territories (states and taxing jurisdictions), as required by 47 U.S.C. § 615a-1(f)(2), under the New and Emerging
Technologies Improvement Act of 2008 (NET 911 Act), as amended by the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021.

The rules are codified at 47 CFR Part 9, Subpart I.  The rules provide that acceptable expenditures of 911 fees or charges for the purposes of the statute are limited to (1) the support and
implementation of 911 services, and (2) operational expenses of Public Safety Answering Points (PSAPs).  As codified in ORS 403.130 (1) A 9-1-1 jurisdiction shall create and maintain a 9-1-1
jurisdiction plan for emergency communications services provided within a 9-1-1 service area pursuant to ORS 403.105 to 403.250 and rules adopted by the Oregon Department of Emergency
Management. The 9-1-1 jurisdiction shall submit the 9-1-1 jurisdiction plan to:

     (3) The department shall review the 9-1-1 jurisdiction plan for compliance with the requirements imposed under ORS 403.105 to 403.250 and rules adopted by the department, and if the plan is:

      (a) In compliance, the department shall approve the plan.
      (b) Not in compliance, the department shall reject the plan.

      (4) If the department rejects the 9-1-1 jurisdiction plan under subsection (3) of this section:

      (a) The 9-1-1 jurisdiction shall revise and resubmit the plan within 90 days after the date the department rejects the plan; and

      (b) The department shall review the revised plan and either approve or reject the revised plan within 90 days after the date the department receives the revised plan.

      (5) Each 9-1-1 jurisdiction shall submit to the department in writing within 30 days any change to a public safety answering point that alters the approved 9-1-1 jurisdiction plan on file with the
department. The changes may include, but are not limited to:

      (a) The address of the public safety answering point;

      (b) Telephone numbers used to satisfy requirements set forth in ORS 403.115;

      (c) Director changes;

      (d) Agencies served by the 9-1-1 jurisdiction; and

      (e) The method used to direct an emergency call once received by the primary public safety answering point.

      (6) If an established 9-1-1 jurisdiction proposes to move a public safety answering point to another location or a governing body proposes to establish a new 9-1-1 jurisdiction with a new primary
public safety answering point, and if either of these proposals will result in control of the 9-1-1 service area by an agency or agencies other than the agency or agencies identified in the approved 9-1-1
jurisdiction plan filed with the department, the 9-1-1 jurisdiction or governing body shall submit a revised 9-1-1 jurisdiction plan setting forth the changes to:

      (a) The department;

      (b) Public and private safety agencies in the 9-1-1 service area; and

      (c) Any other public or private entity in the 9-1-1 service area that may be affected.

      (7) In addition to meeting the requirements imposed under ORS 403.105 to 403.250 and rules adopted pursuant to ORS 403.120, the revised 9-1-1 jurisdiction plan must describe the capital and
recurring costs for the proposed components of the emergency communications system within the 9-1-1 service area.

      (8) The department shall review the revised 9-1-1 jurisdiction plan for compliance with the requirements imposed under ORS 403.105 to 403.250 and rules adopted pursuant to ORS 403.120 and,
if the department determines that the plan is in compliance, approve the plan.

      (9) The department may not approve a revised 9-1-1 jurisdiction plan submitted under subsection (6) of this section unless the revised plan is accompanied by written approval of the governing
bodies of all public and private safety agencies affected by or providing service in the 9-1-1 service area. [Formerly 401.755; 2015 c.247 §10; 2021 c.539 §62]

The Oregon Department of Emergency Management enhanced the reporting process in 2024, migrating from a burdensome email and local document-based submission to a cloud-based survey data
development, distribution and data analysis platform. This enhancement has reduced administrative burden to the department and the PSAPs, created continuity of operations opportunities and
streamlined data reporting and analysis.

Factors Affecting Results
The 9-1-1 landscape changed drastically in 2024 with many Publics Safety Answering Points (PSAPs) experiencing unprecedented staffing shortages. As a result, some PSAPs were combined which
resulted in a numeric value change to the denominator (i.e. fewer PSAPs submitting plans) drastically impacting the numerator (i.e. plan submission value decreased by 2.3%). OEM has implemented
a major process improvement and plans to conduct annual refresher training and provide additional technical assistance in hopes of meeting or exceeding the benchmark for 2026 reporting period.

The Oregon Department of Emergency Management migrated from an annual survey emailed to a limited distribution list of external partners to a survey link embedded in the email signature template
of each staff member. This survey tool invites feedback specific to each interaction and offers the respondent the opportunity to request someone from the OEM leadership team to contact them to
share more about their experience. OEM has exceeded benchmarks across all facets of the customer satisfaction metric and continues to seek opportunities to expand outreach and engagement
opportunities to all we serve.

Factors Affecting Results
Survey participation is voluntary resulting in a small representation of actual interactions. Increased socialization through social media campaigns could increase awareness and participation. There is
no mechanism to monitor or audit employee adoption and no ramifications if staff fail to include the survey link.

Factors Affecting Results
OEM conducted it's second employee engagement survey in October of 2024 noting engagement scores slightly below the intended benchmark. In response OEM launched a series of focused
initiatives strengthen engagement and improve scores for the 2025 survey period. First, OEM partnered with Gallup to provide managers with customized access to tools and resources that support
team development without allowing access to sensitive survey data. Using Gallup's Engagement Action Plans, each section manager reviewed their team's results and developed tailored action plans
to address areas of low engagement. These actions plans were completed 9/19/2025 and are expected to improve engagement scores agency-wide. Additionally, At the leadership level, OEM is
implementing a Director 360 evaluation, concluding in September 2025, to identify opportunities for growth and continuous improvements to agency culture. Beginning in 2026, these 360 evaluations
will expand to all executive leadership and management team members, creating a consistent practice of accountability and professional development. Finally, the agency recently seated a Chief
Resilience Officer dedicated to strengthening organizational culture through mentorship, employee support programs, and interest-based engagement groups and training and professional growth
opportunities.

The Audit KPM aligns with strategic goals by integrating risk management into daily operations and governance, ensuring audit recommendations are resolved on time to strengthen accountability and
agency performance across all operations. The metric provides a clear point-in-time snapshot of audit accountability. Because the source data is openly available and the percentage is easy to
calculate, it works well for both leadership and public reporting.

Factors Affecting Results
Limitations:
        Point-in-time only: The metric shows status at the reporting date but does not capture the history of whether items were late in the past or how long they have been overdue.
        No severity weighting: All recommendations are treated equally, even if some address higher-risk issues than others.
        Management could report that corrective action for an audit recommendation has been completed without effectively mitigating the related risk. This is mitigated by independent validation of
        corrective actions, and recommendations are only closed within the tracker by the auditor.
