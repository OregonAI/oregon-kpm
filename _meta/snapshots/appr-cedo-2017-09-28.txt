(

About the Targets
The chronic absenteeism metric is complementary to an average attendance rate. The average rate includes the days attended divided by total days for all students; chronic absenteeism is the
percentage of students who miss 10% or more of school days. Schools and districts can have relatively high attendance rates but also have high chronic absenteeism rates, as the first metric
considers the entire school population while the second accounts for each individual student.

How Are We Doing?
During the 2015-2016 school year, 86% of 6
grade students are not chronically absent. Students are considered "not chronically absent" if they attended 90% or more of their enrolled days
between the beginning of the 2015-16 school year and May 2, 2016. This means that 14% of 6

high school graduation, this number must improve.

How Are We Doing
Our Strategy
The CEdO reports annually to the Legislature on the state’s progress in this area, coordinating with data drawn from ODE, TSPC, and HECC (see current and former reports at:
http://education.oregon.gov
). The report also draws from insights, analyses, and feedback from close to 20 various stakeholder groups then vetted by a 20-member Educator Equity Advisory Group
that helps assess, evaluate, and advocate for educational policy supporting the diversification of Oregon’s educator workforce.
Whereas some states only track teachers of color, Oregon has set a goal for increasing the number and percentage of culturally or linguistically diverse teachers employed in Oregon public schools
in order to meet local districts desire to hire bilingual educators as well as educators of color. The 2017 report shows slow but steady increases in the number and percentage of culturally or
linguistically diverse teachers and administrators employed in Oregon’s public schools. Likewise, a positive trend can be seen in the racial diversity found in enrollment and completion data for
candidates completing teacher preparation programs. However, a sizable gap still remains between the diversity of students in Oregon’s K-12 schools (38.9%) and the diversity of our classroom
teachers (10.1%) and school/district administrators (11.4%). This is particularly notable in 31 Oregon school districts who serve a K-12 student population whose cultural or linguistic diversity
ranges between 40 to 82%.
About the Targets
The KPM target is significantly higher than the percentage of Oregon educators who are culturally and linguistically diverse in 2017. Implementation of the Educator Equity Advisory Group’s
actual target
Statewide Plan (2016) via SB 182 will help boost preparation, recruitment, hiring, retention, and professional development planning and coordination, helping build a culturally responsive, well-
prepared, and diverse educator population in the State of Oregon. Continued and coordinated Legislative attention and efforts led by the CEdO in this area will allow more rapid progress to occur. It
is anticipated that several early career pathway partnerships now underway involving districts and educator preparation programs will contribute to positive KPM improvements realized within the
next two to three years.
The Educator Equity Advisory Group has identified a number of barriers and challenges that impact the diversity of Oregon’s educator workforce such as early on-ramps, course transfer and career
pathways, financial supports, hiring practices, and career advancement. With the passage of SB 182 during the 2017 session, Oregon will be able to offer scholarships to reduce debt for teacher
candidates who mirror the demographics of Oregon’s culturally and linguistically diverse students. The scholarships will be managed by the HECC Office of Student Access and Completion and will
help improve retention of candidates enrolled in educator preparation programs by providing financial support during their educator preparation coursework and clinical experiences when they are
less likely to be able to work even part-time.
The Educator Equity Advisory Group plans to meet with members of each of the other statewide equity committees to identify common interests and opportunities for further collaboration to move
the dial on this KPM. The CEdO will also be convening a Socratic Seminar discussion engaging district recruiters, community college leaders, and educator preparation deans and directors to
identify each stakeholders’ concerns, strategies, needs, and recommended actions and policy changes relative to educator workforce needs in Oregon. The group will also be engaged in several
statewide discussions regarding the transfer of community college coursework to reduce the loss of credits and provide smoother pathways for teacher candidates, many of whom enroll first at a
community college.
How Are We Doing?
Data from 2016 show that Oregon has hired 163 more culturally or linguistically diverse teachers, bringing the State’s total percentage to 10.1% (N = 3,133). Each year the Oregon Department of
Education revalidates and updates educator equity data for current
and
past years. Due to this revalidation process, previous KPM reports for 2014-15 and 2015-16 are now inaccurate. The
correct percentages show a year-to-year increase in the number of culturally and linguistically diverse teachers over the last three years (2014-15: 8.9%, 2015-16: 9.8%, and 2016-17: 10.1%). The
largest increase in 2016-17 came from culturally and linguistically diverse teachers who, when compared to 2015-16, have shown a greater increase in percentage hired than for all teachers hired.
Factors Affecting Results
Independent decisions made by preparation programs, school districts, and pre-service and practicing teachers are ultimately beyond the control of the CEdO. Up to this time, there has been
insufficient funding allocated to the universal application of promising practices like educator mentoring. While the passage of SB 182 will coordinate efforts across key education stakeholders to
help boost preparation, recruitment, hiring, retention, and professional development planning and coordination, it will take time to see significant outcomes with respect to increasing the diverse
educator population in the State of Oregon.
What Needs to be Done?
Oregon’s Educator Equity Advisory Group’s Statewide Plan (2016) will help guide action and build advocacy around the visioning and valuing of diversity “by creating pathways to increase cultural
and linguistic diversity in its educator workforce and by assisting all educators in becoming more culturally responsive.” New and strengthening activities within CEdO and collaborative partners via
SB 182 will continue to focus efforts on building a stronger and more diverse educator workforce statewide.
About the Data
The data is collected for both licensed and employed educators in all public K-12 schools and is from the July 2017 Oregon Educator Equity Report. For the purposes of this KPM, educators are
defined as teachers; the above numbers do not consider administrators.

How Are We Doing
Our Strategy
A critical element of CEdO’s mission, the research and policy service drives strategic planning, legislation, investment, and subsequent practice across a P-20 seamless system of education. Over
time, the charge of the agency’s research and policy staff has shifted from serving the needs of a board and sub-committees to supporting cross-agency initiatives. This is largely achieved through
community, stakeholder, and cross-sector engagement and collaboration focused on an evidence-based, equitable, outcome focused approach to education policy and practice. It also includes the
organization, coordination, and convening of committees, workgroups, councils, hubs, and teams (e.g.., Education Agency Research Team [EART]) to collaborate on research and translate findings
into promising policy and practice aimed at improving P-20 student outcomes.
How Are We Doing?
The rating for CEdO’s research and policy service is below the target, but has improved substantially over the prior year’s report. The shift to a project-based approach to surveying customers
increased the response rate (49% with 145 total respondents in 2017, versus 22% with 37 total respondents in 2016). Compared to 2016, the percentage of stakeholders who rated the quality of
the research and policy service as “great” or “excellent” increased by 12 percentage points, from 63% to 75%.
Changes in staffing and the reorganization of priorities stemming from the 2017 Legislative Session will subsequently alter research and policy capacity, activities, and outreach within the agency.
However, the resultant shift in research and policy focus (i.e.., a narrowed scope for policy directives with continued cross-agency and key stakeholder collaboration for broader research and policy
actual target
advancement) will maximize service to key customers while simultaneously being attuned to the needs of the greater education system (and therefore reaching a broader customer base). Similarly,
adopting a new, project-based approach to surveying CEdO’s customer base will provide more accurate, timely, and helpful feedback with respect to satisfaction with research and policy service
and opportunities for improvement over time.
Factors Affecting Results
With a shift in research focus over time, it has proved a challenge in previous years to define the customer base in any meaningful way. Findings from earlier surveys reflected general statements ill
defined or applicable to our specific research and policy consumers paired with a very low response rate. To overcome this, the CEdO shifted their customer service survey to a project-based
approach for 2017, meant to more accurately capture targeted feedback from those directly involved, connected to, or impacted by the day-to-day and specific research and policy work of the
agency. Surveys were sent to key stakeholders (i.e.., partners, collaborators, and consumers of work, both cross-agency and community-based), and question frames were customized to more
directly address or prompt project-specific aspects to more clearly target overall satisfaction and performance measures. In this way, results may be more accurate and targeted for this year’s
report.
What Needs to be Done?
Project-based surveys will continue be sent to key customers at intervals that ensure accurate and timely capturing of satisfaction data across all CEdO initiatives. With continued development,
implementation, and bolstering of cross-agency and community-based partnerships, the overall capacity for education research at large will increase and therefore enable the CEdO to provide
better research and policy service to an even broader customer base.

Our Strategy
Changes to the charge and overall restructuring of the CEdO over time has necessitated the refinement and redefinition of our customer base. We have a very small staff and a large set of
customers, as shaped through our mission and continued focus on cross-agency and community engagement, outreach, and partnerships/collaboration in building and coordinating a seamless P-
20 education system. Our strategy is to continue to identify the customers and entities that are key points of leverage in achieving agency and state goals and to focus or target our service to
identified needs and opportunities. Expanding cross-agency and community-based groups convened by CEdO to develop and align legislative concepts and their associated budget components is
a moving target that will require continued attention to the engagement, outreach, and feedback from our customer base over time.
About the Targets
These targets are similar to those of other agencies and represent where the agency needs to be over the next two years.
How Are We Doing?
Ratings for the various aspects of CEdO’s agency service are slightly below targets, but substantially improved over the previous year’s report. The shift to a project-based approach to surveying
customers increased the response rate (49% with 145 total respondents in 2017, versus 22% with 37 total respondents in 2016). Compared to 2016, the percentage of stakeholders who rated
CEdO’s service as “great” or “excellent” overall increased by 15 percentage points, from 66% to 81%. In fact, all metrics specific to agency service increased from 2016 – “helpfulness” increased by
four percentage points (from 76% to 80%); “availability of information” increased 17 percentage points (from 56% to 73%); “expertise” increased 13 percentage points (from 70% to 83%);
“timeliness” increased 18 percentage points (from 60% to 78%); and “accuracy” increased 16 percentage points (from 66% to 82%).
Changes in staffing and the reorganization of priorities stemming from the 2017 Legislative Session will subsequently alter research and policy capacity, activities, and outreach within the agency.
However, the resultant shift in focus will maximize service to key customers while simultaneously being attuned to the needs of the greater education system (and therefore reaching a broader
customer base). Similarly, adopting a new, project-based approach to surveying CEdO’s customer base will provide more accurate, timely, and helpful feedback with respect to satisfaction with
agency service and opportunities for improvement.
Factors Affecting Results
The restructuring of the CEdO over time has prompted significant and pointed efforts toward more clearly defining the agency’s mission and charge. The resultant narrowed scope for policy
directives has necessarily shifted the focus of the agency’s work and service to key critical outcomes for students across the P-20 education system (i.e.., absenteeism, STEM/CTE, graduation,
post-secondary transitional supports, and educator diversity). The resolve to focus agency efforts on systems-level policy has provided opportunity to build cross-agency and stakeholder
collaboration for broader education research and policy advancement, but also a unique opportunity for the CEdO to strategically impact key elements of the education system that will result in
greater student success and achievement from birth to career.
As a result of these key efforts, the CEdO’s customer base has become more clearly defined and will continue to broaden over time as the CEdO continues to maximize service and remain attuned
to the policy needs of the greater education system. These efforts will take time to materialize in a greater capacity, however, and are reliant on the continued support, clear direction, and strong
leadership for this agency as a whole. With the CEdO sunset pending in 2019 and/or changes to the education governance structure, it will be important that the state continues to meet the
challenge to ensure the most deep and pointed impacts are reaching our students, educators, parents/families via our agency’s strong partners and collaborators.
What Needs to be Done?
It is hoped that a renewed dedication to the charge of the CEdO at the Legislative level will continue so the current momentum toward better, more equitable outcomes for students across the state
may continue. Our renewed focus on key policy directives with a keen eye to advancement via strengthened cross-agency, cross-sector, and community partnerships will both better serve our
consumers and also expand the reach of our customer base. These efforts, if allowed to continue over time, will ultimately lead to increased stakeholder satisfaction via better attention to systems-
level needs and subsequently better service to our bottom-line: the students, educators, parents, and families who rely on a seamless system of education from birth to career.
