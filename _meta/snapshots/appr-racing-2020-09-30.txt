How Are We Doing
The Oregon Racing Commission continues to see a downward trend in race-related equine fatalities.
Oregon’s target is no more than 1.5 fatalities per 1,000 starters; the rate for FY 2020 was 1.02. That is below the national average of 1.53 horses per 1,000 starters.
Out of a total of 2,949 starters for FY 2020, at the commercial meet in Grants Pass and 2019 Tillamook and Burns fair meets, there were three race-related fatalities.
Totals are shown in percentages.
1.5 = 100% of goal
1.02 = 147.06% of goal
Factors Affecting Results
A variety of factors affect racing injuries, including: condition of the racehorse, training methods, racetrack surface, medications, jockey skill and racing luck. Reducing equine injuries is a continuing
focus of the Oregon Racing Commission.
In fiscal 2020, the ORC increased the number of pre-race examinations performed so that every horse scheduled to compete on a given day was examined by a Commission veterinarian

that morning, an industry best practice. Medication reforms have been enacted that bring the ORC into alignment with other jurisdictions in an effort to provide safe, therapeutic medication levels
consistent with best industry practices. The ORC has hired a track consultant to assist with fair meets in maintaining safe, consistent track surfaces.
With the opening of a new commercial race meet in Grants Pass in September 2019, the ORC has been an active partner in safety committee meetings and providing advice and expertise in safety
initiatives that included changes to the racing surface, installation of a safety rail, and improvements to lighting, the barns and other facilities.

How Are We Doing
The Oregon Racing Commission's Licensing Office has a target of issuing licenses within five days of receipt. The office generally issues licenses the same day as received, unless staff is out of town
or on vacation. The office meets its target of five days.
Factors Affecting Results
The ORC Licensing Office generally completes license applications the day they are received, depending on the racing schedule and with some exceptions for applicants that require extensive
background checks.
With the COVID-19 shutdown and staff working from home, Licensing staff  has been going into the office to collect mail and process applications once a week, so turnaround time has been slightly
longer, but generally remains within target.

How Are We Doing
actual target
The Oregon Racing Commission is exceeding targets in all categories. Our success continues to hinge on collaboration with stakeholders. We continually reach out to, and work with, the Oregon
Horsemen's Benevolent & Protective Association, the Oregon Quarter Horse Racing Association, the Oregon Thoroughbred Owners & Breeders Association, Grants Pass Downs and the Advance
Deposit Wagering (ADW) companies. We also actively engage with fair managers to provide safe, entertaining and exciting horse races and pari-mutuel wagering at the Eastern Oregon Livestock
Show in Union, the Crooked River Roundup in Prineville, and the Tillamook and Harney county fairs. Collaborative efforts extend to setting policy through our administrative rules process and cross-
training staff.
One respondent to the 2019 survey commented that the ORC website was difficult to use.  We built a new, better, and more user-friendly site in FY 2020.
Factors Affecting Results
The Oregon Racing Commission seeks responses to its online survey by sending an email to everyone on our list of stakeholders, and asking industry groups -- the Oregon Horsemen's Benevolent &
Protective Association, the Oregon Quarter Horse Racing Association, and the Oregon Thoroughbred Owners & Breeders Association -- to ask their members to fill out the survey, run through
SurveyMonkey. The survey is also available in hard copy near the licensing desk at the summer race meets and Grants Pass Downs, with a box available to submit forms. A hard-copy survey is
available in Spanish.
Survey responses, always modest, were further curtailed this year because of COVID-19 shutdowns resulting in cancellation of all the summer fair race meets and the spring meet in Grants Pass.
Nevertheless, those who did fill out surveys expressed satisfaction with the ORC.
