Defense of Criminal Convictions (DCC):
Although we again fell short of our target, our percentage reflects an increase from last year and has improved significantly from where it was during the

Factors Affecting Results
Defense of Criminal Convictions (DCC):
The primary factor affecting the speed with which we brief DCC appeals is our overall caseload and the extent to which we have other cases that need to be
addressed more quickly.  The extent to which the court will or will not allow extensions of time also affects how we prioritize individual cases relative to our work as a whole.  In some cases, factors
beyond our control affect our ability to brief a case more quickly -- for example, when we are awaiting a supplemental brief by the other side.

How Are We Doing
Child Advocacy:
While the division did not fully achieve the target of 100% of permanency hearings where the state agency's position was upheld or partially upheld, the division was only short by
one percent, which is negligible. Division has remained consistently close to or at target from earlier KPM reports.
Factors Affecting Results
Child Advocacy:
Child Advocacy and Protection Division (CAPD) attorneys and support staff prepare for and attorneys appear at juvenile dependency permanency hearings before judges in all 36
counties across the state.  Court requirements and expectations can change depending on the courtroom and experience of the court.  Additionally, while courts make rulings based on the evidence
presented, juvenile dependency cases (unlike some other case types), require the evidence to be current and can change up to or even during the court hearing without the state agency's or the
attorney's advance knowledge.  This unpredictability can be an impediment to the court upholding the state agency's (ODHS') position.
Management has continued to provide consistent and supportive trainings to attorneys and staff regarding statutory obligations and changes in case law relating to presenting evidence to support the
state agency's burden in permanency hearings.

How Are We Doing
Administrative Services:
The results for the state fiscal year ending June 30, 2024 were below the target level.
Factors Affecting Results
Administrative Services:
Some agencies are heavy consumers of DOJ's legal services. Agencies occasionally have questions about their invoices, take time in circulating their invoices for the
appropriate approvals, or even delay payment due to employee absence or vacancies. If even one of those agencies fails to timely pay a DOJ invoice, DOJ's performance on this KPM can slip below
the target mark. During the FY25 timeframe, there were several key positions that process the Legal Invoices that turned over in agencies that are large users of services.  The training period for the
new personnel continued throughout FY25 making processing the Legal Invoices slower for these agencies.
Note: The reporting cycle is the Oregon fiscal year. All attorneys and other legal services personnel routinely enter data into the automated system on billable hours worked. All billing and receivable
processing is done centrally through DOJ's Administrative Services Division. Policies are in place to ensure accuracy and appropriateness of billings resulting from the time capture system for legal
services personnel.

How Are We Doing
Child Support:
For the federal fiscal year ending September 30, 2024, the Oregon Child Support Program's performance on this measure is 87%. This makes two years with the same performance
level.
Factors Affecting Results
Child Support:
ODHS is no longer able to send non-custodial parent information in their data file to DOJ-DCS. This leads to more duplicate child support cases and artificially inflates the total number
of cases. The duplicate cases do not have orders, which negatively impacts this measure. Also, without information related to the non-custodial parent, it takes additional work for DCS to determine
the unknown parent, which delays establishment of child support orders. These factors negatively affect this performance measure. On a positive note, processes and procedures implemented to help
deal with this change from ODHS have allowed performance to stabilize.
Note: The measure is necessary for federal reporting requirements and must be reported based on the federal fiscal year.

Child Support:
For the federal fiscal year ending September 30, 2024, the Oregon Child Support Program’s performance is 63%. The unrounded figure is is less than half of a percentage point of
change lower compared to the prior year.
Factors Affecting Results
Child Support:
During “the Covid years” (especially 2020-2021), more people qualified for and received unemployment compensation. This led to child support programs regularly collecting from
some individuals who had previously not been paying consistently, raising the current collection rate during that period. The collection percentage has stabilized over the last couple of years between
63% and 64%. This amount remains higher than pre-Covid numbers and reflects that more appropriately sized child support orders (therefore more likely to be paid in full on time) and continuing
engagement with participants.
Note: The measure is necessary for federal reporting requirements and must be reported based on the federal fiscal year.
