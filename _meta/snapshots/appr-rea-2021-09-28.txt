How Are We Doing
For the 2021 reporting period 100% of property managers and principal brokers who completed the compliance review process came into compliance within 45 days of the review completion.
Factors Affecting Results
This key performance measure was introduced in reporting period 2014 and has performed well historically by consistently exceeding the target over the last seven years. However, since
implementation the compliance review has been optional. This results in extremely minimal engagement - despite incentivizing the effort with continuing education credit and marketing the program in
the Oregon Real Estate News Journal. Due to low participation, this program fails to reach the licensees that are most likely to benefit, and even though it has a successful KPM associated, it does not
provide the Agency with meaningful data to drive educational efforts.
This KPM has the potential to be more volatile than others in that if any number of licensees fail to comply, the overall compliance rate will suffer dramatically. The Agency often has two or fewer
participants in a month. If just a few in a period do not, or cannot, reach compliance in 45 days then compliance issues may appear outsized.  Recently, we have had several months where no eligible
licensees have opted to complete a compliance review.
Secondly, again due to having few participants, the existing data is not likely to be reflective of the entire pool of licensees. As participation is self-selected those that volunteer appear to be more likely
to be in compliance and unconcerned about having files examined. This is demonstrated when looking at mandatory clients’ trust account reconciliation reviews as a comparator. In the reconciliation
review program where review volume is substantially higher, the noncompliance rates are considerably higher and outcomes have clearly established patterns. This allows the Agency to observe
where failures are recurring, execute broader outreach and monitor performance through subsequent reviews and case related data.
actual target
In response to the missed opportunities inherent in the self-administered compliance review the Agency intends to modify this program in the current biennium. The Agency will introduce a mandatory
compliance review for principal brokers and property managers that will be designed and coordinated by a Clients’ Trust Account Coordinator that is expected to be hired by the Agency in October
2021. The coordinator will review the existing process to identify resource needs, goals and streamline the process. With a new automated process the Agency will be able to collect valuable
information related to noncompliance at a much higher volume. The Agency expects that data gathered in the next reporting period will more accurately reflect the question posed in this KPM as the
pool of participants will have increased significantly over the current average number of monthly participants.

How Are We Doing
For the 2021 reporting period 76% of cases were completed within 150 days of receipt of the complaint, falling short of the 80% benchmark. The Agency has exceeded the 80% standard in this
category since 2017, so reliably that it was raised from 60% in the 2019 Legislative Session. While the rate falls below the goal and marks a downturn in historical data, the Agency has undergone
some significant yet necessary changes under extraordinary circumstances, and this is a result to be proud of especially given that this represents an increase in case closure volume of 36% over the
prior year. The Agency has implemented meaningful change in the Regulation program igniting the current rebuilding period in this Division. Reorganization efforts are producing good results and sub-
data indicates a return to above 80% case completion rate within 150 days again in the next biennium.
Factors Affecting Results
In the most recent reporting period the Agency has been building off a significant number of changes starting in 2020. The Division had a vacancy of a long term Regulations Division manager that
was filled in July of this year after remaining open for an extended period of time. The new manager is reviewing long held business practices for new opportunities to conduct investigative work more
efficiently without diminishing quality. Also, due to the shift of working from home fulltime, the Agency enacted significant changes in business practices, including converting paper files to an electronic
format to maintain effectiveness and security in a distanced environment. Investigators have since adjusted to new tools for effectively conducting interviews and file inspection, like Zoom and Teams
rather than in person field investigations at the site of business and the Agency is observing an increased in case processing times.
However, the most significant contributor to the change in this KPM is the discovery and resolution of a considerable amount of old outstanding open cases that were closed in the current and previous
reporting period. This situation has led to substantial changes to roles and responsibilities in this Division as well as an appreciable but temporary increase to the overall case load handled by
regulatory staff.
actual target
Early in his tenure, Commissioner Strode ordered an internal review of the business operations of the Regulation Division. The purpose of this audit was to identify the depth and cause of informally
observed case delays. Though more specifically, Executive Management detected a distinct need to optimize business processes, coupled with more rigorous and transparent reporting and
accountability. The findings of this review, identified a production administration and case backlog of 100 cases that were greater than two years old without recent activity.
Upon discovery of these issues reorganization efforts commenced immediately.
From March 2020 through January 2021 the Agency dedicated resources to clear the backlog, while simultaneously implementing process improvements for ongoing case management. Since closing
out the remaining old cases, days in administrative review have reduced by significantly, clearing most cases for reviewing within two business weeks. T
The Agency chose to undertake the large case backlog in a large single grouping, knowing that its performance rating in this measure would be temporarily diminished in 2020 and 2021. Though
resolving this issue and preventing it from reoccurring in the future is of high importance. The Agency has been fully transparent with its industry stakeholders - from reporting the findings in the OREN-
J, and providing ongoing updates to the Real Estate Board. Overall, Executive Leadership is heavily engaged in performance and establishing a culture of empowerment and shared accountability
while setting clear expectations for management and staff.
Having instituted new practices and resolving the case backlog the Agency has now returned to exceeding performance in this area.

How Are We Doing
Arriving at 95%, FY 21 is the second year in a row that the Agency has met or exceeded KPM #3 since 2014.
Factors Affecting Results
The Agency has taken a new approach in the settlement process, which has resulted in two years of improved numbers in this KPM. Historically the Agency has taken the position of withholding the
specifics of the settlement offer and using the meeting as an opportunity to present and further negotiate details. However new leadership has consciously modified this approach due to concerns over
unnecessarily delays and opaque communication. The Agency now presents a final written settlement offer as the first settlement offer. The settlement process continues to allow for respondents to
produce new information to allow for a changes in the settlement when appropriate. Respondents now have a clearer understanding of the Agency’s intention and the process is less subject to
miscommunication. However, a respondent may always decline a settlement agreement and prefer to take the case to hearing.

How Are We Doing
The current KPM falls short of the target by 1%, coming in at 74%.
Factors Affecting Results
In the 2017 legislative session the Agency requested the approval of the legislature to change the engagement point with which this KPM was evaluated by the new licensee. Prior to the approved
change, the Agency would contact the licensee six months following licensure. However the comments related to the KPM were heavily focused on the failure of the examination to prepare a new
licensee for success in real estate sales. By inquiring with the new licensee one month following licensure comments have become more relevant to pre-license education and the effectiveness of the
examination.
The Agency observes that many of the comments associated with lower ratings suggest confusion with the question itself. In real estate, most licensees view competency to follow some sort of formal
training program at their brokerage. Pre-licensing exams are not intended to teach sales and marketing. Moreover, “ethics” is often associated with “Code of Ethics” of the Realtor Association,
whereas Oregon statute only refers to “duties and responsibilities” of a licensee.
Within this reporting period, the Agency hosted two work groups comprised of staff and industry leaders from the licensee community, to update the examination content. The updated examinations
were put into place in October 2020. While the overall target was not met and was an exact mirror of the results submitted in 2020, the Agency is confident that the changes put in place will show
promise in coming years, as data from the fourth quarter is already showing an improvement (75%). The number of individuals choosing to take part in this KPM has also gone up significantly year to
year.
actual target
Beginning in October, the Agency's exam vendor PSI began conducting online proctoring for real estate pre-license examinations as a measure to increase safety and reduce spread of COVID-19 as
well as provide greater equity for applicants that have lesser availability to travel to an examination site. The Agency has received generally positive feedback about this new change.
The Agency will continue to closely monitor the comments associated with this KPM following these changes and will be offering clarifying language to the question so that respondents understand the
role of occupational licensure exams as a measure of competent knowledge of statute and rules.

How Are We Doing
actual target
The Agency continues to exceed targets in all areas of customer service.
Factors Affecting Results
The Agency strives to provide outstanding customer service to licensees and the public.
The downtown office location remains closed to the public due to the COVID-19 pandemic. Regardless of this,
Agency staff are available by telephone and email Monday through Friday 8:00am to 5:00pm, and have been exceeding customer service expectations. All applications are available through the
Agency's eLicense system and conducted online. License applications are processed through automated workflows, based on business rules, in real time as requirements are met and do not require
staff intervention outside of the background review. Business applications are completed within three business days. In addition, the Agency is currently working to make it possible for individuals filing
complaints to submit them through eLicense, which will further streamline Agency processes.
The Agency handles approximately 1,800 phone calls per month with an average hold time of 28 seconds.  Multiple licensing staff is available by phone at any given time in order to reduce hold time.
Agency email is responded to within a 24 hour period. The Agency responds to roughly 3,000 emails on average per month. Comments from the customer service survey are monitored on a monthly
basis and are overwhelmingly positive, heaping praise on the well-deserving front line customer service staff. A very small snapshot representative of typical comments on customer service include:

The Agency’s eLicense system is considered a foundational element of the Agency’s overall customer service. This system is beginning to age beyond user expectations in functionality and the
Agency has observed an increase in negative comments regarding its user-interface and features. The Agency intends to request funding for a replacement in the current biennium. The goal of a
replacement is to be more user friendly, streamline existing processes, and give the system a more modern look as the Agency is quickly approaching a decade of usage for the current eLicense
system. Significant changes such as these will lead to a more satisfying and intuitive experience on the user’s end which can only help us maintain or grow our existing numbers in this specific KPM.
