How Are We Doing
With six (6) months of complete data for 2017, TSPC is resolving 31.4% of our Professional Practices cases within 180 days.
TSPC has been making progress in key areas:
The average length of a TSPC investigation in 2017 is currently 8.8 months.
In 2016, the average was reported at 12 months
The 2016 percent of cases completed in 180 days was reported at 22%
To date in 2017, TSPC has improved that number by nearly ten percentage points.
NOTE ON DATA SET: The data for KPM #3 was collected from January 2017 to June 2017. As June was the latest Commission meeting in 2017, and there will not be another until November, all
data collected after the June 2017 Commission meeting is considered incomplete until the conclusion of the November 2017 meeting.
Factors Affecting Results
TSPC currently has three (3) full time investigators and one (1) limited duration investigator working the investigations case load. Having four total investigators has proven beneficial in making
progress toward the agency goal. The backlog of TSPC investigations, which experienced a high mark of 299 cases in June of 2015, has been reduced to around 200 cases in June of 2017. TSPC
was down to 188 cases in January of 2017, which was the lowest total number of pending investigations since 2012.
Beginning in January 2018, TSPC will begin to leverage the provisions provided by HB3351. The new law provides TSPC with the authority to require patrons who submit a complaint to TSPC to
verify that they have made an attempt to resolve the complaint through the employing school district’s complaint process prior to TSPC beginning a full investigation. It is anticipated that the new
flexibility provided by the law will cut down on the number of patron complaints received by TSPC which should be resolved at the local level.
The 2018 performance measure goal of 75% of cases resolved in 180 days is very ambitious, and achieving that mark will be a challenge. There are still numerous variables within the investigation
of any given case which are beyond agency control and can push an investigation beyond the 180 day mark. TSPC will continue to work within the agency’s capacity to reduce the total number of
cases pending investigation, and resolve those cases in the most timely manner possible.

How Are We Doing
TSPC has made tremendous progress in this area in the past three years, due mainly to a decrease in staff turnover and two Limited Duration Public Service Representative (PSR) positions. The
abandon rate for January 2017 through September 2017 was around 10 percent, exceeding the KPM target of 15 percent.  The hold time has also decreased to an average of 3:03 minutes. As a
comparison, the 3
Quarter of 2016 (July, August and September) had average hold times of over 10 minutes and an abandon rate of 33.1%. During that time period, the agency was implementing
the new eLicensing application (online application for new and renewing licenses) and had experienced staff turnover in the PSR team. Throughout the 2017 reporting period, we have remained
fully staffed, contributing to the success in this area.
Factors Affecting Results
The TSPC Public Service Representative Team has been fully staffed during this reporting period, and one of those employees has been granted lead-worker duties. The lead-worker is able to
monitor the call center queue, and assign additional resources as necessary during peak call times. This team of five has worked together to schedule time off to maintain coverage, and they are
constantly attentive to the call center queue. Because of that attentiveness, the average hold time is 3:03 minutes, down from highs in 2016 of 13:33 minutes (August 2016) and 8:06 minutes
(September 2016). Two of these positions are Limited Duration through the end of the 2017/2019 biennium. Loss of those two positions would likely drive the abandon rate and hold times in a
negative direction.

How Are We Doing
In the numbers provided for this KPM, it is evident that the agency is improving. Staff turnover in 2015 and 2016 contributed to fewer skilled employees available to manage customer contact, but
turnover has dropped significantly during 2017. New rules were introduced beginning in July of 2015. Implementation of new licensure rules affects a customer’s perception of the accuracy of
information as an answer to a particular may have changed with the new rules. Throughout 2016 and 2017, the agency has worked to improve the application submission process, including a new
online application and fee portal and redesign of internal processing procedures.
Factors Affecting Results
For many years, the agency operated under two distinct sets of licensure rules, with different requirements for people who were similarly licensed and performing the same work. This led to a great
deal of confusion for staff when giving advice, and as educators discussed licensure matters amongst themselves and compared requirements, the confusion often grew. In 2016, the Commission
adopted an entirely new set of rules, to bring everyone under the same system of licensure. The new rules and requirements are being phased in as licensees renew, with the complete transition to
be completed by 2021. As these systems merge, we expect customer satisfaction to greatly improve.
