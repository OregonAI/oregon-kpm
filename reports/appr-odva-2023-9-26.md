How Are We Doing
The Oregon Department of Veterans’ Affairs (ODVA) strives to provide the highest quality of claims representation to Oregon’s veterans. ODVA is
responsible for accrediting and training County, Tribal, and State Veterans Service Officers to ensure veterans and families are served by skilled,
certified professionals. These Veteran Service Officers are assisted by a strong ODVA Appeals team which represent veterans and families in
USDVA Board Hearings, and Virtual Hearings. This performance measure compares the average annual Oregon disability compensation and
pension benefit payment made to an individual veteran (actual) to the national average (target). The initial Federal Fiscal Year 2022 target of
$19,533 was established based upon historical average rate of growth. The target for 2023 has been adjusted to $20,969 to reflect the projected
https://kpm.dasapp.oregon.gov/Reporting/View/4326?NoMenu=False 11/19
national average based upon an annual growth rate of 4.81%. Oregon’s average disability and pension compensation amount is approximately
9.2% less than the national average.
Factors Affecting Results
The factors influencing the dollar amount of a disability compensation and pension payment include the quality of representation (reflected in the
knowledge and expertise of the Veterans Service Officer and board hearings officer) and the type of claim (disability compensation or pension)
presented to the USDVA. Other factors include the age and overall health of veterans. For instance, older veterans may receive a pension benefit,
which is designed to support them by augmenting fixed incomes burdened by exceptionally high out of pocket medical costs such as assisted
living facilities, or to prevent homelessness for those veterans with an extremely low income.
Note: FY23 actual data is provided by the USDVA and is not available until mid-year 2024. FY23 target will be adjusted at that time.

Partnerships between the Oregon Department of Veterans’ Affairs (ODVA), Tribes, and Counties providing services to veterans and families have
never been stronger. Coordinated efforts to reach and engage with veterans from all walks of life and historically underserved populations continue
to grow. The number of new Powers of Attorney (POA)/Appointments of Representation provided by veterans to ODVA, Tribes, and Counties is
generally a reflection of new claims being filed on behalf of veterans and their family members. For fiscal year 2023, there was an increase in the
total number of new POAs from the prior year and has been rising since the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic seen in 20271.
https://kpm.dasapp.oregon.gov/Reporting/View/4326?7NoMenu=False 13/19
The impact of the COVID-19 pandemic cannot be overstated for this KPM. The traditional methods of reaching underserved, houseless, or under
resourced veterans and families through outreach and events known as “Standdowns’” were largely cancelled or postponed due to social distancing
requirements. Thankfully these have now largely returned to in-person at the same rate and frequency as seen pre-pandemic.
Agency efforts to tackle challenges facing historically minoritized or have come from the hiring of specialized coordinators such as the Houseless
Veteran Coordinator, Women Veteran Coordinator, LGBTQ+ Veteran Coordinator, and Tribal Veteran Coordinator. Additionally, ODVA has now entered
into MOUs with three of the nine federally recognized tribes in Oregon to provide training and accredited claim representation from Tribal Veteran
service Officers.
Factors Affecting Results
The primary factor affecting recent results was a 2015 directive from the USDVA mandating that all historical POA’s older than five years old be
renewed. These POA renewals resulted in substantial increases to the total number of POAs filed between 2015-2016 as established clients had to
be refreshed. Starting in 2017 and continuing into 2020, the number of new POA filings began to average to totals typically seen prior to 2015. Due
to the leveling off of POA’s, after the required refresh from USDVA, the Oregon state legislature adopted new targets for this KPM beginning in
FY22. In
FY23 the new target goal was 7,650.
Despite a decrease in the overall population of veterans as Oregon’s veteran population age, outreach efforts to underserved veteran’s population has
improved. For example, over 1,300 new claims submitted in FY23 for houseless, elderly, and extremely low income veterans as well as surviving
spouses. Additionally, there were over 4,400 new appeals and supplemental claims filed to assist veterans and claimants in ensuring that they are
receiving the maximum benefit they are entitled, at the earliest possible effective date.
Note: Beginning in March 2020, County and Tribal Veteran Service Offices, and ODVA experienced an immediate decrease in monthly POA’s obtained.
The noticeable decline in new POA’s has been attributed to the reduced ability for in-person meetings and more limited outreach activities due to the
COVID-19 Pandemic. This decrease continued through the completion of FY21 but has since shown signs of rebounding in FY22 and FY23.

How Are We Doing
80% of Veterans households who were contracted after receiving OHCS homelss assistance funds had retained their permanent housing. This is in-
line with the traget of 80%
Factors Affecting Results
It is important to note that the denominator used in this calculation is the number of people successfully contacted after six months, not everyone
who was due for a follow-up. The KPM therefore reflects the percentage of those contacted who are still in permanent housing after 6 months and
does not include those who were not contacted.
https://kpm.dasapp.oregon.gov/Reporting/View/4326?NoMenu=False 18/19
There are several possible reasons some clients were not contacted after exiting homeless assistance programs. Due to the way the past KPM report is
written in the data system, not everyone due for a follow-up was being entered into the system. Therefore, it is probable that some people due for
follow-up were not contacted. In response, OHCS staff are working to update the report across a wider range of homeless services programs to
automatically prompt service providers to conduct the correct number of follow-ups. This has resulted in improved information and data accuracy in
several areas of reporting and the goal is to expand it to additional programs. OHCS recognizes that improving data quality and reports is an iterative
process and is continuing to work on addressing these issues over time.
As with all programs this last year, COVID-19 impacted program delivery. While traditional Homeless Services Programs at OHCS have continued,
there have been multiple new COVID related emergency rental assistance programs. These programs have been larger in scale than other previous
rental assistance programs. Due to the emergency nature of these programs a more limited set of data is collected—and in particular the six month
follow up is not required. Therefore, these programs do not factor into the KPM, but it is important to note the history investments in rental assistance
over the last year.
Obstacles to continue meeting the 80 percent goal include: difficult economic circumstances, high unemployment rates, a shortage of affordable
housing units, low rental vacancy rates, impacts from COVID -19, and poor data quality and collection over the past several years. Many of these
challenges are systemic and OHCS and partners will continue to increase the rates of housed Veterans.
Lastly, the KPM is only a limited snapshot of the services provided to Veterans and their families. OHCS housing and shelter programs provided
services such as emergency shelter, rapid rehousing, case management and more to Veterans throughout the state. In addition, the OHCS Veteran
Leadership Team helped convene our State counterparts, Federal partners, and other service providers to discuss best practices and build collective
knowledge through peer-to-peer sharing across regions. Through increased collaboration and improved data, we are hopeful in continuing to make
progress towards placing more Veterans in permanent housing.
