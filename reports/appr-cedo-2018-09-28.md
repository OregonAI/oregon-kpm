Our Strategy
HB 4002 (2015) directed the CEdO and ODE, in collaboration with DHS, OHA, ELD, and other key education stakeholders, to develop Oregon’s Chronic Absenteeism Statewide Plan
(
http://www.ode.state.or.us/superintendent/priorities/2016---december---chronic-absenteeism-report-(hb-4002).pdf
). The plan, released in December 2016, incorporated findings from the Chief
Education Office’s Chronic Absenteeism Report (2016;
http://education.oregon.gov/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/CEdO_ChronicAbsenteeismReport__May_2016-WEB_rev2.pdf
), exploring
perceptions and attitudes of students most likely to be chronically absent (i.e., students of color, students in poverty, students receiving special education services, students from an Oregon Tribe,
and students who have received at least one out of school suspension). To further help reduce chronic absenteeism rates in Oregon, HB 4002 also directed the CEdO to develop and implement a
three-year pilot study focused on a trauma-informed approach in schools (
http://education.oregon.gov/trauma-informed-pilot/
) . This pilot is currently moving to its third year.
About the Targets
The chronic absenteeism metric is complementary to an average attendance rate. The average rate includes the days attended divided by total days for all students; chronic absenteeism is the
percentage of students who miss 10% or more of school days. Schools and districts can have relatively high attendance rates but also have high chronic absenteeism rates, as the first metric
considers the entire school population while the second accounts for each individual student.
How Are We Doing?
actual target
th
During the 2015-16 school year, 87% of 6
grade students were not chronically absent. Students were considered "not chronically absent" if they had attended “90% or more” of their enrolled days
between the beginning of the 2015-16 school year and May 2, 2016. This meant that 14% of 6
grade students missed 10% or more of their enrolled school days. In 2017, however, ODE replaced
the “Not Chronically Absent” data category with the “Regular Attenders” category, defined as students who attended “more than 90%” (i.e., slight difference in definitions) of their enrolled days.
Because attendance is a key component to high school graduation, this number must improve.

results.

How Are We Doing
Our Strategy
The CEdO reports annually to the Legislature on the state’s progress in this area, coordinating with data drawn from ODE, TSPC, and HECC (see current and past years’ reports at:
http://education.oregon.gov
). The report also draws from insights, analyses, and feedback from close to 20 various stakeholder events hosted and vetted by a 20-member Educator Equity Advisory
Group that helps assess, evaluate, and advocate for educational policy supporting the diversification of Oregon’s educator workforce.
Whereas some states only track teachers of color, Oregon has set a goal for increasing the number and percentage of culturally or linguistically diverse teachers employed in Oregon public schools
in order to meet local districts’ desire to hire bilingual educators as well as educators of color. The 2018 report shows slow but steady increases in the number and percentage of culturally or
linguistically diverse teachers and administrators employed in Oregon’s public schools. Likewise, a positive trend can be seen in the racial diversity found in enrollment and completion data for
candidates completing teacher preparation programs. However, a sizable gap still remains between the diversity of students in Oregon’s K-12 schools (39.4%) and the diversity of our classroom
teachers (10.7%) and school/district administrators (11.8%). This is particularly notable in 30 Oregon school districts that serve a K-12 student population whose cultural or linguistic diversity ranges
between 40 to 84%.
About the Targets
The KPM target is significantly higher than the percentage of Oregon educators who are culturally and linguistically diverse in 2018. The implementation of the Educator Equity Advisory Group’s
actual target
Action Plan (2018) and the Educator Advancement Council’s (EAC) development of networks via SB 182 will provide the EAC resources that the networks can use for preparation, recruitment,
hiring, retention, and professional development planning and coordination; thus, impacting the development of a culturally responsive, well-prepared, and diverse educator population in Oregon.
Continued and coordinated Legislative attention and efforts led by the CEdO in this area will also allow more rapid progress to occur. It is anticipated that several early career pathway partnerships
now underway involving districts and educator preparation programs will contribute to positive KPM improvements realized within the next two to three years.
The Educator Equity Advisory Group has identified barriers and challenges that impact the diversity of Oregon’s educator workforce in the domains of early on-ramps, course transfer and career
pathways, financial supports, hiring practices, and career advancement. With the passage of SB 182 in the 2017 session, the EAC in partnership with the Higher Education Coordinating
Commission’s Office of Student Access and Completion, has now awarded the first round of scholarships to reduce debt for teacher candidates who mirror the state’s demographics of culturally and
linguistically diverse students. These scholarships are being managed by CEdO/EAC staff and the HECC Office of Student Access and Completion with a program coordinator who helps recruit,
network, support, and track the Oregon Teacher Scholar candidates enrolled in educator preparation programs by providing financial support during their coursework and professional learning
experiences combined with networking with other educators of color and potential employers.
In 2017-18, the Educator Equity Advisory Group convened members of each of the other statewide equity committees to identify common interests and opportunities related to Oregon’s Educator
Workforce for further collaboration and to make progress on this KPM. The CEdO also convened a first-ever Socratic Seminar discussion engaging district recruiters, community college leaders,
and educator preparation deans and directors in order to identify stakeholders’ concerns, strategies, needs, and recommended actions and policy changes tied to the needs of the educator
workforce in Oregon. The group has also engaged in several statewide discussions regarding the transfer of community college coursework to reduce the loss of credits and provide smoother
pathways for teacher candidates, many of whom enroll first at a community college. Since starting in spring 2018, the Oregon Educator Equity Advisory Group (OEEAG) has served as a key
resource for facilitating and providing data/recommendations for the Confederation of School Administrators legislative priority Work Group 3 focused on Educator Workforce Development. And with
the addition of related goals for the Joint Committee on Student Success Work Group 3, representatives from both the OEEAG and EAC have shared findings and recommendations with state
legislators.
How Are We Doing?
Data from 2017 show that Oregon has hired more culturally or linguistically diverse teachers, bringing the State’s total percentage to 10.7% (N = 3,329). Each year the Oregon Department of
Education revalidates and updates educator equity data for current
and
past years. Due to this revalidation process, previous KPM reports are now inaccurate. The correct percentages show a
year-to-year increase over the last three years (2015-16: 10.2%, 2016-17: 10.1%, and 2017-18: 10.7%).
Factors Affecting Results
Independent decisions made by preparation programs, school districts, and pre-service and practicing teachers are ultimately beyond the control of the CEdO. Heretofore, there had been
insufficient funding allocated to the universal application of promising practices such as educator pathways and scholarships. While the passage of SB 182 is coordinating efforts across key
education stakeholders to help boost preparation, recruitment, hiring, retention, and professional development planning and coordination, it will still take time to observe significant increases in the
diversity of the educator population throughout Oregon.
What Needs to be Done?
Oregon’s Educator Equity Advisory Group’s Action Plan (2018) provides guidance on how to build advocacy around the visioning and valuing of diversity “by creating pathways to increase cultural
and linguistic diversity in its educator workforce and by assisting all educators in becoming more culturally responsive.” New and strengthening activities within CEdO and collaborative partners via
SB 182 will continue to focus efforts on building a stronger and more diverse educator workforce statewide.
About the Data
The data is collected for both licensed and employed educators in all public K-12 schools and is from the July 2018 Oregon Educator Equity Report. For the purposes of this KPM, educators are
defined as teachers; the above numbers do not consider administrators.

and
past years. Due to this revalidation process, it would be helpful to have access to
revise previous KPM/APPR fields to reflect accurate and updated data.

How Are We Doing
Our Strategy
A critical element of CEdO’s mission is the research and policy service that drives strategic planning, legislation, investment, and subsequent practice across a P-20 seamless system of education.
The work of the agency’s research and policy staff has shifted from serving the needs of a board and sub-committees to supporting cross-agency initiatives. This is largely achieved through
community, stakeholder, and cross-sector engagement and collaboration focused on an evidence-based, equitable, outcome focused approach to education policy and practice. It also includes the
organization, coordination, and convening of committees, workgroups, councils, hubs, and teams (e.g., Education Agency Research Team [EART]) to collaborate on research and translate findings
into promising policy and practice aimed at improving P-20 student outcomes throughout Oregon.
How Are We Doing?
The customer rating for CEdO’s research and policy service is 81%, which is above the target (70%) and an increase over the last two years (75% in 2017 and 63% in 2016). The survey response
rate (16%) has, however, decreased substantially from the previous two years (49% in 2017 and 22% in 2016). That will need to improve next year.
Changes in CEdO’s staffing and the reorganization of priorities stemming from the 2017 Legislative Session are altering its research and policy capacity, activities, and outreach. The resultant shift
in research and policy focus (i.e., narrowed scope for policy directives with continued cross-agency and key stakeholder collaboration for broader research and policy advancement), however, will
maximize services to key customers while also being attuned to the needs of the greater education system and reaching a broader customer base. Similarly, adopting a new, project-based
actual target
approach to surveying CEdO’s customer base will provide more accurate, timely, and helpful feedback with respect to satisfaction with research and policy service and opportunities for improvement
over time.
Factors Affecting Results
With a shift in research focus over time, it has proved a challenge in previous years to define the customer base in any meaningful way. The CEdO shifted their customer service survey to a project-
based approach for 2017, meant to more accurately capture targeted feedback from those directly involved, connected to, or impacted by the day-to-day and specific research and policy work of
the agency. Surveys were sent to key stakeholders (i.e., partners, collaborators, and consumers, both cross-agency and community-based), and question frames were customized to more directly
address or prompt project-specific aspects to more clearly target overall satisfaction and performance measures.
What Needs to be Done?
Project-based surveys will continue be sent to key customers at intervals that ensure accurate and timely capturing of satisfaction data across all CEdO initiatives. With continued development,
implementation, and bolstering of cross-agency and community-based partnerships, the overall capacity for education research at large will increase and therefore enable the CEdO to provide
better research and policy service to an even broader customer base.

Our Strategy
Changes to the charge and overall restructuring of the CEdO over time has necessitated the refinement and redefinition of our customer base. We have a very small staff and a large set of
customers, as shaped through our mission and continued focus on cross-agency and community engagement, outreach, and partnerships/collaboration in building and coordinating a seamless P-
20 education system. Our strategy is to continue to identify the customers and entities that are key points of leverage in achieving agency and state goals and to focus or target our service to
identified needs and opportunities. Expanding cross-agency and community-based groups convened by CEdO to develop and align legislative concepts and their associated budget components is
a moving target that will require continued attention to the engagement, outreach, and feedback from our customer base.
About the Targets
These targets are similar to those of other agencies and represent where the CEdO needs to be in the next two years.
How Are We Doing?
The customer ratings in 4 out of the 6 domains of the satisfaction survey are above targets. Also, in nearly all of the domains, customer ratings of “great” or “excellent” have improved over the
previous two years: (1) Helpfulness is 94% in 2018, versus 80% in 2017 and 76% in 2016; (2) Availability of Information is 77% in 2018, versus 73% in 2017 and 56% in 2016; (3) Expertise is 84% in
2018, versus 83% in 2017 and 70% in 2016; (4) Accuracy is 87% in 2018, versus 82% in 2017 and 66% in 2016; (5) Overall service is 87% in 2018, versus 81% in 2017 and 66% in 2016.
Staffing changes and reorganization of priorities from the 2017 Legislative Session will subsequently alter CEdO’s research and policy capacity, activities, and outreach. However, the resultant shift
in focus will also maximize agency services to key customers while simultaneously being attuned to the needs of the greater education system (and therefore reaching a broader customer base).
Similarly, adopting a new, project-based approach to surveying CEdO’s customer base will provide more accurate, timely, and helpful feedback with respect to satisfaction with agency service and
opportunities for improvement.
Factors Affecting Results
The restructuring of the CEdO over time has prompted significant efforts toward more clearly defining the agency’s mission and work. The resultant narrowed scope for policy directives has
necessarily shifted the focus of the agency’s work and service to key critical outcomes for students across the P-20 education system, such as chronic absenteeism, STEM/CTE, graduation, post-
secondary transitional supports, and educator diversity. The resolve to focus the CEdO’s efforts on systems-level policy has also provided opportunities to build cross-agency and stakeholder
collaboration for broader education research and policy advancement, and for the agency to strategically impact key elements of the education system that will result in greater student success and
achievement from birth to career.
As a result of these key efforts, the CEdO’s customer base has become more clearly defined and will continue to grow over time as the agency continues to maximize service and remain attuned to
the policy needs of the greater education system. These efforts will take time to generate greater capacity, however, and are reliant on the continued support, clear direction, and strong leadership
for this agency as a whole. With the CEdO sunset pending in June 2019 or changes to the education governance structure, it will be important for the state to continue meeting these challenge to
ensure the most beneficial impacts are reaching our students, educators, and families via our partners and collaborators.
What Needs to be Done?
It is hoped that a renewed dedication to the work of the CEdO at the Legislative level will continue so the current momentum toward better, more equitable student outcomes across Oregon
continues. Our renewed focus on key policy directives with a keen eye toward advancement via strengthened cross-agency, cross-sector, and community partnerships will both better serve our
consumers and also expand the reach of our customer base. If allowed to continue, these efforts will lead to sustained stakeholder satisfaction through improved attention to systems-level needs
and better service to the bottom-line: the students, educators, and families who rely on a seamless system of education from birth to career.
