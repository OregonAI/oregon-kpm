How Are We Doing
We have met our goal of investigating cases and referring to the board within 120 days.   The average number of days it takes before a case is referred to the board depends on when the complaint is
received in relation to the date of the board meeting.  Board meetings are held every three months.
Factors Affecting Results
The board averages about 20 cases per year.  Some cases require a formal investigation by the board contract investigator and some do not. Cases with a formal investigation occur when we receive
a complaint that falls within our jurisdiction.  We average about 5-6 investigations per year.  Investigations can take longer before they are referred the board, usually 60-120 days  The length of an
investigation is dependent on many factors.  Complexity, number of allegations, availability of the respondent, complainant and witnesses, response time to a subpoena, number of medical records to
review, etc.
Examples of cases that do not involve a complaint and formal investigation: when a past arrest is not reported on an application but shows up on a background check, an applicant with a recent arrest,
an applicant that has been sanctioned in another state, or an OT practitioner who practiced unlicensed.  These cases are generally ready for board review within a couple of weeks.
