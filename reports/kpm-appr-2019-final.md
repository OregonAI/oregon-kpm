How Are We Doing
For the 2019 tax season, the department processed nearly two million tax returns; about 65 percent (1.3 million) were refund claims. Thismeasurement is the average amount of time it takes from when the agency received a return until a refund was issued. The agency averaged 16days to refund in 2019, eight days longer than the target. In response to a desire to balance timeliness with fraud reduction, the OregonLegislature modified the target to 16 days beginning in 2020.
The primary factor that impacts return processing is whether or not a return is suspended. Suspended returns are diverted from automaticprocessing for manual review because errors exist or additional information is needed to process the return; either may indicate a potential forfraud. Factors Affecting Results
Aside from suspended returns, other factors within the agency’s control that impact return processing are:
Business edits/rules on processing returns—the agency has over 200 rules that: incorporate statutory limits, correct math errors, matchagainst third-party information, and allows the agency to request additional documentation. Some rules are system processed (matherrors), while others require a manual review. Annually, business rules are improved to close known gaps and stop fewer legitimate returns.The fraud module in the agency's tax processing system uses analytics to identify potentially fraudulent refunds. The attributes forfraudulent returns have become so similar to legitimate returns that it’s difficult to differentiate between the two. Each year, the agencyreviews fraud triggers to address known gaps and to make improvements. There will always be a need for manual review to combat fraud,but we are working to better identify those that the system can sort through.Early season refund hold—the agency does not issue refunds prior to February 15. This allows the agency time to load informationsubmitted by employers (due January 31) and match it to the information reported by the taxpayer on their tax return.The number of seasonal staff hired to open mail, key paper returns, process correspondence, etc.

How Are We Doing
The agency exceeded its performance goals for this measure in years 2015 to 2017, reflective of the intensive training required of nearly all staffas a part of the Core Systems Replacement (CSR) Project. The agency missed the increased target set by the legislature in 2018, in part due tothe limited scope of the final implementation stage of the CSR Project. The figures for 2019 reflect the first year there was no required trainingrelated to the CSR Project as it’s now complete. Given this, the agency expected to miss the target.
This measure allows the agency to prioritize supporting employees to keep pace with changing technology, policies, and processes. It also honorsbuilding a respectful and professional workplace, increases understanding for equity, inclusion, and diversity, and ensures adequate knowledgetransfer and succession planning required for seamless function of mission critical operations. Training has also been identified as a driver foremployee engagement in the last several engagement surveys. These factors make it an important internal measure; however, it is not an optimalmeasure of agency performance. Given this, the agency will recommend deletion of this measure in the future with the understanding that this willremain an internal measure maintained by the Human Resources Section and available upon request.
Factors Affecting Results
In the previous four years, the majority of agency staff were required to complete training on our new core system. This training requirement wascritical to successfully implementing the system, so, funding for training was included in the project budget. Now that this project is complete, theagency's performance dropped to 37 percent.
In an effort to ensure employee access to appropriate professional training, the agency purchased licenses for unlimited training on a number oftopics. This maximized available training dollars. The agency also worked with local training providers to offer popular trainings at a discountedprice. In addition, the agency focused on capturing training records in iLearn and encouraged employees and managers to create professionalgrowth goals involving training to enhance knowledge, skills, and/or abilities.

Effective Taxpayer Assistance

How Are We Doing
Our overall score of 81 is an increase over the 2018 score of 79. While performance increased from the previous year, it fell short of the target of90 set in the 2017 budget process.
This measure aligns with the agency’s strategic priority to enhance taxpayer assistance. The goal is to provide self-help access for those whowant it and provide effective one-on-one assistance where necessary (or desired). In practice, the agency believes this measure is too complexand weighted too heavily on timeliness rather than actually measuring the taxpayer’s ability to access the support they want or need. The agencyis exploring alternatives with the hope of proposing an improved measure of taxpayer assistance.
Factors Affecting Results
The results of this measure are impacted by the performance of three components that form this composite measure. Each component isweighted:
Call wait time. Calls with a wait time shorter than five-minutes made up 77 percent of total calls, up from 71 percent in 2018. Staffingincreases were the primary driver of this improvement (weight: .4).“Where’s My Refund” inquiries. The percentage of “successful” inquiries made through phone or web applications was 85 percent, whichreflects a 1 percent increase from 2018. Successful inquiries are defined as any response other than "not found,” which means the return isnot in the system when the taxpayer asks. Typically, it’s because taxpayers didn't wait the suggested two weeks from when they file beforerequesting a status update (weight: .5).Customer service rating. The percentage of customers rating our service as "good" or "excellent" was 91 percent, up from 81 percent in2018 (weight: .1).
While these three components are all important indicators of customer service performance, aggregating the results into a compositemeasure does not allow for flexibility or provide true insight related to taxpayer assistance expectations. Additionally, it is heavily skewed towardspersonal income tax (Where’s My Refund) rather than being agency focused.

KPM #7Appraisal Value Uniformity - We will demonstrate our ability to deliver high quality business results by measuring appraisal equity anduniformity for DOR industrial accounts.
Data Collection Period: Jul 01 - Jun 30
* Upward Trend = negative result
Report Year 2015 2016 2017 2018 2019
Appraisal Value Uniformity
Actual 28% 29% 18% 20% 15%
Target 20% 20% 20% 30% 32%
How Are We Doing
The reported metric is the measure of variance between the values from our site-specific appraisals and the values from our annual mass-appraisal program of processed taxpayer-filed industrial property returns. Performance for 2019 was 15 percent, well below the adjusted target of32 percent.
Measuring the coefficient of dispersion (COD) is an appraisal industry standard metric for uniformity. The metric is reliant on market saletransactions. In the last five years, the agency hasn’t had a large enough sampling of usable sale transactions, so, the agency used site-specificappraisals to calculate results. The agency would ultimately like to eliminate this measure due to the small number of transactions. Substitutingsite-specific appraisals makes the measure performance appear more volatile than may be the case. Given this, the agency has reached out toour county clients through the Executive Committee of the Oregon State Association of County Assessors for input on a more meaningfulreplacement measure. The agency anticipates proposing a replacement measure once the results of this solicitation process have been received.
Factors Affecting Results
The agency is required to appraise Oregon’s highly complex industrial properties engaged in processing and manufacturing activities. Theseproperties don’t sell often. When they do, there are many factors that make it difficult to compare the sales price to assessment roll values.Therefore, the limited number of sales transactions has decreased the reliability of the measure.
Some properties have valuation issues including omitted property, taxpayer reporting errors (duplicates, age of assets, failure to report), andreclassification of assets. These issues create a greater separation between the value determined via our mass appraisal process and the valuedetermined via our site-specific appraisal process, resulting in higher COD and greater deviation between RMV and the roll value.
In 2017, the agency refined its business strategy and set a goal to appraise industrial sites on an eight-year cycle. Once our first regular appraisalcycle is complete, a more consistent COD trend should emerge. Through the implementation of our eight-year appraisal cycle, we shouldcontinue to see a reduction in the deviation between roll values and RMV; however, this cycle won’t be complete for another four years. Lookingahead, the agency anticipates proposing a more informative outcome measure for this program.

How Are We Doing
The agency’s cost of funds for direct enforcement for fiscal year 2019 was $0.24. For this measure, lower performance is better. This represents adecrease from the 2018 cost of $0.27. Dollars received increased from $237.8 million in 2018 to $266.4 million in 2019. The enforcement functionfunding in our Legislatively Adopted Budget (LAB) remained constant at $64.3 million for both fiscal year 2018 and 2019.
At the heart of this measure is a complex enforcement revenue calculation based on numerous variables that periodically need to be adjusted dueto new information. The challenge is that some factors, including automation supporting efficiency, can impact the calculation in unintended ways.Trying to adjust the calculation to appropriately compensate for those changes becomes a bit of trial and error. As a result, the agency’sconfidence in the accuracy of this measure has decreased. In addition, the measure is very complex to explain. The agency anticipatescompleting a comprehensive assessment of its KPMs during the 2019–21 biennium. The goal is to find a more suitable measure of enforcementoutcomes.
Factors Affecting Results
There are many factors that impact cost of funds. Some of the external factors affecting enforcement revenue include the health of the overalleconomy and the labor market participation rate; however, we don’t have a way to measure the impact of those factors on our activities.
Internal factors influencing our enforcement revenue include:
Increases or decreases to our LAB.The Corporation Section has a lot of volatility in its enforcement revenue received. In past fiscal years, one audit payment has made a largedifference in the measure’s performance. Corporation enforcement revenue dollars increased this fiscal year which caused the largestimpact to the measure. This increase is directly tied to relatively few audit payments.

How Are We Doing
Improving employee engagement has been a long-standing priority for the agency. In 2013, the agency contracted with the Department ofAdministrative Services to access an employee engagement measurement tool used by several other state agencies. This tool was used to reportresults from 2013 to 2018. In 2018, the Department of Administrative Services stopped offering access to the tool and the contract with the vendorwasn’t renewed. DOR was given a one-time extension to have access to the tool with reduced features: closed ended questions without keyreporting capability. In 2019, the agency did not have access to this tool so an employee engagement survey was not completed. Given this, theagency would like to delete this measure and undertake an internal review to determine an appropriate replacement measure for this KPM.
Factors Affecting Results
At the request of the legislature, the Oregon Secretary of State (SOS) conducted an organizational culture audit in 2018. This audit providedagency leadership additional insights into employee engagement and overall morale. The agency has implemented actions based on the findingsof the SOS audit with a focus towards increasing morale, engagement, and improving the overall culture of Revenue. The agency also hired aconsultant to evaluate the current state of outcome-based management practices and policies within the agency. An outcome-based managementapproach is built on a foundation that looks to engage and empower employees at all levels of the organization. Given this, the agencywill explore new ways to measure organizational health beyond employee engagement with the understanding that this information will bemaintained as an internal measure by the Human Resources Section and available upon request.
