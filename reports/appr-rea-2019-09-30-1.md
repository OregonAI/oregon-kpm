How Are We Doing
For the 2019 report period 100% of those property managers and principal brokers who completed the compliance review process, including the follow up survey, came into compliance within 45
days of the review completion.
Factors Affecting Results
This key performance measure was implemented in the reporting period 2014. In this initial cycle, results were far below the 90% target at 69%. The low compliance rate reported at that time was
due to a lack of clarity in the communication with participants regarding the time commitment, required follow up and 45 day resultant evaluation required to collect the necessary data to report on
this measure. In response, the Agency modified the administration of the reviews to both clarify the process for participants and eliminate questions the essentially could not be corrected within a 45
day process. Since these corrections the rate has continually exceed the target.
A continuing challenge remains obtaining enough participants to produce a meaningful measure. In this reporting period only six licensees completed this process. The Agency is currently
considering new streams of communication and better visibility of this process to encourage greater participation.

How Are We Doing
In the 2019 reporting period 88% of cases were completed within 150 days of receipt of the complaint, well exceeding the 60% target. Given recent performance in this KPM the legislature increased
the Agency target from 60% to 80% for reporting periods FY20 & 21.
Factors Affecting Results
During this reporting period the Agency experienced two Financial Investigator vacancies. Drops in staffing in the Regulations division has a significant effect on this performance measure. Existing
cases must be worked at the same level of depth to ensure consumer protection resulting in a delay in the assignment of pending cases, thus decreasing the percentage of cases that can be
completed in 150 days.
As a use of developing Agency talent, the Administrative Specialist in the Regulations division was offered a promotional opportunity to train and work as an investigator while an Administrative
Specialist from the Administrative Services division backfilled the remaining administrative workload. While experiencing a dip from the previous reporting period, the Agency was able to maintain a
rate well above target.

The Agency rate of settlement of contested case actions dropped slightly from 88% to 85% since the last reporting period, falling short of the 95% target.
Factors Affecting Results
The Agency has not achieved the target since the performance measure implementation in 2014 when the target was set as a benchmark of performance at that time. The settlement process plays

later rescinded their request for a formal hearing after the initial request.

How Are We Doing
In FY2019 the Agency failed to meet the 75% target by 2%, at a 73% result.
Factors Affecting Results
In 2017 Legislative session the Agency requested and received approval for a data collection change to this KPM. Formerly this question was asked of new licensees six months after obtaining their
license. The comments related to the ratings indicated that the licensee was dissatisfied with the examination's preparation for selling real estate and the daily functions of marketing a real estate
professional's business.
By changing the reporting period from six to one month after obtaining a license the measure's performance improved from 67% to meeting the goal for the first time at 75%. However in the recent
reporting period we are seeing that this number is beginning to decrease once again and the comments related to the measure continue to confuse the Agency's role in preparing a licensee for
selling real estate.
Given that the measure inquires as to the licensee's perspective on the examination’s efficacy as a screen for competent and ethical professionals, and ethics are not regulated by the Agency and
competency is regularly confused for success in the business, the Agency is considering a request to sunset this measure and replace it with a more meaningful measure related to education and
examination in the next session. The Agency will work with the Budget and Legislative Fiscal Analyst in the interim to identify potential replacements for legislative consideration.

How Are We Doing
The Agency meets or exceeds all customer service targets in all categories except for Helpfulness. This category is at 80%, falling short of the 85% target by 5%.
Factors Affecting Results
The Agency has taken a very proactive approach to customer service by prioritizing resources to answer the phone and respond to emails on a close to real time basis. The average hold time for
phone calls is currently 26 seconds and all emails are answered in the same day.
There are two primary factors contributing to helpfulness failing to meet the target. First, helpfulness is historically the lowest performing category in the customer service survey. The comments
associated are often related to how the Agency didn't help resolve an issue that is not within the Agency's purview. The Agency does however make great efforts to direct the caller to the correct
organization to resolve their issue. Secondly, the Agency observed that the survey participation is down significantly over previous periods. We have now instituted a new policy to end every call
with a request to participate in the customer service survey. This has increased participation significantly and as a result increased performance in all areas. We expect our results in the next fiscal
year to reflect an improvement.
