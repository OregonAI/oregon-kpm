How Are We Doing
Overall the agency has made progress on this performance measure.  There is a direct relationship between staffing levels and the timely response on the email.  On January 1, 2016, the agency
received legislative authority to increase the licensing fees. The increased revenue has allowed the agency to hire more public service representatives to answer emails. In spring 2016, the agency
experienced staff turnover in the area responsible for email correspondence.  At present, the agency has approximately a two month backlog of emails. As the agency re-hires positions, the email
response time should improve significantly. The agency is also planning to implement technological and procedural changes to help alleviate some of the email workload.
The agency answers all phone calls directly and does not have adequate staffing levels to respond to recorded messages.  In light of this process, the agency did not include telephone calls into
the response percentage.
Factors Affecting Results
In January 2016, the agency implemented a new teaching licensing structure.  Though the new structure is simpler and easier to understand, the agency did experience a significant increase in
questions related to the transition from the old structure to the new one.  In addition, the agency is experiencing longer calls in order to help educators through the new online application process.
The ability to answer emails is dependent on staffing levels.The agency has recently experienced staff turnover in the area responsible for emails.  While new hiring is currently taking place, it will be
several months before the new staff is sufficiently trained to answer specific licensure email questions.

How Are We Doing
The agency is not meeting the KPM target for this area.  In the past couple of months, however, the agency is seeing significant improvement in production.  At present, the agency is processing
approximately 1,000 more applications per month over the previous year.  If this level of production continues, the agency will be able to reduce greatly the time of processing by the end of
biennium.
Factors Affecting Results
The agency recently implemented an online application and payment system as well as implemented a newly restructured licensure system.  During the first phase of implementation, only the paper
application and payment system was eliminated from the agency's workload (e.g., applications and money are now collected online).  Thus, the first phase had minimal effect on how licenses are
processed. It simply made it easier for applicants to submit applications and fees. The second phase of the online application system includes the actual licensing processing functions. These new
functions will eliminate the need for most incomplete notices to applicants, allowing staff to focus on issuing all complete applications.  The agency expects this new functionality to reduce
significantly application processing time.  The second phase of the online application system is scheduled to be finished in winter 2016.

In 2015, the Commission considered 363 cases, which was a significant increase from previous years. As of July 2016, the agency had 233 pending investigations. TSPC received 281 new cases in
2015 and is on track to receive a similar number of cases in 2016.
Factors Affecting Results
Due to investigation staffing reductions and turnover, the agency lost ground in 2013-2015 on completing investigations compared to the incoming caseload. The addition of a limited duration
investigator throughout the 2015-2017 has improved the number of completed investigations. The overall number of complaints coming into the office remains high.  The agency currently has 233
pending investigations.
In addition to increased staffing, the agency has also implemented a “fast-disposition” system for cases that would normally result in dismissal, revised complaint forms with instructions for school
districts and patrons to provide more clarity, and reviewed other policies to assist with efficiency.  These changes helped the agency process 169 more investigations in 2015 than in 2014.

How Are We Doing
The customer service rating is relatively unchanged from the previous year. The agency continues to fall significantly short of the KPM target for this area. The agency believes that new initiatives
related to online application process and new teaching license structure will ultimately improve this KPM area.
Factors Affecting Results
On January 1, 2016, the agency transitioned to an online application system.  During the implementation process, applicants experienced technical difficulties with the new system.  Our system
developer is continuing to make improvements to the online application system.  At present, most applicants are applying online without any issues.  The agency has heard antidotal feedback from
educators and districts that they appreciate the ability to apply and pay through the internet.  In addition to the new online service, the agency has implemented a new teaching license structure.
The new structure provides more options to educators, streamlines the number of endorsements and places every educator on the same license system. The agency is receiving positive feedback
on this new structure. As the new services and structure become fully implemented, the agency hopes to see an improvement in the customer service numbers.
The agency has increased staffing positions in the licensure and public service representative areas.  At maximum staffing levels, the agency is able to respond to calls and emails within a
reasonable time frame.  When the agency is short staffed due to turnover or staff absences, it is much more difficult to provide an adequate level of service.  The agency is currently in the process
of replacing staff and anticipates that this will result in a higher percentage of positive customer service ratings.
