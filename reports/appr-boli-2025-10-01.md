How Are We Doing
Customer satisfaction has improved but remains below expectations: overall satisfaction rose from 25% last year to 43% this year, indicating meaningful progress from our improvement efforts but still
leaving a majority of respondents unsatisfied. Timeliness continues to lag other areas and remains the primary drive of low scores, reflecting ongoing delays and service backlogs despite gains in
other metrics such as helpfulness and expertise.
Factors Affecting Results
We have refined our survey approach to better represent the full set of people who interact with the Bureau including complainants, respondents, apprenticeship committees, and employers served by
Employer Assistance, so results more accurately reflect the range of customer experiences. This broader sampling, combined with continued high caseloads relative to capacity, helps explain current
scores: the data now captures more critical perspectives while backlogs and limited staffing constrain timeliness and overall service outcomes.

How Are We Doing

Factors Affecting Results
The Division continues to receive a significant number of wage claims. As the intake and screening teams made progress on the backlog of cases at that stage, this means that the backlog is now with

How Are We Doing
The Wage and Hour Division received 3,637 wage claims in FY 2025, which is a decrease from 4,307 wage claims in FY 2024, including claims eligible for payment from the Wage Security Fund
(either due to an employer’s closure or as the result of a final order or judgment). The Wage and Hour Division met the goal of processing Wage Security Fund claims within 30 days 59% of the time in
FY 2024, falling short of its goal of 85%.  In FY 2025, the Wage and Hour Division processed Wage Security Fund claims within 30 days 61% of the time, showing a 2% increase in efficiency
compared to the previous year and a 5% increase in efficiency compared to FY 2023.
Factors Affecting Results
It is likely this drop in wage claims from FY2024 to FY 2025 may be attributed to the triage that was implemented in October 2024.  While the number of wage claims received decreased in FY2025,
the number of claims pending assignment for investigation by a compliance specialist continued to increase.  As the intake and screening teams made progress on the backlog at those initial stages, it
means that the backlog has now moved to the investigative stage of the process.  This additional workload for staff, especially with a focus on priority wage claims, including minimum wage, child
labor violations, and Wage Security Fund, has resulted in delays in the overall efficiency of investigating wage claims, including determining whether the claim is eligible for payment from the Wage
Security Fund.  The number of claims eligible for payment has dramatically impacted our ability to meet this KPM.

How Are We Doing
The Wage and Hour Division has shown improvement in responding to requests for Prevailing Wage Rate (PWR) coverage determinations in FY 2024 compared to FY 2025. In FY 2024, the division
responded to these requests within 15 days 97% of the time. In contrast, in FY 2025, the Division responded to requests for PWR predeterminations within 15 days 98% of the time. The Division has
made a concerted effort to respond to these requests in a timely manner over the past year, however that involves removing prevailing wage investigators from their cases to do so, which you can see
reflected in the extended length of time it is taking to process those cases.
Factors Affecting Results
The Division received a 41% increase in determination requests, from 86 in FY 2024 to 122 in FY 2025. As indicated above, due to the time-sensitive nature of determination requests, the Division
must shift valuable resources away from prevailing wage investigations to timely respond.  Ultimately, this significantly impacts the Division’s performance goals for prevailing wage investigations.
Lastly, the complexity of these requests has also increased due to the introduction of new funding sources, such as "Lift Funds" and community block grants, which require additional analysis to
determine if they meet the definition of public funds and whether exemptions apply.

How Are We Doing
The Wage and Hour Division met its performance target of completing 25% of Prevailing Wage Rate (PWR) investigations within 60 days, but did not meet its targets in completing 50% of the
investigations within 90 days and 75% within 120 days.  There’s been a modest improvement for timeliness for completing 25% of investigations within 60 days.
In the 2025 KPM timeframe, the completion rate stayed relatively stable from the previous year, although the number of case closures increased by approximately 13% from FY 2024.
Factors Affecting Results
The primary factors in the timely completion of PWR investigations are the availability of trained staff to conduct the investigations and the complexity of the cases, additionally encumbered by the
number of parties involved in these cases. Because this unit has yet to have the staff capacity necessary to perform prevailing wage determinations (including having rejected fiscals when the
prevailing wage is expanded), we sometimes must pull investigators off their cases to perform the determinations. If determinations are not performed promptly, developers and contractors could pay
the incorrect rate and then be penalized. The unit has recently re-prioritized timely wage determinations due to the negative impact it can have when they don't receive the determinations promptly.
In addition, PWR investigations typically involve the time-consuming examination of extensive payroll records of multiple contractors for work performed on public works projects, often covering a
lengthy period of time and sometimes requiring litigation to resolve. These records denote each hour of work at different classifications of work per employee. Further, the level of cooperation from
contractors, subcontractors, and sureties often impacts the timeliness of investigations. The time required to obtain and analyze relevant documents and resolve issues discovered during the
investigation also affects the division's ability to conduct timely investigations. Lastly, prevailing wage cases involve many affected workers on multiple projects, often delaying the closure of many
cases until each actual target worker is located and paid their unpaid wages and damages.
