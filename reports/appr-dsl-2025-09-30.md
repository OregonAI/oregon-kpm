How Are We Doing
The FY25 Department KPM 2a results are better than in the previous five years. Revenues for forest lands were down $1 million from FY24, but up $580,000 from FY23. Forestland expenses
decreased by $1.5 million compared to the previous year.

impacting industrial, commercial, and residential revenue in FY25. Some of the income shortfalls are cyclical and may rebound in FY26.

How Are We Doing
The target of no net loss of wetlands was not met for FY 25. There was a net loss of 43.62 acres of wetlands through permitted impacts, enforcement actions, compensatory mitigation and voluntary
restoration.
Factors Affecting Results
The Department applies mitigation requirements based upon its regulatory authorities and responsibilities. Additionally, wetlands are gained from voluntary habitat projects conducted by private
organizations and agencies. This measure varies greatly from year to year, typically due to when there are gains from mitigation banks and voluntary habitat projects.
DSL plans to report on this measure differently in the future. DSL is developing a new database that is expected to go-live in 2026 and will improve how we track this information. Currently, gains from
mitigation banks – larger wetland habitat projects approved to sell credits to others as compensation for permitted wetland losses - are counted the year the project is constructed. However, these
credits are sold to compensate for permitted losses of wetlands over 5-10 years, or more. In contrast, loss of wetland is counted in the fiscal year when a permit is issued.

How Are We Doing
The Department averaged 94 days to make a permit decision in FY 25. This does not meet the target of 60 days or less.
Factors Affecting Results
This measure reports decision timeframes for Individual Permits, which are often related to large projects with significant impacts. The applications often require a high level of technical information

The statutory requirement for the agency to reach a permit decision is 120 days, which includes a 30-day completion review, a 30-day public review period, and 60 days to make a permit decision.

How Are We Doing
The Department did not meet the target of responding to a wetland delineation report within 60 days. The average agency response time for FY 25 was 96 days, which is 15 days more than FY 24.
However, the agency has met the 120-day statutory requirement since FY 2009.
Factors Affecting Results
Delineation reports provide information on wetland boundaries and information about the wetland needed for permitting. The complexity and number of delineation reports submitted for review affects

respectively. Other factors include the retirement of two senior staff, training new staff, and balancing DSL responses to wetland notices from local governments and requests to review wetland
presence/absence from the public (wetland determinations), which are reviewed by the same staff reviewing wetland delineation reports.

How Are We Doing
The Department did not meet the target of responding to wetland land use notices within 22 days. The average agency response time for FY 25 was 26 days, which is 1 day more than FY 24. The
statutory requirement is 30 days.
Factors Affecting Results
Wetland land use notices are submitted to the Department by local governments when there is a project that may be in wetlands or waters. The number of wetland land use notices received is
influenced by how well the economy is doing, by how well DSL explains notification requirements to local governments, and by compliance by local governments in submitting notices.
The number of notices in FY 21, FY 22, FY 23, FY 24 and FY 25 were 1180, 1228, 1004, 861, and 867 respectively. While the number of notices received has decreased from a high in FY 22, the
percent of notices with a response within 30 days has been lower over the last two years.
Factors include the retirement of two senior staff, training new staff, and balancing DSL responses to wetland delineations needed for permitting and requests to review wetland presence/absence
from the public (wetland determinations), which are reviewed by the same staff reviewing wetland land use notices.

How Are We Doing
No data is available for 2025 – the Department conducts a statewide customer satisfaction survey every other year.
Key takeaways from the 2024 Customer Satisfaction Survey include:
All satisfaction ratings declined from the 2022 survey.
All ratings were also lower than the 2020 survey, with five of six indicators also declining from the 2018 survey. The steepest declines
from 2022 to 2024 were timeliness (6% decline), availability of information (5% decline) and overall quality of service, (4% decline.)
Customers desire timely, consistent, responsive service.
Themes in survey comments indicate desire for:
Faster application and decision processes and increased responsiveness to inquiries.
Improvements in processes to ensure timeliness and consistency in application reviews and permit/authorization requirements.
Additional or clearer guidance and more support for applicants.
Better information about application status, including ability to receive automatic notices or check status online.
Additional staff, changes in policy or process, or improved technology were all identified as ways customers thought service could be improved.
Many customers report positive experiences.
Comments discussed times when staff provided exceptional service, going above and beyond to help customers.
Many customers appreciate the services provided by DSL programs.
In particular, South Slough Reserve’s variety of educational and recreational offerings were commended.
Factors Affecting Results
Factors that may have affected 2024 results include:
Cumulative service impacts of inadequate staffing levels.
Multiple satisfaction indicators are affected when staffing is inadequate to meet baseline expectations for service delivery, with
timeliness and availability of information – which had the lowest scores – particularly dependent on having adequate staff to provide basic services.
Fewer survey responses than in 2022, particularly for South Slough Reserve.
300 people completed the survey for all DSL programs; in 2022 364 people completed the survey. South
Slough had significantly fewer responses in 2024 (52) than 2022 (116). South Slough’s satisfaction ratings are consistently higher than ratings for other DSL programs. This may be attributed to
the excellent service provided by the South Slough team, as well as the reality that services like recreation and education are perceived differently – and often more positively – than services
with regulatory or enforcement components.

How are we doing?
During 2005-07 the Department of Administrative Services received a budget note to develop best practice performance measures to be applied to governance of boards and commissions.  The 2007-
09 Legislature added KPM 17 for all agencies with governing Boards and Commissions. The State Land Board currently meets all but one of the 15 criteria.
Factors Affecting Results
The Director does not receive a written annual performance review.  The Board and Director meet on a regular basis and regular feedback is provided, but the feedback structure does not align with
the prescribed format. The Department of Administrative Services worked with Gallup to implement a 360-degree performance evaluation process for all State Agency Directors.  The Department of
State Lands will participate, and the Director will receive a performance evaluation.  A new Director started at the Agency on August 1, 2025 and we plan to conduct the 360-degree evaluation within
the first year of her appointment, therefore being able to meet all the criteria for this performance measure in 2026.
