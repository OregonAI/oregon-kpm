How Are We Doing
Our agency is statutorily mandated to inspect, at least once every two years (a biennium), the premises and records of all licensed facilities – we currently regulate 730 licensed facilities located
throughout the state. In order to satisfy this requirement, our inspectors must complete at least half of these facility inspections (approximately 365) during each July 1 - June 30 reporting period. For
the period of July 1, 2020 through June 30, 2021, our inspectors performed and completed a total of 335 facility inspections. These inspections included 296 random biennial inspections and 39
biennial inspections as part of a licensing action. The number of completed full biennial inspections equate to a rate of 92% of licensed facilities not less than once per biennium.
Factors Affecting Results
Although our agency faced a number of inspection challenges during this reporting period as a result of the pandemic and wildfires throughout the state, our inspection completion rate actually
increased significantly: 61% from last year – 31% for 2020 to 92% for 2021.
During 2020, our agency implemented protocols for conducting virtual inspections, which allowed our inspectors to continue to work and strive towards meeting our statutory mandate, while fully
complying with all of the statewide and public health restrictions.
This transition from on-site inspections to virtual ones allowed our agency to make significant progress towards satisfying the requirement for facility inspections.

How Are We Doing
For last year's reporting period, our agency was unable to provide any data since we had recently transitioned to a different website management system, which unfortunately deactivated our customer
service survey. During this reporting period (specifically, September 2020), our agency posted an updated web-based customer survey. A link to the survey is located on the home page of our
agency's website & is also included in all staff's signature lines.
Factors Affecting Results
For the reporting period of January 1, 2020 - December 30, 2020, our agency received 15 customer service survey responses. Agency staff continues to have discussions to determine ways in which
to enhance the survey's effectiveness and provide additional opportunities to receive appropriate feedback from licensees, stakeholders, and people from the public.
Timeliness was the lowest scored category, which has been significantly impacted by the pandemic. While the survey is currently not designed to request more feedback relating to specific categories,
agency staff is working on updating this particular section of the survey so that we have context regarding what is causing the perceived delay when some individuals contact our office. Agency staff is
also working on ways to be reached more easily, which includes but is not limited to, simplifying our main phone number's message to be less complicated.

How Are We Doing
The Best Practices Self-Assessment Evaluation is available on every Board Member's iPad and is reviewed during every board meeting. Board Members are encouraged to ask questions and raise
any issues/concerns they have during the year, so they may be addressed immediately. During the July 13, 2021 general session board meeting, all Board Members who served between July 1, 2020
and June 30, 2021 completed the Best Practices voting slip. Our agency scored close to the 100% target (98%) – it appears that one Board Member seeks additional information relating to the
agency's budget/financial information.
Factors Affecting Results
Generally, by reviewing the Best Practices Self-Assessment Evaluation during each board meeting, all questions/issues/concerns are being properly and sufficiently addressed in a timely manner to
ensure that all Board Members can clearly understand and identify the agency's expectations and determine if these expectations are being met.
Specifically in response to the Board Member who seeks more information relating to the agency's budget/financial information, our agency has scheduled a training session in late October that will
include DAS Shared Financial Services, the Board Officers, and the agency's Executive Director. The training session is intended to provide an opportunity for the Board Officers to better understand
and become more familiar with the financial documents that are provided during the year. Once the training session has concluded and the Board Officers are more knowledgeable about the
documents, the Board Officers will work with the Executive Director to determine how these financial documents will be presented during future board meetings to ensure that all Board Members are
aware of the agency's financials and eventually understand them better.

How Are We Doing
For the reporting period of January 1, 2020 through January 1, 2021, our agency opened 38 cases. Out of the 38 total cases, 33 cases were closed within 9 months of receiving each complaint,
resulting in a rate of 87%.
Factors Affecting Results
Our agency's investigator is also responsible for conducting background investigations for certain license applications. The recent increase in applications received that require background
investigations has significantly reduced the time available for assisting with case closure. Since the beginning of the pandemic, our agency's compliance manager has served as the agency liaison
with the State Office of Emergency Management as well as performing the essential duties/responsibilities of the currently vacant Indigent Disposition Program (IDP) Administrator. Both of these
significant roles have negatively impacted the ability to close certain cases within the designated period of time. It is also important to mention that our agency's counsel is working with us on some of
the open cases that require additional legal advisement and expertise.
