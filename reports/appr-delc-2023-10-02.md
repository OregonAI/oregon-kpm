Regional Managers review the Serious Valid Compliance Verification report monthly in 1:1 meetings with their team members. The Child Care Licensing Division (formerly known as the Office of Child

Factors Affecting Results
The dataset includes data on each facility’s desired capacity by age group. Desired capacity is the number of slots they desire to fill and may differ from their licensed capacity since a facility may be
licensed for more slots than they chose to fill. Therefore, the desired capacity better represents the amount of child care available.
Relevant notes from the 2022 Child Care Desert Report:
Between early 2020 (pre-COVID) and 2022, increased state funding led to growth in the number of contracted slots offered by Oregon Prenatal to Kindergarten, Preschool Promise, and Baby
Promise.
Public slots included in this report do not capture all public investments in child care. For instance, investments in the Employment-Related Day Care program (ERDC) are not included in this analysis
of slots.
In addition, the number of publicly funded slots is likely underestimated. Local entities such as school districts or counties sometimes use their own public funds to deliver child care services, but that
information was not in the available data and so those slots could have been missed. Head Start Child Care Partnerships use federal dollars to fund child care slots within community facilities, but
those community facilities may not have been identified in the dataset. Also, families receiving Relief Nursery services often participate in a publicly funded early learning program, but a reliable
number of those who participated in these programs was not available.

How Are We Doing
Data collection for this KPM is in progress, but delayed and the reported actual percentage represents data collected in 2019 (January - December) and 2020 (January - March).
Factors Affecting Results
A person is considered part of the workforce in a given year if they worked any part of that year based on Oregon Registry Online (ORO) start, hire, and end dates. ORO serves as Oregon's child care
professional learning registry for the child care workforce. By matching data for multiple years using a person’s unique identification number, we can see which years the person has been in the
workforce. “Remained in the workforce” is anyone in the 2019 workforce who continued into the 2020 workforce. Unlike previous years, the 2020 workforce is anyone who worked in the first three
months of 2020 (Jan-March 15, 2020). [Because child care was closed during the start of the pandemic and an emergency licensing structure was used, hire/position dates weren’t updated in the
system in a consistent way to have confidence to know who was truly working in the later part of 2020].
