How Are We Doing
The Marine Board monitors patrol statistics quarterly and works with service providers to prioritize efforts to better serve Oregon’s boaters.  We encourage on-water patrol if possible, but recognize
that shore-based patrol can also be an efficient means of contacting boaters, and in drought years, it may be the only way to contact boaters.  Extreme, sustained weather conditions are the
primary reason that on-water patrol declined significantly in 2016-17. Fall drought conditions followed by a heavy winter, late spring and cold, high flows continuing well into spring were unfavorable
to boating.
Factors Affecting Results
Factors that could impact the ability to achieve the target include boating activity (number of boat use days), weather, salary, and fuel cost, water levels, boater compliance, and other related marine
duties.  The number of on-water patrol hours is directly related to the level of funding for patrol services as well.  With improved funding, we had hoped to see an increase in general patrol statistics.
However, continuing drought drove marine patrols towards more shore patrol in summer and fall 2016, and early winter rains that lead into a heavy winter ended the boating season abruptly.  Many
waterways that receive low moderate use in the late fall and winter were obstructed during the winter, and then opened very late due to heavy, late snow accumulations.  Rivers had high, sustained
flows well into spring, and the extended rainy season was not conductive to boating.  In addition, several larger marine programs experienced significant staff turnover, and some programs have had
ongoing difficulties hiring replacement officers thus leaving positions vacant through the boating season.

How Are We Doing
This year the fourth straight year of BUII arrest increases in Oregon.  With legal recreational marijuana, the Marine Board has increased emphasis, training, and outreach to BUII.  BUIIs increased
another 22% this last year due in part to increased emphasis and effective saturation patrols.  OSMB continues to participate in the national Operation Dry Water campaign, continues Advanced
Roadside Impaired Driver Enforcement training for officers, and is conducting saturation patrols in select areas with good success.  Alcohol continues to be involved in a percentage of accidents but
alcohol was not conclusively linked to any fatalities during the 2016-17 fiscal years.
Factors Affecting Results
Boater education on the impact of impairment, patrol presence (saturation patrol efforts) and officer training and commitment affect results.

How Are We Doing
The Marine Board sent an electronic survey to Aquatic Invasive Species Permit holders, boating facility providers, and registered boat owners. The 2017 survey showed a significant increase in the
proportion of respondents rating the Marine Board as “good” or “excellent” in timeliness and helpfulness. There was not a significant change in rating in other areas: accuracy, information
availability, or expertise. The overall score (average of all areas) slightly increase to 84%. The slow recovery in rating is anticipated to continue as the Marine Board improves processes with the
new registration database and resolves backlogs from the transition. Many customers were not satisfied with the amount of time it took to receive boat titles or registrations in the mail. Customers
also noted frustration with phone calls and voicemails being unanswered.
Factors Affecting Results
In January 2017, the Marine Board transitioned to a new phone system that included a phone tree and a “hunt group” to route callers to available registration agents. The system did not work well
and many customers reported their calls were unanswered or that they were routed to voicemail. In August 2017 the phone system was upgraded to a call center that so far has been very
successful in handling the call volume.
