How Are We Doing
The percentage of emails responded to within 30 days is slightly down this last year, primarily due to challenges from the COVID pandemic and a staffing reduction. Two Public Service
Representatives are responsible for replying to general licensure questions and processing all incoming documentation (e.g. transcripts and district correspondence). With the increase in numbers of
applications during the summer of 2021, the volume of incoming email and eLicensing Messages also increased. The PSR team answers emails and messages in the order they are received, and
keeps the two queues on the same date. But even with improvements to the website and instructions within the eLicensing system, educators struggle to understand the licensing process. These
factors are reflected within this year's performance on this measure.
NOTE: The email response rate was calculated by dividing the number of reporting days with no email pending more than 3 days (70) by the total number of reporting days in the collection period
(177). This similar to the method used in 2020. It differs from the method utilized prior to implementation of the new system in April 2019. Therefore, comparisons of the 2020 and 2021 rates with 2018
and prior rates are not advised. No response was provided in 2019 due to the actual system transition period.
Factors Affecting Results
Agency staff continued to work remotely during the COVID pandemic. The temporary workflow changes put in place during the early phase of the pandemic, needed to be adjusted to what has
become a longer disruption of normal agency protocols. Several temporary licensing provisions that have provided much needed flexibility to school districts in staffing licensed educators
have contributed to increased staff time addressing related emergent concerns. In turn, this has impacted our ability to continue our positive trend in improvement on this measure. As mentioned
above, the agency saw a significant increase in applications submitted in the summer of 2021, partially due to increased hiring as schools prepared to reopen to in-person instruction, as well as
applicants completing their teacher preparation programs. These, too, have impacted email response rates in the last several months.

How Are We Doing
The reported result of applications initially processed within 30 days continues to improve. Nonetheless, this indicator does not fully reflect the continued improvement the agency has seen in
evaluating applications more quickly. For five of this year's reporting months, applications were processed within 3-4 weeks; another two months saw applications processed within 5-6 weeks. The
overall average of days to initial review of applications for this reporting period is 37. The final result is impacted greatly by the number of applications received in the last few months of this reporting
year. Nonethless, for more months during the year than ever, license applications have been processed in less than four weeks.
Factors Affecting Results
Spring and Summer of 2021 saw record numbers of applications filed, as schools hired staff to permit the reopening of in-person learning. Some of the backlog was exacerbated by a delay in
fingerprint processing at the Oregon State Police, which prevented us from completing initial evaluations much sooner during the busy summer months.

How Are We Doing
The Professional Practices Division is dedicated to reducing the number of cases which have been pending investigation for ten or more months. The Key Performance Measure (KPM) established for
the Professional Practices Division sets a lofty goal of having 75% of TSPC investigations which have not pended in Other Venue completed in six months or less. The TSPC Professional Practices
Division has always prioritized student safety cases over all other case types, regardless of the date received. The passage of Senate Bill 155 (2019) has gone a step further by making Educator
Sexual Conduct cases the top priority for TSPC investigations upon reception of the complaint.
Due to our commitment to investigate Senate Bill 155 cases and other existing prioritizations, TSPC cannot devote 100% of agency investigation personnel to addressing the backlog of cases 100% of
the time. We are required to shift limited agency personnel and resources between tackling Educator Sexual Conduct and other student safety cases immediately, and addressing a backlog of lower
priority cases when time and circumstances allow us to do so. This is why the Division must address meeting the KPM strategically, and over an extended timeframe. But when the Division achieves
our strategic annual goals of bringing the backlog of cases to within ten months or less of the present date, logic dictates that the Division’s KPM numbers will reflect dramatic improvement.
Factors Affecting Results
The only way the Professional Practices Division will ever come close to achieving the KPM goal is if staff eeliminate, through completion of investigation, existing cases which have been awaiting
TSPC investigation for extended periods of time (ten or more months). Only by systematically eliminating the back-log of cases to be investigated, and by bringing the oldest case pending TSPC
Investigation to within ten months since the case was received by TSPC, will the Professional Practices Division be able to legitimately have a discussion about coming close to meeting the
established KPM. To that end, the Professional Practices Division has established annual goals which commenced in 2020 and will run through 2025. These goals are purposed with reducing and
eliminating the TSPC cases pending investigation for longer than ten months. For 2020 -2021, the Division’s goal was to bring all pending investigations to within twelve months of the date the
complaint was received. For 2022, the annual goal will be to further reduce the backlog by bringing all pending cases to within nine months of the date the complaint was received. The annual goals
proceed in that manner until, ultimately, the goal is to have all pending TSPC investigations, which have not pended in Other Venue, to within six months of the date the complaint was received.

The agency has spent this past year developing a new, comprehensive customer service measurement protocol. The new methodology is effective October 1, 2021. Surveys of customer service will
include common questions applicable across the agency while including unit-specific indicators that can be utilized to improve practice within individual units.
There has been no valid data collected regarding this KPM since the implementation of the eLicensing Version 2.0 in April 2019. The new system did not provide the methodology to survey this
metric. Nonetheless, prior to the April 2019 transition, customers completing the survey generally expressed satisfaction with the agency’s services. Remarks at the time of this survey include:
The services have greatly improved as compared to five years ago when I last renewed my teaching license.
I've only called TSPC a couple of times, and each time I was barely on hold if at all and spoke to a very friendly and most importantly helpful person.
