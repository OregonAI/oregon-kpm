How Are We Doing
DELC exceeded the target value for 2025. The percentage of citations for serious valid findings that received timely follow-up to confirm compliance was 95%, a five percentage point increase from
the previous year. In this legislative fiscal year, there were 535 confirmed citations with serious valid findings. Of these serious valid findings, 510 had compliance verified timely, within 33 business
days of the citation.
Factors Affecting Results
The Child Care Licensing Division (CCLD) continues to use a legacy database to collect data needed to report on this KPM and extracting this data is complex and requires substantial data cleaning.
It is difficult to interpret the observed change in the value for this KPM from 2024 to 2025. However, clear processes and procedures helped to support staff in doing this work. Managers used data
from reports when meeting with staff on a monthly basis. Managers were able to help support staff by monitoring the data in the reports for visits that require a follow up to a serious valid finding and
then discussing with staff during the monthly meetings to ensure visits are getting completed according to the timeline requirement. CCLD is in the process of transitioning to a modern database with
an anticipated launch date in the fall of 2026. CCLD anticipates improvements in data collection with the launch of the updated database, which will help managers in supporting staff to meet timeline
requirements for serious valid finding follow up visits.

How Are We Doing
We exceeded the target set for 2025 by 6.8 percentage points. This KPM is from DELC's Estimating Supply dataset, which is updated in partnership with Central Coordination and the Oregon Child
Care Research Partnership. The Estimating Supply data are collected every two years and so targets for this KPM are set on a two-year cycle. Data is current as of December 2024.
Factors Affecting Results
DELC's Growing Oregon Together Strategic Goal 3: Infants and Toddlers -- All families with infants and toddlers are supported through early learning and care programs that recognize and meet their
unique needs relates directly to this key performance measure.
DELC's Baby Promise program allows families in participating regions to bypass the ERDC waitlist.
The number of publicly funded child care slots for infant and toddlers has increased by 274 since 2022. The increase in public programming for infants and toddlers has supported two of Oregon's
counties to move out of child care desert status for infants and toddlers (Multnomah and Gilliam). Without publicly funded child care slots for infant and toddler, all counties in Oregon would still be
child care deserts for infants and toddlers (
Oregon's Child Care Deserts 2024
).

We exceeded the target set for 2025 by 1.72 percentage points. This KPM is from DELC's Estimating Supply dataset, which is updated in partnership with Central Coordination and the Oregon Child
Care Research Partnership. The Estimating Supply data are collected every two years, and so targets for this KPM are set on a two year cycle. Data are current as of December 2024.
Factors Affecting Results
Currently, 9 counties in Oregon are child care deserts for preschool age children. Of the 27 counties that are not preschool child care deserts, 19 would become deserts without publicly funded slots.
Public slots make up 18% to 100% of preschool slots in these counties. Only eight counties would continue to not be deserts without publicly funded slots. ​
Counties that would become deserts without publicly funded slots include Clackamas, Clatsop, Columbia, Coos, Crook, Curry, Grant, Harney, Hood River, Jackson, Jefferson, Josephine, Klamath,
Lake, Lane, Lincoln, Malheur, Umatilla, and Wasco (Oregon's Child Care Deserts 2024).

How Are We Doing
Overall retention remained relatively constant (-0.23 percentage points) compared to our 2024 submission. The 2024 retention submission was 77.49% and this year's 2025 retention submission is

Factors Affecting Results
A person is considered part of the workforce in a given year if they worked any part of that year based on ORO start, hire, and end dates. By matching data for multiple years using a person’s unique
identification number, we are able to see which years the person has been in the workforce. “Remained in the workforce” is anyone in the 2022 workforce who continued into the 2023 workforce. The
number of people in the 2022 workforce who continued to work in 2023 is 18,532. While the percentage of retained workforce experienced no meaningful change from our 2024 submission, the
workforce has grown such that the number of workforce members retained increased by 658.

How Are We Doing
The target 2025 percent was exceeded by 24 percentage points. When comparing the 2024 calendar year (this report submission) to the 2023 calendar year (the previous report submission) reporting
periods, the timely central background registry approvals increase by 20 percentage points.
Factors Affecting Results
In 2024, DELC added staff to both the intake and background check teams. Through ongoing collaboration and communication, process streamlining continued. Reporting needs were identified
allowing the development of new data tools that guide the work. Administrative systems were stood up that help intake and background staff more effectively track their work. Inter-office resources
were built to provide staff with information in one central location that is updated in relative real time. System improvements included adding information relevant to staff members' work that helped
with prioritization. These activities increased staff engagement which contributed to a decrease in staff turnover. Additional activities occurred to support staff retention to maintain high functioning
teams. The data tools created by Intake and Customer Service management made a tremendous positive impact on the Background Check Unit management and team.
