How Are We Doing
Alcohol licensees recorded an 81% pass rate on 1706 alcohol minor decoy operations.  This is below the target KPM of a 90% pass rate.
The Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission resumed full-scale minor decoy operations in FY 2023 after a two-and-a-half-year hiatus for the COVID-19 emergency.
Factors Affecting Results
The Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission resumed minor decoy operations in FY 2023.  Licensees and their employees seem to have developed a low-perception for likelihood of detection as
initial operations resulted in very low initial licensee pass rates.  As the rate of inspections increased to full tempo in FY 2024, licensee pass rates have returned to historical averages, which are still
well below the target pass rate of 90%.
Licensees participating in the Responsible Vendor Program outperformed non-participant licensees, with a 9% improvement in pass-rates for RVP members.
With about 12,500 retail, minor decoy visits are not as frequent per licensee as in the marijuana program  due to the sheer number of retail outlets versus the number of Alcohol Regulatory
Specialists.

How Are We Doing
The rate of second violations for FY 2025 was 11%, with 69 premises out 625 premises receiving a notice of violation for at least two separate cases over the 3 year period. That is a 2% decrease
over the prior reported rate, and below the target rate of 12%.
The period covered by the current rate includes violations received by the Administrative Hearings Division over the period between July 1, 2022 through June 30, 2025.
Factors Affecting Results
During the 3-year period covered by this metric, the commission has returned to a rate of minor decoy operations that is comparable to the rate before the 2015 implementation of the recreational
marijuana program.  More cases are being sent to the Administrative Hearings Division with 400 alcohol license cases forwarded to the division in FY 2025 alone.
The way data is managed has also allowed disaggregation of cases with multiple separate charge events rolled into a single settlement.  In the past, those were treated as a single case event.  The
rate of violations is also being calculated this year to include any repeated violations by premises within 3-years of the reporting year.

How Are We Doing
The FY 2025 average licensing time statewide was 74 days from receipt of the application, a steep reduction from last year’s time-to-license of 137 days. This is below the target of 75 days.
Factors Affecting Results
There are many factors affecting the number of days it takes to issue a liquor license; both internal and some external.
Internal factors continue to be identified and streamlined through process improvements and technological solutions.
External factors affecting processing times are applicant responsiveness to the process and the license application review by the local governing body (city or county). Statute gives local governments
up to 90 days (45 days plus an additional 45 day extension if requested) to review a license application within their jurisdiction and provide a recommendation (positive, negative, or neutral). The
OLCC cannot complete the processing of an application until the local government review is completed.

How Are We Doing
This year the Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission customer service survey resulted in an average rating of 69% across all 6 survey questions and the 6 stakeholder groups that are surveyed by
the commission.  The commission missed the 85% evaluating service as excellent or good in all categories.  Alcohol licensees responded with average satisfaction that exceeded targets in all
categories except for timeliness and availability of information.
The lowest ratings came from the permit-holder stakeholder sample.  Frustrations with knowledge and information availability marked the lowest average scores for that group (33 and 50%
respectively).  The marijuana licensee stakeholder group was also most frustrated with timeliness and information; many comments noting that staff were helpful, but processes slowed by
implementing new software and confusing legal and regulatory environment.
Factors Affecting Results
Low response rates from licensee and permitee stakeholder groups is affecting the reliability of the survey results.  Permitee response rates were below 5% with alcohol and cannabis rates at about
15%.  There is a high likelihood of selection bias with only highly motivated and dissatisfied respondents returning surveys.  Surveys are sent to sample respondents starting in July with weekly and
then bi-weekly response reminders.
Implementation of new licensing software systems impacted the perception of timeliness and information availability for marijuana and alcohol licensees.  Alcohol and marijuana licensees expressed
frustration with software vendor support for licensees trying to navigate all three of OLCC's vendor-run online interfaces (CAMP, OPTO and METRC).  The public expressed suspicion of liquor store
agent practices regarding rare liquors, and related comment on the past rare liquor scandal.  Liquor store agents respected their DM's but want more education and face time with staff.  They also
expressed frustration with POS and returning order mistakes.

How Are We Doing
The OLCC rate of return in FY 2025 was $1.86 distributed for each dollar spent.  With lower revenue and higher operating costs, the ROI for FY 2025 was lower than 2024 and below the reduced
target of $2.25 per dollar spent.
Factors Affecting Results
The reduction in the rate of return is primarily driven by the drop in revenue from reduced case sales and flat prices combined with costs related to the debt service payments for the warehouse and
technology projects, plus cost-of-living adjustments for staff.  Distillery retail outlet sales were a slightly higher proportion of total sales and the observed 35% agent compensation rate for DRO sales
meant that agent compensation did not drop in line with sales.
OLCC also increased the cost share for marijuana operations to ensure that the marijuana fund has sufficient reserves going forward.  This also reduced the distribution during FY 2025.
The target of $2.25 may be attainable in the future with a return to sales growth plus increased throughput from retail expansion and the new distribution center, however 2025-2027 biennium
forecasts indicate that OLCC may be below target this biennium as well.

How Are We Doing
The average time to license was 156 days for the 364 cases for which a
new
recreational marijuana license was issued during fiscal year 2025. The median time to license was 142 days indicating
that the average is a reasonable estimate of licensee experience but a few licenses pull the average higher.
Factors Affecting Results
Many factors affect the number of days it takes to issue a recreational marijuana license; some are internal and some external.
The primary internal factors are the recent changes in business processes and implementing the software.  The most significant factor impacting the metric is that business process that marks the
starting event for a license application has changed.  The start event for counting the days to license had been assignment to a license investigator.  That assignment used to happen after the
commission received approval for the Land-Use Compatibility Statement from the county containing the applicant’s proposed premises.  Under the new licensing system, assignments happen once an
application is received through the CAMP software.  Applications are not screened for completeness and accuracy before being assigned to investigators.
External factors influence time to license are related to applicant engagement in the process.  The CAMP software requires applicants to submit documentation with their applications before the
application is considered submitted, however, applicant documentation is often incorrect or incomplete.  Applicants currently have up to 120 days from submission to complete their applications; if they
do not get documentation in order, they are returned to the end of the queue to be re-assigned.  This means that licensees can take up to 120 days of the licensing process before investigators can
process their applications.
