How Are We Doing
actual target
The Legislative Policy and Research Office (LPRO) continues to adhere to the office's vision, mission, and values of providing the Oregon Legislative Assembly with professional nonpartisan staffing,
research, and analysis. From 2023 to 2024, LPRO's overall performance exceeded the target rate of 90%. For 2026 (January through July), LPRO’s overall performance rate was 92.8%, exceeding
the target rate. The timeliness and accuracy of services provided to members, including staffing policy committees, completing complex research activities, supporting legislatively created task forces
and workgroups, and providing language access services, continue to rank high among respondents, with 91% indicating overall timeliness as being "excellent" or "good,” and 93% as “excellent” or
“good” for accuracy. The expertise (90.9%) and helpfulness (89.1%) of LPRO staff also continue to almost meet or exceed the 90% target rate. The availability of information reported by respondents
was rated as "excellent" or "good" by 90% of those who completed the survey. From 2021 to 2025, LPRO's scores for four of the five measures exceeded the 90% target for service, with one
exception: helpfulness (89.1%). In the most recent years, 2022-24, LPRO's performance on helpfulness exceeded the target of 90%. One respondent shared, “the information is always helpful and
accurate.”
Factors Affecting Results
Leading up to the 2025 session, during the 2023-2024 interim, LPRO staffed seven task forces, the Joint Committee on Transportation Statewide Tour, and multiple workgroups, assisted policy
committees, co-led the New Member Academy and Chair/Vice-chair Trainings (November 2024, January 2025), and completed over 100 research requests from members. The Language Access
Team provided ASL interpretation services to all committees during the 2025 legislative session and offered these services to the LPRO-staffed task forces. LPRO maintains a searchable website with
information and resources for committee services, research, and language access.  LRPO continues to experience a high workload, both in session and in the interim, particularly with committee
services and legislative task forces. During the 2025 legislative session, a high volume of measures was referred to policy committees. The survey does not allow LPRO to ascertain whether the high
volume of measures might have contributed to a slight decrease in LPRO’s helpfulness measure. High bill volumes, along with numerous public hearings and work sessions across multiple
committees, exceeded the historical average for long sessions (2017-2023) and strained the office’s capacity during the session. Overall, the slight declines in several measures from 2023-2025
reflect the inherent challenges of staffing during a long legislative session.

How Are We Doing
The legislature adopted LPRO's proposed new measures in the 2023 session. This is the second reporting cycle, featuring new measures designed to capture the central services and products that
LPRO provides to the legislature, including interim work. During the previous interim, LPRO’s entire team, from legislative and research analysts to committee assistants and language access staff,
assisted seven legislative task forces and supported the Joint Transportation Listening Tour. Staff involvement in task forces and workgroups was extensive during the last interim, including policy
research, analysis, and policy development support. Relatedly, the Language Access Team ensured ASL services were provided at task force meetings, interim legislative days, and throughout the
actual target
entire 2025 session.
The survey used to collect information for KPMs did not include a question about committee services. Therefore, there is no measure to report for this cycle
.  For
publications, LPRO continues to release Summaries of Legislation, Issue Briefs, interactive dashboards and maps, legislative task force reports, and research memos. Among the measures, research
was the only measure that did not achieve the 90% target, with 87.8% respondents reporting “excellent” or “fair.” Language Access exceeded the 90% benchmark, with 100% of respondents reporting
“excellent” or “good.”
Factors Affecting Results
Among the individuals who responded to the survey, several questions were completed by 46 respondents, while other measures received responses ranging from 28 to 33 respondents, indicating a
low response rate. Based on the results shared with LPRO, only 46 individuals completed the questions about LPRO, while 47 respondents skipped them. In calculating the measures, LPRO excluded
individuals (ranging from 1 to 17 people depending on the question) who answered “don’t know” to the following topics: accuracy of information, staff expertise, availability of information, task force
staffing, language access, and legislative publications. No respondents indicated “poor” for the new measures when rating LPRO services. Generally, a response rate of fewer than 50 individuals is
not ideal for surveying purposes. Furthermore, it is unclear who took the survey and whether or how familiar those who responded were with the range of services LPRO provides. LPRO continues to
hire exceptional legislative and research analysts, committee assistants, and language access professionals. The interim workload requires LPRO to critically assess, on an ongoing basis, whether
existing levels of resources can sustain the workload. In session, the workload and pace of the committee process make it challenging to respond to requests for information promptly and provide in-
depth research when faced with quick turnaround times. Due to the low response rates and limitations of the survey, it is difficult to address how to improve the quality of research during legislative
sessions. In 2026 and 2027, LPRO will ensure prompt responses to requests for information outside of the day-to-day committee process and strive to provide timely, relevant information to members
and legislative staff.

How Are We Doing
LPRO continues to receive high marks in providing nonpartisan services to members and the branch, with 75.5% of respondents reporting "excellent" and 11% reporting "good." LPRO
did
achieve the
90% target in the two previous years.
Factors Affecting Results
LPRO provides all new employees with training on nonpartisanship expectations, including a review of the office's policy on nonpartisan expectations for both limited-duration session staff and
continuing staff. LPRO's nonpartisan policy provides a common understanding of the expectations for nonpartisanship and objectivity among our staff. Newly hired staff are assigned a mentor to
reinforce expectations around professional conduct, including avoiding partisanship and bias. All staff participate in an annual nonpartisanship training, which in 2025 was conducted by the Council of
State Governments West. In alignment with LPRO's mission, we strive to maintain an agency that is neutral, unbiased, and objective, which is imperative to foster the confidence and trust of all
members of the Oregon Legislative Assembly and the state of Oregon. While most survey respondents affirmed LPRO’s commitment to nonpartisanship, the office did not meet the 90% target. Survey
responses do not provide insights into the individual responses or indicate who responded. Therefore, it is difficult to know why some respondents perceived the office as not fully nonpartisan. Only
one respondent reported “poor” among the 45 who answered this question.
