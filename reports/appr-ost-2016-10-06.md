How Are We Doing
The goal of KPM 1 is to exceed the actuarial discount rate approved by the PERS Board, while applying the investment standards established in ORS 293.721 and 293.726. Net of fees, OPERF’s
average annual investment performance essentially matched its policy benchmark for the five-year period ended 12/31/15 (8.2% vs. 8.4%). OPERF’s average annual investment performance
ranked in the top quintile (i.e., top 20%) of all large public funds for the five-year period ended 12/31/15, per OIC consultant, Callan Associates.
Factors Affecting Results
Financial market conditions during the five-period ended 12/31/15 ranged from lackluster (e.g., 2011 and 2015) to robust (e.g., 2013 and 2014). More recently, low interest rates and extended
equity market valuations have become potential impediments to higher
ex ante
(i.e., future) return expectations.
The dominant factor influencing OPERF investment performance is prevailing economic and financial conditions as largely determined by monetary, fiscal and regulatory policies on both national
and international levels. Social, political and foreign policy matters also impact investment performance in the form of overall investment sentiment. In addition, the increasingly complex structure of
institutional-quality investment portfolios, particularly the growing deployment of private market investment strategies, has become a primary theme, both in terms of forward-looking, return-seeking
opportunities and also in terms of heightened risk management considerations and aggravated resource and personnel constraints. Consistent with its fiduciary duty, the OIC has directed OST
investment staff to diversify OPERF among multiple asset classes, geographies and strategies, including a substantial and peer-leading commitment to private market and other alternative
investments. This deliberate and broad diversification is designed to maximize risk-adjusted, long-term investment returns, and OPERF’s historically strong performance record, which has
significantly reduced contribution obligations for Oregon taxpayers and businesses, is testament to the success of this approach.
However, current economic and financial conditions are historically unique and present, relative to the OIC’s mission and fiduciary responsibilities, an unprecedented set of investment and
governance challenges. Global interest rates are at historic lows (and actually negative in many parts of the developed world), while U.S. public equity markets are trading at or near all-time
highs. The recent collapse in oil and other commodity prices has adversely affected asset values in emerging markets, while decades of expanding free trade have been abruptly interrupted by
protectionist movements in several developed market economies. These multiple macro forces now combine to create an investment environment in which liquid, high-quality investments produce
negligible or very low, single-digit returns which in turn has pushed investors of all types further out on the risk spectrum in search of the higher yields needed to meet those investors’ liabilities and
cash flow obligations.

How Are We Doing
The goal of KPM 3 is to provide investment returns and banking services that are cost-efficient compared to external sources. The Oregon Investment Council (OIC) determines the OSTF
benchmark. Meeting or beating that benchmark, net of fees, is the fund's performance goal. In 2015, OSTF's investment performance exceeded its benchmark (rolling 91-day Treasury bill returns),
while the fund’s five-year annualized return outperformed that same benchmark by 0.62% for the period ended December 31, 2015.
Factors Affecting Results
Fund investments with original maturities greater than three months are marked-to-market on a monthly basis; therefore, interest rate and credit risks affect OSTF investment performance. Low
short-term interest rates continue to challenge the fund’s return potential. The Federal Reserve’s Federal Open Market Committee increased the target Federal Funds rate by 0.25% at their
December 2015 meeting. This decision marks the first Federal Funds target rate increase in nearly 10 years and will provide OSTF with enhanced return opportunities that accompany higher
available cash investment rates.

How Are We Doing
The goal of KPM 6 is to control costs relative to the procurement of banking services. Treasury’s strategy is to aggressively manage banking services contracts to keep costs low for our customers
while helping them identify and implement cost-effective cash management practices. Treasury’s target is to maintain an average cost per transaction that is no more than half (50 percent) of the
average retail cost paid by other customers of our primary banking services provider.

Factors Affecting Results
This KPM compares the standard (or “retail”) pricing realized by most of our primary banking services provider’s commercial and government customers.  The State of Oregon continues to enjoy
significant pricing advantages. The savings highlight the significant savings achieved through central banking of state agencies and many public universities. A recent Treasury analysis indicates
that retail banking fees have increased by 4 percent over the last two years, while Treasury’s per transaction banking fees are at their lowest point in the last eight years.
As part of the Cash Management Improvement & Renewal Program (CMIRP), Treasury is in the midst of reevaluating our primary banking and cash management service delivery model and
partnerships. Our goal in this effort is to mitigate risk, and better support contingency planning and readiness, while striving to continue to provide low cost services to customers. As we pursue
changes in this area, impacts to cost per transaction may occur, including potential for some increase overall. Notwithstanding these potential impacts, Treasury expects to meet or beat the target
moving forward.

How Are We Doing
The goal of KPM 10 is to track and report state debt to the Governor and Legislature to assure that it remains at a sustainable levels,  which helps maintain Oregon’s high state credit ratings and
attractive long-term interest rates when we borrow for various capital projects.  The amount of state debt issued and outstanding increased substantially over the first half of the past decade,
although in recent years, overall state debt levels have remained level, with existing debt being retired at roughly the same pace as new debt is issued
Factors Affecting Results
State net tax-supported debt increased significantly over the latter part of the 2000s due to issuance of bonds to fund high-priority capital projects such as new correctional facilities, the
replacement of the state mental hospital, expanding university and community building needs, and transportation improvements throughout Oregon. In more recent years, new debt has been issued
at a pace that is roughly equivalent to the amount of debt that is being retired.
