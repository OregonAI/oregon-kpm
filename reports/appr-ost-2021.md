The Oregon Short Term Fund, known as the OSTF, is managed by the Oregon State Treasury to keep taxpayer dollars safe and to help governments of all sizes to stretch public funds. The OSTF
enables governments to earn a rate of return on money between when revenue is received and when the money is needed to pay bills. A portion of the earnings is required to pay for fund operations
at Treasury, but are also a drag on returns. Statute has a fee cap of 0.435 bps per month. This KPM compares the expense rate to that Lipper Money Market Fund Peer Group Management Fee.
Biennially asset management fees are generally set based on the legislatively approved budget, but current fees are set at the statutory cap of 0.435 bps per month. Actual rates charged will be
reported.  The target is based on the Lipper Money Market Mutual Fund Peer Group Management Fee for March 31 at the time of the legislative session. This reflects the median management fee of
the peer group over the last twelve months. As the target rates are not reset, there will be a natural time disconnect between the actuals and target rates. Standard reporting to the OIC and legislature
uses consistent time periods, so any non-KPM reporting would show the current actual expenses compared to the peer group median rate for the same time period.
The goal of KPM #2A is to provide investment and banking services that are cost-efficient compared to external sources. The Oregon Short Term Fund consistently provides low-cost investment and
cash management services to state agencies and local governments. As of June 30, 2021, OSTF had an annual management fee of 0.0522% compared to the Lipper Money Market Mutual Fund peer
group median of 0.15%.
Factors Affecting Results
actual target
The Oregon Short Term Fund is operated not-for-profit and as such provides services at a lower cost than external, for-profit commercial enterprises. As the fee for OSTF is set by statute and the
Lipper Money Market Mutual Fund peer group median remains relatively stable from year to year, we expect the fee differential to remain stable.

Treasury helps governments across the state, including schools and cities and counties, to stretch taxpayer dollars. Between the time that revenue is received and when the money is needed to pay
expenses, governments can deposit money and earn a rate of return by accessing the Treasury-managed Oregon Short Term Fund (OSTF) through the Local Government Investment Pool. Any
municipality, political subdivision, or public corporation of this state that by law is made the custodian of, or has control of, any public funds may participate in the pool. Sovereign Tribes are also
eligible to participate.
The purpose of KPM 3 is to monitor changes in the number of governments that choose to participate in the pool since local and tribal governments can choose among various statutorily authorized
investment options. Because the pool has been operating for several decades, and because the pool is used to distribute various taxes and revenues, most eligible governments participate.
Accordingly, Treasury’s target is to see no more than minimal change in the number of governments participating. The KPM is calculated as a year-over-year percentage change in the total number
of governments participating in the pool.
In 2021, we achieved our target of no more than minimal change with a 0.6% increase in the total number of governments participating in the pool.
Factors Affecting Results
actual target
Several factors impact governments’ investment decisions, including budget size, cash flow needs, moneys available for investment, financial goals and priorities, and risk tolerance. Participation in
the pool also can be impacted by governments’ satisfaction with Treasury services. Treasury’s strategy is to offer an attractive short-term investment option with a strong emphasis on customer service
and a competitive rate of return.

The 529 College Savings Program was established to assist Oregonians in their efforts to receive post-secondary education. The public policy goal of increasing attendance is met most significantly
not by account value, but by having an account. Students with an account are 25% more likely to attend college than those without. The KPM measures how well the program is doing in its efforts to
help eligible Oregonians have an account compared to national averages.
The College Savings Program consultant prepares a quarterly report for the 529 board that includes the reference data showing the total number of accounts divided by the total eligible population
compared to the national average. This is reported to the board as the “Account Penetration Rate”. Since the APPR report is due in October of each year, the prior calendar year end results are
reported.  As there is a national average reported to the board, it was determined that this provides the most effective benchmark. However, the data is reported after the period, so the most recent
calendar year national average would be used as the target for the next two periods. Historically Oregon has outperformed the national average, so to set a more aggressive target, the average out
performance of the last six years could be added to the most recent national average. For 2021-23 this would add 0.6% to the target.

We received almost $86 million in unclaimed property and returned about $30 million. Our refund rate was 35%, which is higher than the 33% we returned in FY 2020. Overall, we returned $4 million
more in FY2021, which is a 15% increase from FY2020. During FY2021, we impacted a record number of people by paying more than 37,000 claims. This represents a 42% increase over FY2020
and comes at a time when people need money more than ever.
FY2021 is the first year for this measure at OST, but data exist to show a longer trend. In the last 10 years, we returned $270 million to property owners. Our 10-year average return rate is 42%. FY
2019 has been our best year to date, with $33 million returned and a 48% rate of return.
The Unclaimed Property Professionals Organization surveys states on their unclaimed property rate of return the last survey covers the 2013-2017 period. During that time frame, Oregon ranked 18 of
51 states and had a return rate of 42%. Only three states returned more than 60% of receipts to their owners.
Factors Affecting Results
Our rate is below our 50% target for the following reasons:
Record receipts.
The $86 million we took in FY2021 is 9% higher than the amount we received in FY2020; that equates to $7 million in more unclaimed property received. Even though we returned
actual target
more money than in the previous year, the substantive increase in unclaimed property exceeded our claims approval capacity and overshadowed the claims gain of FY2021.
Low staffing.
For at least one quarter of the year, we were operating with 33% fewer resources, as one of our three claims examiners was on medical leave and did not return to work. The return
rate would have been closer to 40% had we operated at full capacity
Training.
When we hire new claims examiners to fill our vacancies, they undergo a lengthy training process that can last up to six months. During this period, new staff focus on claims of smaller
value and gradually approve larger claims as they become more experienced. This was the case in FY2021, as the team on-boarded a new person. That increased focus on lower-level work during
the on-boarding process brought down the return rate due to the claims the person was qualified to process.

The Unclaimed Property program aims to respond to claims within 120 days of receipt, as established in ORS 98.402. Currently, the response timeline is around 360 days, or three times the expected
rate.
This is the first year of this measure, but data exist to show a longer trend. The last time the program operated below the 120-day response target was in 2017. Since then, response times have
consistently increased every year. During FY2020, it averaged 300 days, and in FY2019, it took a little more than 200 days to respond to claims.
Factors Affecting Results
Our delayed response rate is due to the following reasons:
Record receipts.
The program has been receiving more unclaimed property every year. In the last five years, receipts have averaged a 10% yearly increase. Claims and receipts are directly
correlated; thus, the more unclaimed property we receive, the more claims are filed by property owners seeking to recover their assets.
Increased publicity.
Local and national news took a higher interest in unclaimed property during FY2021. There were several TV and newspaper stories promoting unclaimed property, which
resulted in an exponential number of claims being filed during FY2021. One effort the program took in October 2020 to proactively return funds to owners without the need to file a claim was widely
actual target
publicized; that media attention alone resulted in a 35% increase of new claims in about a month.
New unclaimed property system.
When Oregon implemented a new unclaimed property system in the fall of 2019, claimants received an enhanced customer service experience. One of the benefits
of our new system is the ability to file claims electronically and submit all necessary documentation with nothing more than a smartphone. This ease of filing resulted in a significant increase in claims.
Stagnant staffing levels.
The program has been operating with the same number of staff for about 10 years, despite claims more than doubling during that time. Multiple legislative requests for staff
were denied, until June 2021, when the program received an additional full time equivalent claims examiner. Until that position is filled, the program has five people responsible for 15,000 claims.
Increase in garnishments.
In addition to regular claims increasing, the program has also experienced an exponential growth in garnishment requests. Garnishments now account for 20% of claims
work. Because they are court-mandated and have a short response timeline, they leapfrog ahead of other claims. The more garnishments we receive, the longer regular claims wait for a response.

The goal of KPM 11 is to maintain a high level of customer satisfaction. Targets have been established at the 100% level in all categories. While this may not be possible, the target offers stretch goals
for the agency.
The survey associated with this KPM was previously only conducted in even years. Based on discussion with the legislature during the 2019 legislative session Ways and Means hearings, Treasury
was scheduled to utilize this customer service KPM to report on Oregon College Savings Plan (OCSP) customer satisfaction with the transition in 2018 from the previous plan administrator (TIAA-
CREF) to the new administrator (Sumday) in the 2020 APPR report.  However, due in part to the pandemic and other issues, the survey that served as the basis for the report was not completed until
after the 2020 APPR report was submitted, and accordingly Treasury is providing that report in this 2021 APPR, and will reset the KPM reporting period to odd years.
A customer service survey was sent out to a random selection of 2,000 OCSP participants in late 2020 to evaluate satisfaction with the plan.  OCSP received 191 responses. The percentages reported
in the table above are based on respondents rating services as ‘excellent’ or ‘good’ (and excluding those rating services as ‘don’t know’).
Most response categories were at or above the mid-80% range. One category received a surprisingly lower score of 64% for “Availability of Information,” and while there are no clear indicators why
respondents felt this way, OCSP staff will continue working with the plan provider to improve the availability of information in multiple formats and geared to a diverse population. The Oregon Treasury
Savings Network’s outreach team and communications director is currently establishing a comprehensive outreach and communication strategy to ensure we make this savings opportunity available
to all Oregonians.
Factors Affecting Results
