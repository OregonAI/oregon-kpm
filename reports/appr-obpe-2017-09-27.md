How Are We Doing
In 2017, 95% of supervisors and residents who responded to the survey rated the supervision process as "good" or "excellent" as effective preparation for competent and ethical
professionals. This is right at the Board's target of 95%, and is a one-point increase from the 94% result of the last survey administration in 2016.  Agency results on this metric have remained fairly
consistent.  The 3-year average survey result is 96%.
Factors Affecting Results
This measure is affected by the quantity and quality of available residency sites and supervisors in the state.  The Board continues to look for opportunities to improve the quality of the residency
experience for both residents and supervisors, regardless of the success of the program. In the past few years, the Board has made additional resources available on the website to educate
residents and supervisors on residency requirements and best practices.  NOTE: This survey was not administered 2012 through 2014; therefore, there is no data for the agency to report for these
years.

How Are We Doing
In 2017, 81% of licensee submitted continuing education reports met the requirements at first review.  This falls short of the Board's goal of 90%; however, it is a significant increase from the 43%
rate achieved in 2016.  The Board has consistently fallen below the target on this metric, with a four-year average of just 70%.
Factors Affecting Results
For various reasons, a number of licensees audited each period will not be found to not be in compliance with the continuing education requirements on first review. Some licensees will not fully
understand how to apply the criteria for what is acceptable education, the categorical limitations, what is acceptable documentation of completion, or how to fill out the forms. Most errors/omissions
are corrected immediately upon follow-up with the licensee.  The Board strives to respond quickly and completely to licensees' inquiries about the CE requirements, and to keep the website updated
with clear forms, extensive explanatory materials, and helpful FAQs.  With increased outreach to licensees about acceptable continuing education and reporting, the Board desires these numbers to
trend in a higher direction, reflecting licensees' understanding of the requirements. Additionally, the results will vary with the thoroughness of the reviewer.  Consistent with feedback from the Joint
Committee on Ways and Means Subcommittee on Education, the Board will be assessing the adequacy of this performance measure, and determining whether to edit or delete it for 2019-
21.  NOTE: In 2013 there was no continuing education audit completed because of the Board's transition to biennial birth month renewals in 2011.

How Are We Doing
The percent of uncontested case consumer complaint investigations completed within 6 months has remained relatively consistent over the past five years; however, it is below the target of 80%.
The Board has never met this target in the nine years it has been measured.  In 2017, the 66% of investigations were completed within the desired timeframe, which is a seven-point increase from
2016.
Factors Affecting Results
The Board has set an ambition goal to complete complaint investigations within six months; however, there are many factors affecting our ability to complete investigations. These include the need
for additional investigative staff, complexity of cases, traveling time, coordinating witness interviews, coordinating licensee and attorney schedules for interviews, waiting for necessary records to be
submitted, and emergency cases that take staff resources away from other cases.  The Board meets bimonthly, so timing is often a factor as well.  Also, Consistent with feedback from the Joint
Committee on Ways and Means Subcommittee on Education and the Legislative Fiscal Office, the Agency will be assessing the adequacy of this performance measure, and will likely edit it for 2019-
21 to reflect a more reasonable and achievable target to alignment the Board with its sister board, the Board of Licensed Professional Counselors and Therapists.
