How Are We Doing
Approximately 79 percent of Oregon homeowners are aware of the CCB. This finding is based on a scientific survey of 800 Oregon homeowners conducted in spring of 2025.  This is a 1-point
increase over the prior survey, and 29 points over the goal of 50%.
*The survey is conducted every two years. New survey results will be available in 2027.
Factors Affecting Results
Outreach efforts over the last five years have taken the form of webinars, press releases, mass emails, joint publications with other agencies, PSAs, radio and television interviews. These efforts have
been very successful, as the data shows. CCB continues to explore new ways to reach people, and has just implemented the agency's first "Home Improvement Month". During this time, the agency
hosted three consumer webinars on different topics. The agency also has been actively seeking new avenues to reach homeowners through agency partners that include other state and local
government agencies, stakeholders and contractors themselves. In previous years, the agency's primary outreach focus included home shows and in-person outreach. The agency now attends some
home shows in the spring, but home show attendance has declined somewhat in recent years. CCB will continue to perform the majority of its outreach activities through virtual platforms, partnerships
with stakeholders and media, as this allows the agency to reach wider audiences.

How Are We Doing
This measure reflects contractor behavior as well as the agency's performance in finding unlicensed work. The recidivism rate improved from the prior year, still falling slightly outside the target rate of
5% for this reporting period. It is worth noting that there is a high degree of variability on this particular measure, as the recent history demonstrates. More importantly, the underlying data actually show
that overall rates of compliance are improving, which is a positive trend. It does however cause the relatively small number of repeat violators to skew the percentage higher as part of a smaller pool of
unlicensed violators. This data is also a reflection of how effectively the agency positions the Field Investigative team across the state in order to target where unlicensed work occurs.
NOTE: As part of the 2024-2025 reporting period, CCB has conducted a review of current KPM measures and identified several KPMs for revision or replacement at the next opportunity in 2027. KPM
2 will likely see a proposed amendment to better align the measure prompt with the way the data has been historically collected and reported.
Factors Affecting Results
While this particular measure is outside the target, the underlying data provide reason to feel positive about CCB's overall enforcement effort. First, the total number of repeat violators cited during this
reporting period declined from 30 last period to 18 this period. More importantly, the total number of unlicensed violations for the past two years is an average of approximately 30% lower than the prior
three years. This is in line with CCB's general experience of finding higher rates of compliance across the many site checks performed each year. Several factors may have resulted in this increase.
The agency's focus on using site visits to educate and promote compliance as part of a comprehensive approach focused on compliance may contribute to this trend. Additional factors that may affect
this data include a shift in focus providing greater coverage in the more populated areas. This shift has increased checks in densely populated areas and may have increased success in identifying
individuals continuing to operate outside adopted standards.

How Are We Doing
The agency was well under the target maximum for FY25 (7/1/24-6/30/25), the agency achieved 26 percent.
NOTE: As part of the 2024-2025 reporting period, CCB has conducted a review of current KPM measures and identified several KPMs for revision or replacement at the next opportunity in 2027. KPM
3 will be proposed for elimination does not reflect any actual CCB staff performance or incorporate any behavior directly within the control of the CCB. It is largely a holdover from prior versions of the
agency's dispute resolution program and is not relevant to the current program.
Factors Affecting Results
The agency’s mediation service offers parties alternative ways to resolve disputes involving minimal cost to complainants. By doing so, contractors who may not be able to pay a large debt have the
opportunity to take care of the dispute in a way that preserves their license. Because of the 2011 changes to the Dispute Resolution Services, process complainants are more likely to settle disputes
through mediation without going to court.  Because a high percentage of the claimants that do go to court use the Small Claims Courts, the contractor’s bond is able to act as a backup when the
contractor can’t pay a judgement award.

How Are We Doing
The agency has gone beyond the target expectation by 13 days for FY 2025 (7/1/24-6/30/25).
NOTE: As part of the 2024-2025 reporting period, CCB has conducted a review of current KPM measures and identified several KPMs for revision or replacement at the next opportunity in 2027. KPM
5 retains some terminology and approach from prior iterations of the DRS system that are no longer relevant and includes significant amounts of time related to court proceedings that are outside our
control. However, this measure can be adjusted to be both relevant to current pattern and practice as well as a better reflection of the agency's actual performance on handling of complaints within our
jurisdiction. This approach will be proposed in our proposed amendments.
Factors Affecting Results
The current program offers mediation to the parties in an attempt to reconcile the dispute. If the parties cannot come to an agreement, the complaining party must file a complaint in court. If a judgment
is awarded, the complaining party may file the judgment with the agency seeking payment from the contractor’s bond. One factor that contributes to the timeliness of our services is the time it takes for
a court or arbitration to issue a ruling for parties that chose to file actions. This is not within the scope of the agency’s control; it adds to the time calculated for resolution. A significant additional factor
that negatively affected this reporting period was a bond audit process triggered by the identification of some invalid bonds. This problem added time to the processing as each bond had to be
confirmed by the licensing unit and the third-party bonding company prior to approving a complaint to move forward, both of which were out of the dispute unit's control. This issue has been largely
resolved and the agency anticipates this measure returning closer to target in the upcoming year. In addition, the proposed amendments to this measure should provide a more accurate perspective
on agency performance and aspects of the process within the agency's control.

How Are We Doing
The Licensing section consistently strives for high level of customer satisfaction and has established a very high target to reflect this goal. The agency’s performance in FY 2025 (7/1/24-6/30/25) fell
below the target for Licensing and Renewal Processing, while still reflecting a significant improvement over the previous FY2024. The implementation of the new electronic application program and
call center Callback feature has allowed the CCB to better serve new applicants by incorporating an electronic workflow and improving overall customer service to existing licensees by reducing call
wait times. The Licensing section continues to broaden the knowledge and skills of existing staff to better serve the customer base. With a focus on call center customer service, overall satisfaction
should increase for FY2026.  The agency continues to work toward establishing a strong online business culture.
NOTE: As part of the 2024-2025 reporting period, CCB has conducted a review of current KPM measures and identified several KPMs for revision or replacement at the next opportunity in 2027. KPM
7 and 8 have been identified for consolidation. The two separate measures reflect a period when there were two units being surveyed and reported. The work units are now consolidated, and it makes
sense to eliminate what is now an essentially redundant measure by eliminating KPM 7 and reporting KPM 8, which provides more granular level of detail about agency performance.
Factors Affecting Results
Staffing issues such as retirements, training replacements and several overlapping leave periods contributed to lower-than-normal satisfaction in the prior reporting period. Since then, the combination
of bringing new staff up to proficiency as well as deploying new electronic tools have improved the speed of processing for new applications and resulted in lower call-volume and thus lower phone
wait times. During FY2025 the agency also experienced a period of higher-than-normal new license applications creating some backlog. The agency continues to work on improving its website,
streamlining forms and processes, and providing ongoing staff training and outreach about contractor requirements, continuing education, and law and rule changes.
