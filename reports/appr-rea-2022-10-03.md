How Are We Doing
The Agency is consistently exceeding the target of property managers and principal brokers meeting compliance within 45 days of a compliance review. Licensees take advantage of the opportunity to
have their business practices evaluated by the Agency to ensure they are in compliance with regulated activities. In prior years the Agency had concerns that due to the high performance rate, the
program may not be producing the desired value and considered  replacing for another performance measure. However based on the positive feedback of participating licenses and observations by
lead staff, management sees opportunity in expanding this program in the next biennium.
The Agency has historically had a passive approach to promoting participation in this program but as part of the Agency's Strategic Plan, the Agency is dedicating more resources and a greater
attention to compliance in property management and client trust funds. The Agency views this program to be a distinct opportunity to provide early compliance feedback to new licensees proving those
licensed services. The Agency intends to mandate participation in this program in the coming biennium for licensees entering property management practice. We believe that this will serve as clarity
for licensees that have completed the theoretical knowledge application in their education and examination yet still require Agency guidence in the live business environment.
The Agency expects the compliance rate in 2023 to recede but has decided to maintain the target of 90% as a realistic long term goal.
Factors Affecting Results
As has been noted in previous biennia, the participation in this program is relatively low and thus typicaly results in very high performance. Further participation is entirely voluntary and largely invites
highly organized compliant licensees rather than licensees that would benfit from such a review.
The Agency seeks to move this to a mandatory review in the coming biennium to improve impact.

How Are We Doing
In June 2021, the Agency hired a new Regulation Division Manager, tasked at onboarding with analyzing existing enforcment and investigation practices, and building recommendations for process
improvement. This effort resulted in a series refinements adopted over the following year including the introduction of clear procedures and expectations within the case management process, time
blocked due dates for document intake and review, scope management and strategy sessions between lead workers and investigators at the outset of investigations, and increased information
sharing amongst the team with transparent team performance reporting and mandated status check-in meetings between management and invesigators, when investigations are nearing target
deadlines.
Factors Affecting Results
These efficiencies have resulted in tremendous results. The division has returneed to high performance which was disrupted in the prior biennium by a change in leadership and the limitations of the
pandemic.

How Are We Doing
The Agency continues to exceed target in the goal to settle 95% of contested case actions with a 98% result in the most recent reporting period.
Factors Affecting Results
The Agency approaches the disciplinary process with transparency, equity and consumer protection at the forefront. While not a requirement, the Agency has designed a settlement process for nearly
all sanctions. Proposed sanctions are sent to respondents along with the completed case investigation report and an invitation to participate in a settelement conference hosted by a lead worker in the
Regulation Division. Licensees are invited, along with representing counsel, to rebut any information presented in the investigation and provide mitigation circumstances that were otherwise not
accounted for in the Agency's investigation and proposed resolution.
To maintain consistency as well as equity in the sanctioning process, the Agency enters the settlement with a clearly stated position and intent. The respondent has several weeks to prepare and
provide any new information that was previously not known to the Agency so that the two parties may meet and discuss. The Agency aims distrupt inequitable outcomes and seeks to provide the most
fair and consistent offer from the oustet.
All respondents retain the right to refuse the Agency's offer and seek a hearing before an Administrative Law Judge. Though these changes in Agency methods and transparency in the process have
proven a noteable improvement in this KPM. Historically performance in this area fell below the goal and has now been consistent in the three years since implementing the above changes.

How Are We Doing
The Agency is currently meeting target for KPM #2, percent of licensees who rate the board-administered exam as "good" or "excellent" as an effective screen for competent and ethical professionals.
Factors Affecting Results
While the Agency is meeting target for this performance measure, many questionnaire respondents provided critical feedback. In real estate, most licensees view competency to follow some sort of
formal training program at their brokerage. Pre-licensing exams are not intended to teach sales and marketing. Moreover, “ethics” is often associated with “Code of Ethics” of the Realtor Association,
whereas Oregon statute only refers to “duties and responsibilities” of a licensee.
Last reporting period, the Agency hosted two work groups comprised of staff and industry leaders from the licensee community, to update the examination content. The updated examinations were put
into place in October 2020. As projected, the changes put in place have shown promise in the years following. The number of individuals choosing to take part in the survey has increased year to year.
Beginning in 2020, the Agency's exam vendor PSI began conducting online proctoring for real estate pre-license examinations as a measure to increase safety and reduce spread of COVID-19 as
well as provide greater equity for applicants that have lesser availability to travel to an examination site. The Agency has received generally positive feedback about this change.
The Agency will continue to closely monitor the comments associated with this KPM.

How Are We Doing
The Agency currently has achieved its highest performance ratings on record in this reporting period. Agency staff are very proud of this achievement and seek to continue to build on their reputation
of excellence.
Factors Affecting Results
The Agency strives to provide excellent customer service to licensees and the public.
This priority is documented in the Agency's Strategic Plan, echoed in all staff meetings and adopted as a
business culture norm within the Licensing & Education Division.
All applications are available through the Agency's eLicense system and conducted online. License applications are processed through automated workflows, based on business rules, in real time as
requirements are met and do not require staff intervention outside of the background review. Business applications are completed within three business days.
Most recent statistics show that the Agency handles approximately 1,900 phone calls per month with an average hold time of 58 seconds.  Licensing staff are available by phone from 8am - 5pm,
Monday through Friday, and the Agency has a fulltime in office Receptionist trained to answer licensing questions and assist individuals in person, at the Agency office in Salem, Oregon. Agency
email is responded to within a 24 hour period. The Agency responds to roughly 3,000 emails on average per month. Comments from the customer service survey are monitored on a monthly basis
and are overwhelmingly positive, as reflected in peformance statistics.

The Agency’s eLicense system is considered a foundational element of the Agency’s overall customer service. This system is reaching end of life and is now falling short of user expectations in
functionality. The Agency has officially initiated the eLicense Replacement System Project and is seeking funding in Policy Option Package 101. The Agency seeks to follow through on our
commitment to outstanding customer service and provide a technical platform that is intuitive to users, reduces the need for customer service support and shortens processing times for applications
and complaints. If funding is approved, the Agency intends to launch a new system in 2024.
