For the period between January 1, 2025, through October 10, 2025, 38% of cases have been resolved within 180 days. The processing time of cases has been longer than strived for by our
Professional Practices team. Their timeline for investigating cases remains dependent upon the availability and responsiveness of complainants, witnesses, and accused educators. The team
continues to prioritize cases impacting the safety of children.
Factors Affecting Results
The unpredictability of the sheer number of cases and the severity of cases is an important factor in this KPM. The time necessary for each investigation varies, depending on cooperation of
complainants, witnesses and the educators themselves. With a consistent history of greater than 50% of received complaints being dismissed by the agency, TSPC continues to evaluate how to best
communicate with the field regarding offenses that would lead to a sanction from the agency. In addition, TSPC is evaluating how to best ensure that complaints submitted are tied to an actionable
offense by statute and rule. By ensuring that cases reported to TSPC tie to statute and rule violations under the agency's authority and reducing the number of reported cases which do not, staff will
have more time available to investigate relevant misconduct reports.

How Are We Doing
For the past four consecutive years, the agency has successfully processed 100% of new program and major modification proposals for approval within 30 days.
The Educator Preparation & Pathways Unit is responsible for reviewing and processing Educator Preparation Program (EPP) proposals related to all Oregon educator licensure areas, including
teacher, principal, professional administrator, school counselor, school psychologist, and school social worker. All programs—whether offered by public or private, state-approved institutions—that
lead to Oregon licensure must receive approval from the Teacher Standards and Practices Commission (TSPC) prior to candidates completing their programs.
The approval process is subject to strict timelines. Proposals must follow the schedule below:
Submission:
At least 30 days before the Commission meeting
Program Approval Committee Review:
Recommendation for approval made 14 days prior to the Commission meeting
Commission Consideration:
Final approval considered at the Commission meeting
Factors Affecting Results
Though the agency has not considered an alternative KPM as anticipated, the changes required to meet the new early literacy education preparation standards will have an impact across all Oregon
Educator Preparation Programs. Staff anticipate an increase in the number of new program and major modification proposals and, thus, recommend this measure stay in place for the next biennium.
