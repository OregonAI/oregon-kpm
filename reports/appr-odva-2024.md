How Are We Doing
This metric measures the percentage of the Department’s home loans (in units) that are 90 or more days delinquent or in foreclosure. For fiscal year 2024, the Department successfully achieved better
results than the Key Performance Measure target. This figure remains better than the adjusted KPM metric reset last year.
Factors Affecting Results
Oregon’s economy remained strong through the fiscal year, with home values increasing. This contributed to a low percentage of mortgage delinquency, especially when compared to the Federal VA
loan program delinquency in Oregon. This figure includes 2 loans that are farm loans with annual payments, which will report delinquency until the annual payment is made. ODVA underwriting
remains diligent in our lending practices, and our experienced collection coordinator makes contact early should any borrower show difficulties in meeting their payment obligations. Further, ODVA has
not experienced any foreclosure activity that resulted in a State-Owned Property (SOP) during the past 6 years.

How Are We Doing
The Oregon Veterans' Homes averaged 79% occupancy for the fiscal year 2024. Individually, the Veterans' Home in The Dalles averaged 65% occupancy and the Lebanon Veterans' Home averaged
93% occupancy.  The Veterans’ Homes have higher occupancy rates than private skilled nursing facilities (SNF) in Oregon which averaged a 59 percent occupancy rate in 2023.
Factors Affecting Results
The Veterans’ Homes are limited to admitting only eligible veterans, spouses, and parents that medically require skilled-nursing care. Both Veterans’ Homes provide high-quality care that honors
veterans service and is sensitive to military culture and aging issues.  Both Veterans’ Homes also have endorsed memory communities, which is very rare for nursing homes in Oregon.
Average occupancy levels at the Oregon Veterans' Homes decreased during the pandemic and The Dalles Home is still recovering from this. Other challenges impacting census at the Oregon
Veterans' Home in The Dalles include lack of provider resources, location, lack of provider agreements with Medicare Advantage plan providers, and to some extent staffing challenges.  The largest
factor affecting admissions to The Dalles Home is the Homes inability to serve veterans with behavioral and mental health needs.  This is due in part to the specialized training needed by staff to care
for these veterans and lack of payor sources as well as facility adaptations required for behavioral health residents.

How Are We Doing
The two Oregon Veteran Homes have historically offered, and will continue to offer, private pay rates for veterans below the median rates charged by private skilled nursing facilities in Oregon. ODVA
wants to ensure all veterans who require skilled nursing care have access to the high quality, veteran specific care offered in our two Homes.  We accomplish this by offering private pay rates that are
lower than those charged by private nursing facilities across Oregon.  In addition, veterans who are rated at 70 percent service connected or more and in need of skilled nursing care are eligible to
have the entire cost of care at the Veterans Homes paid for by USDVA. Both Homes are also Medicare/Medicaid certified facilities and accept payment from long-term care insurance as well.
Factors Affecting Results
The factors influencing the veteran private pay rates generally include traditional cost drivers in the long-term industry including the needs of the veteran requiring care and the critical costs of
recruiting, training, and maintaining a qualified nursing workforce who provide care to residents in the Veterans' Homes. Over the past two years, direct care nursing costs have increased an average
of 9.37 percent at the Homes.  Reimbursement rates from the USDVA and Medicaid have increased, although these increases have not been sufficient to offset the increased cost of care and
operations.  Due to increased staffing costs and general costs of operation, private pay rates will continue to increase to help offset a portion of the additional costs of care.

How Are We Doing
The Oregon Department of Veterans’ Affairs (ODVA) strives to provide the highest quality of claims representation to Oregon’s veterans. ODVA is responsible for accrediting and training County,
Tribal, and State Veterans Service Officers to ensure veterans and families are served by skilled, certified professionals. These Veteran Service Officers are assisted by a strong ODVA Appeals team
which represent veterans and families in USDVA Board Hearings, and Virtual Hearings. This performance measure compares the average annual Veterans Benefits Administration (VBA) Disability
Compensation and Improved Pension benefit payment made to an individual veteran in Oregon (actual) to the national average (target).
For Federal Fiscal Year 2023 Oregon’s average Disability and Improved Pension compensation amount is approximately 6.6% less than the national average. Note: FFY24 actual data is provided by
the US Department of Veterans Affairs and is not available until mid-calendar year 2025 and the target will adjust accordingly.
Factors Affecting Results
The factors influencing the dollar amount of a disability compensation and pension payment can include the quality of representation (reflected in the knowledge and expertise of the Veterans Service
Officer and board hearings officer) and the type of claim (Disability Compensation or Improved Pension) presented to the VBA. Other factors include the age and overall health of veterans. For
instance, older veterans may receive an Improved Pension benefit, which is designed to support them by augmenting fixed incomes burdened by exceptionally high out of pocket medical costs such
as assisted living facilities, or to prevent homelessness for those veterans with an extremely low income. Additionally, this KPM measures all veterans in Oregon, not just veterans who are represented
by ODVA. Thus, a veteran who chose to file claims on their own may not be aware of the full scope of benefits available to them.

How Are We Doing
Partnerships between the Oregon Department of Veterans’ Affairs (ODVA), Tribes, and Counties providing services to veterans and families have never been stronger. Coordinated efforts to reach
and engage with veterans from all walks of life and historically underserved populations continue to grow. The number of new Powers of Attorney (POA)/Appointments of Representation provided by
veterans to ODVA, Tribes, and Counties is generally a reflection of new claims being filed on behalf of veterans and their family members. For fiscal year 2024, there was an increase in the total
number of new POAs from the prior year and has been rising since the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic seen in 2021.
The impact of the COVID-19 pandemic cannot be overstated for this KPM. The traditional methods of reaching underserved, houseless, or under resourced veterans and families through outreach
and events known as “Standdowns” were largely cancelled or postponed due to social distancing requirements. Thankfully these have now largely returned to in-person at the same rate and frequency
as seen pre-pandemic.
Agency efforts to tackle challenges facing historically minoritized or have come from the hiring of specialized coordinators such as the Houseless Veteran Coordinator, Women Veteran Coordinator,
LGBTQ+ Veteran Coordinator, and Tribal Veteran Coordinator. Additionally, ODVA has now entered into MOUs with four of the nine federally recognized tribes in Oregon to provide training and
accredited claim representation from Tribal Veterans Service Officers.
Factors Affecting Results
The primary factor affecting recent results was a 2015 directive from the USDVA mandating that all historical POA’s older than five years old be renewed. These POA renewals resulted in substantial
increases to the total number of POAs filed between 2015-2016 as established clients had to be refreshed. Starting in 2017 and continuing into 2020, the number of new POA filings began to average
to totals typically seen prior to 2015. Due to the leveling off of POA’s, after the required refresh from USDVA, the Oregon state legislature adopted new targets for this KPM beginning in FY22. In
FY23 and FY24 the new target goals were exceeded.
Despite a decrease in the overall population of veterans as Oregon’s veteran population age, outreach efforts to underserved veteran’s population has improved. For example, over 1,000 new claims
submitted in FY24 for houseless, elderly, and extremely low-income veterans as well as surviving spouses. Additionally, there were almost 14,000 new claims and supplemental claims filed to assist
veterans and claimants in ensuring that they are receiving the maximum benefit they are entitled, at the earliest possible effective date.
Note: Beginning in March 2020, County and Tribal Veteran Service Offices, and ODVA experienced an immediate decrease in monthly POA’s obtained. The noticeable decline in new POA’s has been
attributed to the reduced ability for in-person meetings and more limited outreach activities due to the COVID-19 Pandemic. These decreases continued through the completion of FY21 but has since
shown signs of rebounding in FY23 and FY24.

How Are We Doing
In FY 2024, we saw 92% of veterans who exited OHCS funded programs from homelessness into permanent housing, maintained their housing for six months or longer.
Factors Affecting Results
The KPM is only a limited snapshot of the services provided to Veterans and their families. OHCS housing and shelter programs provided services such as emergency shelter, rapid rehousing, case
management and more to Veterans throughout the state.
The denominator for determining this metric is limited, due to OHCS only receiving homeless data on programs which OHCS funds.
We do not receive data for the entire homeless response system, so it is very possible that veterans who exited homelessness into housing, funded by OHCS, may have fallen back into homelessness
but did not access state funded services, thus not being represented in data available to OHCS to calculate the measure. Additionally, due to the Covid-19 pandemic and the influx of emergency
services, a six month follow up is no longer a required part of OHCS funded homeless services and can no longer be relied on to capture this measure.
OHCS is working in partnership with the CoC’s across the state of Oregon to establish data sharing agreements to understand the entire homeless response system, the pathways in and out of
homelessness, and to capture a better picture of retention of housing.
