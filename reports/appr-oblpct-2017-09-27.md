How Are We Doing
The percent of complaints presented to the Board within 90 days has remained consistently well below the target.  In 2017, the 29% of investigations were completed within the desired timeframe,
which is an eight-point improvement from 2016 (21%), and a ten-point improvement from 2015 (19%).
Factors Affecting Results
The Board's target is very ambitious; Oregon law allows 120 days from the time the complaint is received until presentation of the investigation report to the Board.  There are many factors affecting
our ability to present investigations within 90 days, including the need for additional investigative staff, complexity of cases, traveling time, coordinating witness interviews, coordinating licensee and
attorney schedules for interviews, waiting for necessary records to be submitted, and emergency cases that take staff resources away from other cases.  The Board meets bimonthly, so timing is
often a factor.  Also, the Board has seen significant increases in the number of license applications and licensees; the volume of investigations has increased by an average of 21% per year over
the past five years.  Also, Consistent with feedback from the Joint Committee on Ways and Means Subcommittee on Education and the Legislative Fiscal Office, the Board will be assessing the
adequacy of this performance measure, and will likely edit it for 2019-21 to reflect a more reasonable and achievable target in alignment with its sister board, the Board of Psychologist Examiners.

Factors Affecting Results
In 2015-2016, the Board experienced significant staff and management turnover, not very long before the 2016 survey was sent out to stakeholders.  Staffing shortages created some backlog in
Board delivered services, and surely affected the survey results. In 2016-2017 the Board has hired additional staff, is under new management, and has reorganized under Mental Health Regulatory
Agency with a prioritized refocus on customer service.  This is reflected in the 2017 survey results.
Generally, survey results are affected are some stakeholders who have experienced an adverse enforcement action, or do not agree with laws or rules that the Board is charged with enforcing or
the policy direction of the Board.  These individuals will often respond "poor" to each satisfaction area, regardless of their experience with agency staff.

How Are We Doing
The Board achieved a 99% score in 2017, which is an 11 point improvement from 2016's score of 88%, and a 14 point improvement from 2015's score of 85% This is one point below below the
target of 100%.
Factors Affecting Results
Board management continues to look for opportunities for improved performance and increased transparency, regardless of the Board members' reports of success. Board staff sends materials
with this survey to help clarify the survey questions and explain how they directly relate to agency operations; however, members report on their perceptions of best practices which could reflect
aspirational rather than actual performance levels. The survey can fall below the target of 100% if just one Board member indicates that any one of the 15 best practice measures are not being
met.  In this case, a Board member responded "no" to the best practice that Board members identify and attend appropriate training sessions, lowering the score by one point. NOTE: This survey
was not administered 2014; therefore, there is no data for the agency to report for this year.
