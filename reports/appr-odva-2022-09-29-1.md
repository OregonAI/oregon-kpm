How Are We Doing
This metric measures the percentage of the Department’s home loans (in units) that are 90 or more-day delinquent or in foreclosure. For the fiscal year 2022, the Department successfully achieved
better results than the Key Performance Indicators.
Factors Affecting Results
The Department has maintained a historically lower percentage of loan accounts that were great than 90-days or more delinquent as compared to other lenders. This lower percentage of seriously
delinquent accounts is a factor of prudent loan underwriting and proactive management with homeowners experiencing payment difficulties. Even during a time when much of the market had
borrowers working through forbearance issues, ODVA was able to minimize the negative effects brought by the COVID-19 pandemic. Further, ODVA did not experience any foreclosure action during
this fiscal year.

How Are We Doing
The two Oregon Veterans' Homes averaged 76% occupancy for the fiscal year 2022. Individually, the Veterans' Home in The Dalles averaged 65% occupancy and the Lebanon Veterans' Home
averaged 86% occupancy.
Factors Affecting Results
Homes have been significantly higher than private SNFs. Average occupancy levels at the Oregon Veterans' Homes have decreased in 2021 and 2022 due to the COVID-19 pandemic.
Factors impacting census during the past year at the Oregon Veterans’ Homes include COVID-19 in the Veterans' Homes, suspension of admissions of new residents at the Veterans'
Homes, and staffing challenges.
The Veterans' Homes are limited to admitting only eligible veterans, spouses, and parents that medically require skilled-nursing care. Both Veterans' Homes provide high-quality care that honors
veteran service and is sensitive to military culture and aging issues. Both Veterans' Homes also have endorsed memory care communities, which is very rare for nursing homes in Oregon.

How Are We Doing
The two Oregon Veteran Homes have historically, and will continue to, offer private pay rates for veterans that are below the median charged by Oregon skilled nursing facilities. By offering this
reduced rate, veterans who require skilled nursing care can have the same access to veteran-specific care in our two Homes at a rate lower than the private market in civilian nursing facilities
across Oregon.
Factors Affecting Results
The factors influencing the veteran private pay rates generally include traditional cost drivers in the long-term industry including the needs of the veteran requiring care and reimbursement from the
Federal VA/Medicare/Medicaid sources. Inherent in these factors are the critical costs of recruiting, training and maintaining a qualified nursing workforce who provide care to residents in the
Veterans’ Homes. Due to the COVID-19 Pandemic and increased staffing costs, private pay rates will continue to increase to cover the additional costs of care.

How Are We Doing
The Oregon Department of Veterans’ Affairs (ODVA) strives to provide the highest quality of claims representation to Oregon’s veterans and their eligible dependents.  ODVA is responsible for training
County, Tribal, and State Veterans Service Officers to ensure veterans and families are served by skilled, accredited professionals.  These Veterans Service Officers are assisted by a strong ODVA
Appeals team which represents veterans and families in U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs Board in-person and virtual hearings.
This performance measure compares the average annual Oregon disability compensation and pension benefit payments from the USDVA, made to an individual Oregon veteran (actual) to the
national average (target).  The initial Federal Fiscal Year (FFY) 2021 target of $17,318 was established based on the historical average rate of growth. The target for 2021 has been adjusted to
$18,636.63 to reflect the actual national average for 2021. Oregon’s average disability and pension compensation amount is approximately 5.7% less than the national average.
In addition to monthly disability and pension compensation paid to Oregon veterans in FFY 2021, USDVA expenditures included $1.58 billion in healthcare, and $133 million in education payments,
bringing total expenditures in Oregon to $3.46 billion.
Actual 2022 data is anticipated to be released by the USDVA in July 2023.
Factors Affecting Results
The factors influencing the dollar amount of disability compensation and pension payment include the number of claims being filed and the type of claim (disability compensation or pension) presented
to the USDVA.  Other factors include the age and overall health of veterans, as well as conditions related to presumptive illnesses such as Agent Orange exposure for Vietnam veterans and Blue
Water Navy Sailors.  For instance, older veterans may receive a pension benefit, which is designed to support them by augmenting fixed incomes burdened by exceptionally high out-of-pocket medical
costs such as assisted living facilities or to prevent homelessness for those veterans with an extremely low income.
For FFY 2021, the number of veterans receiving USDVA service-connected disability benefits continued to climb, and the average amount for receiving compensation also grew. Note: This number
includes all veterans in Oregon who are receiving compensation or pension benefits from the USDVA. It is not exclusively veterans who are represented by ODVA through the County, Tribal, and
State Veterans Service Officer network.

How Are We Doing
Partnerships between the Oregon Department of Veterans’ Affairs (ODVA), Tribes, and Counties in providing services to veterans and families remained very strong throughout the COVID-19
pandemic. Coordinated efforts to reach and engage with veterans from all walks of life and historically underserved populations continue to grow. The number of new Powers of Attorney
(POA)/Appointments of Representation provided by veterans to ODVA, Tribes, and Counties is generally a reflection of new claims being filed on behalf of veterans and their family members, as well
as advocacy on their behalf. For the state fiscal year 2022, there was an increase in the total number of new POAs from the prior year.
Although the number has increased from the previous year, the COVID-19 pandemic significantly impacted this KPM. The traditional methods of reaching underserved, houseless, or under-resourced
veterans and families through outreach and events known as “veteran stand downs” have just begun to resume.
Further, the shift to online advocacy meant that communities without stable or solid internet were highly impacted. Agency efforts to tackle these challenges have come from the hiring of specialized
coordinators such as the ODVA Houseless Veteran Coordinator, the Women Veteran Coordinator, and the LGBTQ+ Veteran Coordinator. Additionally, ODVA maintains a Memorandum of
Understandings with three of Oregon’s nine federally recognized tribes to provide training, support, and accredited claim representation from Tribal Veteran Service Officers.
Factors Affecting Results
The primary factor affecting recent results was a 2015 directive from the USDVA mandating that all historical Power of Attorney (POA) older than five years be renewed. These POA renewals resulted
in substantial increases in the total number of POAs filed between 2015-2016 as established clients had to be refreshed. Starting in 2017 and continuing into 2022, the number of new POA filings
began to average to totals typically seen prior to 2015. Due to the leveling off of the number of POAs, after the required refresh from USDVA, the Oregon Legislature adopted new targets for this KPM
beginning in FY22. In FY22 the new target goal was 7,100 POAs, and this number was exceeded.
In the past several years, outreach efforts to the underserved veteran population continuously improved. State, tribal, and county-level advocacy for veterans and their families include targeted support
for women veterans, tribal veterans, houseless veterans, student veterans, incarcerated veterans, and veterans who are a part of the LGBTQ+ community. These efforts have led to an increase in
veterans and their families seeking and obtaining benefits through ODVA representation.
Note: Beginning in March 2020, County and Tribal Veteran Service Offices, and ODVA experienced an immediate decrease in monthly POAs obtained. The noticeable decline in new POAs is
attributed to the reduced ability for in-person meetings and more limited outreach activities due to the COVID-19 Pandemic. This decrease continued through the completion of FY21 but saw an
increase in FY22 when in-person restrictions were lifted.
