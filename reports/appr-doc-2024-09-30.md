How Are We Doing
The Department did not meet the target for this reporting period. The percentage of Ballot Measure 17 compliant adults in custody (AICs) dropped from 71% to 69% in 2023-2024. Although not
meeting the full 40-hour requirement for compliance, a majority of AICs have engaged in some type of work/education activity. It remains difficult to reach the target for a variety of reasons
compounded by staffing limitations. To work on an outside work crew, AICs must be classified as minimum custody and meet strict criteria to ensure public safety. The Department must also find a
balance in prioritizing case management to support work opportunities, treatment, and education – all of which can be part of an AIC’s corrections plan. Oregon Corrections Enterprises (OCE) works in
partnership with the Department of Corrections by providing work opportunities that, as much as possible, mirror real world employment experiences.
Factors Affecting Results
Institution maintenance, janitorial, kitchen help, call centers, laundry, and garment, metal, and wood manufacturing are examples of work counting toward the 40-hour requirement. Qualified programs
like education and treatment for alcohol and drug addictions take priority over work program assignments and provider availability creates conflicts with scheduling. The average AIC population
remained relatively stable, while the number of exempt AICs increased slightly and the number of compliant AICs reduced slightly. The number of AIC jobs needed to support institution operations
remained relatively unchanged. Limited funding for programs and staff support will not allow us to reach all those in need. Outside work crews are limited by the amount of funding public entities have
available to finance those activities, coupled with continued restrictions barring sex offenders from outside work crews reducing the number of AIC available for these crews. OCE continues to work on
the expansion of relevant work opportunities offered through public and private partnerships. Similar to the previous reporting period, over 2,200 AICs completed more than 120 hours of OCE training,
with approximately 1,350 AICs participating in OCE programs at any given time. While the Department and OCE add positions every year, fluctuation in the AIC population across institutions inhibits
the Department’s ability to increase the percentage assigned to qualifying assignments.

How Are We Doing
After a steady increase in recidivism, the last couple of years have shown a steady decline. Given the multitude of factors that impact re-arrest, convictions and incarcerations, it is difficult to pinpoint
what may be contributing to the sudden drop.  Regardless, the measure is headed in the right direction and showing better results.
Factors Affecting Results
The Department and county community corrections agencies put considerable effort into assessing AICs and developing a comprehensive corrections plan that will address identified risks and needs.
This includes use of the Level of Service/Case Management Inventory (LS/CMI), a measure of risk and need factors, as well as a fully functional case management tool.   Additionally, the department
and county community corrections agencies utilize the Women’s Risk Need Assessment (WRNA) tool which targets identification of risks and needs correlated with the female population.  Ongoing
system improvements have been made to better target in-prison interventions and to conduct more careful and coordinated release planning. In addition, community corrections agencies statewide
continue to work collaboratively with the department to enhance the effective and efficient transition of AICs from prison to our communities. Such efforts are centered on a commitment to implement
and enhance evidence-based practices throughout Oregon’s criminal justice system.

How Are We Doing
This KPM reflects the performance of two varieties of DOC transitional leave programs: Non-Prison Leave associated with the Alternative Incarceration Program (AIP), and Short-Term Transitional
Leave (STTL).
The Oregon Department of Corrections offers AIP to inmates who have been sentenced by a judge as eligible for AIP, and who apply to participate and meet other statutory and department policy
requirements to participate. The programs are based on intensive interventions targeted at criminogenic risks, rigorous personal responsibility and accountability, physical labor, and service to the
community. Additionally, AIP consists of an institutional phase for a minimum of 180 days, followed by a period of non-prison leave of up to 90 days, for a total minimum program length of 270 days.
During the institutional phase, these programs provide 14 hours of structured and regimented routine every day for a minimum of 180 days. Adults in Custody (AIC) who are eligible to participate in
AIP are screened through the multidisciplinary Treatment Assignment Screening Committee to determine the most appropriate program to meet the AICs’ needs.
The Department also offers STTL of up to 120 days to AICs who are not enrolled in an Alternative Incarceration Program. As with AIP Non-Prison Leave, AICs must meet the statutory and department
policy requirements to be approved for Short-Term Transitional Leave.
Regardless of whether Non-Prison Leave or Short-Term Transitional Leave, participants are closely monitored for compliance with their transition plans while in the community. The higher the
percentage of successful completions, the greater the success.
For the 2023-2024 reporting period, the transitional leave completion rate for STTL decreased slightly (82.54%) when compared with the 2022-2023 reporting period (83.9%). The completion rate for
AIP Non-Prison Leave during 2023-2024 decreased (89%) when compared with the completion rate during the 2022-2023 reporting period (92.2%).
Short-Term Transitional Leave participation rates for this reporting period increased from an average of 91.6 per month in 2022-2023, to an average of 103.6 per month, representing approximately
one fifth of the monthly releasing population. A significant reason for the increase in participation back to pre-covid levels was the impact of COVID ending and housing becoming more available. A
consistent barrier to increasing AIP transitional leave participation, is limited treatment program bed space, which must be used for both AIP and non-AIP participants.
DOC continues to review, refine, and streamline its internal processes to ensure it is targeting those most appropriate for STTL. This includes development of automation designed to further increase
both efficiency and effectiveness of the process, as well as substantially improve both the quality and quantity of data collected and analyzed. Phase I of automation was completed in 2017 and Phase
II, which was designed to greatly enhance efficiency and the ability for all stakeholders to track individual offender history and current STTL status, rolled out September 2019. Phase III, aimed to
greatly improving data collection efficiency as well as quality and quantity of program data, was completed in April 2024. In addition to automation efforts, DOC continues its focus on developing solid
transition plans that will increase the successful reintegration of all AICs, into the community.
Factors Affecting Results
As stated above, overall transitional leave participant numbers have returned to pre-covid levels as a result less restrictions for private and county-based housing.  The decrease in successful
completion rates during the reporting period was minimal and difficult to report to one factor.

How Are We Doing
For this reporting period, the Office of Inspector General conducted eleven full Use of Force Reviews.  Of those, four were deemed to include excessive use of force.
Factors Affecting Results
All uses of force are reviewed by several layers of management and administration.  The Officer in Charge conducts the first review, which then proceeds to the Assistant Superintendent of Security at
that facility, and then to the Superintendent.  From there, the reviews move to a state-wide review process beginning with the Eastside- or Westside-Administrator.  Based on the Administrator's
determination, the review
may
then proceed to the Office of Inspector General for a full review.  These many layers, including some that are independent of the facility itself, help ensure that
deficiencies are noticed and remedied quickly.
