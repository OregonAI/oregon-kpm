How Are We Doing

How Are We Doing
The division either met or exceeded its targets in all three "phases" of this composite KPM. Demand letters to employers were sent in less than 12 days for 100% of the claims received in FY 2015-
2016 (Phase 1). Likewise, the division assigned 100% of wage claims for investigation with 30 days (Phase II). The division met its goal of completing one-half of its wage claim investigations within
35 days (Phase III) by completing 49.6% of its investigations within this time period.
Factors Affecting Results
The single most critical factor in the division's wage claim performance is the availability of trained staff to process and investigate wage claims. In order to resolve wage claims efficiently and
expediently, the division requires adequate compliance resources to retain compliance staff and maintain service levels. Over the last few years, the division has received an average of 1,600 wage
claims per year and has recovered an average of $1.7 million in back wages per year.

How Are We Doing
While the scope and complexity of prevailing wage investigations makes it difficult for the division to meets its goal of completing 64% of such cases within 90 days, more than half of the

How Are We Doing
The division failed to reach its target of 1,200 new journey workers in FY 2016.
Factors Affecting Results
Between 2004 and 2014, an average of approximately 1,200 apprentices received their journey level certificate each year.  Beginning in November 2008, total apprenticeship registrations gradually
fell as the Great Recession eroded employment opportunities to a greater degree than initially anticipated.  Accordingly, new apprentice registrations remained stagnant until approximately July
2013, when a rebounding economy resulted in an increased demand for registered apprentices.
The low number of new apprentices registered between 2009 and 2013 and the lack of work available for active apprentices resulted in fewer program completions than previously targeted by
ATD.  ATD anticipated that the total number of journey level certificates awarded would begin to increase in 2016-17, reflecting the increasing number of new registration from July 2013 to date.
In FY 2015, apprentice completion reached their lowest point in 16 years with 860 individuals receiving journey level certificates.  Completions increased to 973 in FY 2016 showing a gradual
increase, as anticipated, resulting from the gradual increase in the number of new registrations staring in 2013 as the economy recovered, and spurring more employment.  Given the increase in
new registrations over the past 30 months and the increasing availability of work hours for apprentices, ATD expects another incremental increase  in the number of apprentices receiving journey
level certificates in FY 2017,  but does not believe that completions will reach the target level of 1,2000 individuals receiving journey certificates until 2017, when  a substantial number of
apprentices who registered with programs during the economic recovery compete or should be close to completing their programs.

Factors Affecting Results
The economic acceleration the state experienced in 2013 has continued to gradually build and shows no signs of deterioration in the short term. Statewide job growth is currently at the strongest

their programs. ATD hopes to offset any deterioration in construction industry registrations with an increase in new apprentices from the manufacturing sector resulting from its efforts in
collaboration with the Employment Department and the broader state workforce system.
