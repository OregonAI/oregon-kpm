How Are We Doing
The FY 2021 average licensing time statewide jumped up to 105 days. This is well above the legislatively set target of 75 and 20 days higher than the fiscal year 2020 average of 85 days. The
commission continues to adapt in order provide prompt service to alcohol licensees.
Factors Affecting Results
There are many factors affecting the number of days it takes to issue a liquor license; some internal and some external.
Internal factors continue to be identified and streamlined through process improvements and technological solutions. The COVID-19 emergency caused the OLCC to pivot licensing rules and allow for
accelerated applications for off-premises licenses for bars and restaurants, license investigators were tasked with helping licensees adapt to new rules and public health guidelines.
Additionally, several alcohol license investigators were re-assigned to help with processing the backlog of marijuana license renewal investigations.  Monthly break-downs of days to license show
substantial slowing in process times beginning in March 2020 with process times returning to historical averages by June 2021.
External factors are difficult to control. A primary external factor affecting how quickly a liquor license can be issued is the license application review by the local governing body (city or county). Statute
gives local governments up to 90 days (45 days plus an additional 45 day extension – if requested) to review a license application within their jurisdiction and provide a recommendation (positive,
negative, or neutral). The OLCC cannot complete the processing of an application until the local government review is completed. Lengthy application review by local governments usually occurs in
the larger metropolitan areas, such as Portland. These areas also have higher numbers of license applications, in absolute terms, which influence the overall statewide average licensing times.
Additionally, the timeliness of the applicant in providing materials necessary to the application investigation can impact overall processing time. Applicants not prepared for or committed to the process
may have longer processing times.  A 2011 analysis found that staff processing time totaled to an average of only 32.9 days per license; only 36% of the total time to issue a license. The remaining
64% of the time was driven be external factors described above and are outside the control of the agency.
The 2021 results of this measure indicate that the OLCC is above the current target set by the legislature and is continuing to implement staffing and business process strategies for resuming
improvement.  The OLCC is pursuing other solutions to its business needs that include regulatory innovations, the implementation of streamlining measures, and the development of an enterprise-
level licensing system that will unite many disconnected processes and increase the agency’s online service capacity
The license investigation process is largely manual, involving many phone calls between investigators and applicants in order to guide them to their license.  In addition, financial investigations of
liquor licenses can also become labor intensive for complex business structures. The agency has budgeted more alcohol license investigator positions to increase staff dedicated to liquor license
investigations and providing more timely service to our industry partners.

How Are We Doing
The overall agency rating was 64 percent (rated as good or excellent) compared to 74 percent in 2020. The OLCC missed the 85 percent target in all five areas when averages were taken from the six
stakeholder groups. However, the OLCC met or exceeded the 85 percent average target for surveys from alcohol licensees with 87 percent excellent or good service.
Liquor agents indicated a frustration with the availabillity of products due to warehouse staffing issues and the changing information and gave the commission an average positive score of 81%- four
oints lower than last year.
Marijuana licensees provided an evaluation of 69 percent excellent or very good service- driven by complaints of poor timeliness and difficulty in aquiring information.
The lower average scores were driven by the much less favorable responses from the general public compared to prior years, which may be the result of a very small group of motivated respondents.
Factors Affecting Results
The agency continues to make efforts to increase information availability through Gov Alerts and posting updates on the agency website. The Commission is endeavoring to improve licensing times for
marijuana licensees by hiring more staff.
The lower average scores were driven by the much less favorable responses from the general public compared to prior years, which may be the result of a very small group of motivated respondents.
Comments from our few respondents are driven by complaints about worker permit information or liquor agents "hoarding" limited release whiskeys.
There were a total of 446 respondents from the six survey stakeholder groups. There was a significant difference in responses between the general public, alcohol and marijuana licensees or agents.
Those with business before commission provided a much higher evaluation of the Commission than did the public survey or worker and service permittees.

How Are We Doing
The Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission distributed 2.61 dollars in revenue for every dollar the commission spent during fiscal year 2021.  The rate of return is five percent below the target of
2.70 dollar return per dollar spent. Distributable revenue went up about ten percent over 2020.
Factors Affecting Results
Agents compensation costs increased proportionally to the sales because it is a rate of sales. Liquor agent commissions increased last biennium from 8.93% of sales to 9.02% of sales and that would
cut into the ratio with higher costs.   Direct agency expenditures increased about $5.5 million per year which was 10% over the previous fiscal year due to increased investment into IT systems like the
privilage tax system.
Beer and wine tax collections and license fee collections were below projections due to COVID shut downs but more than made up for it in liquor sales.  O license refunds will be minimal I think and
those are occurring now and not last biennium.  So revenues went up 10% last year but so did costs.

How Are We Doing
The FY 2021 mean (average) marijuana licensing time statewide was 88 days for 676 qualifying licenses that were issued during the year; only 3 days above the target measure. This compares very
favorably to the 195 days for the 237 cases for which a new recreational marijuana licenses issued in FY 2020. The median time to license is 73 days indicating that there are a relatively small number
of cases that disproportionately raise the average length of licensing above the target. Additionally, licensees wait an average of 9 days to pay their license fee, so while the average time to license is
above the target, the majority of licensees are ready to pay before the target. The Commission continues to try to increase staff and to adapt in order to implement the recreational marijuana program
and provide prompt service to applicants.
Laboratory licenses took the shortest period to process, 16 days on average, while wholesalers were the longest at an average of 124 days.
Factors Affecting Results
There are many factors affecting the number of days it takes to issue a recreational marijuana license; some internal and some external. Internal factors continue to be identified as business
processes are developed to implement newer marijuana law and rules.
During FY 2021 the Commission redeployed some alcohol license investigators to help focus on marijuana licensing activities, as the marijuana program has experienced a large number of
complicated license sales in addition to working through the backlog of applications that had been submitted prior to June 15, 2018.
The Commission continues to hire licensing staff, implement process improvements and technological solutions (automation). The recreational marijuana program continued to fill a number of
vacancies in license investigator positions during fiscal year 2021.  The renewal process also saw a large proportion of applicants with complicated renewals containing site and/or ownership
changes.  Renewal applications, location changes or ownership changes of less than 50% are not included in this metric, but do represent a licensing burden.
External factors are difficult to control. A primary external factor affecting how quickly a recreational marijuana license can be issued is the license application review by the local governing body (city
or county). The issuance of Land Use Compatibility Statement by the local governing unit is the triggering event that allows the Oregon Liquor Control Commission to assign an investigator to begin
actively processing a marijuana application.
Applicants have also struggled to complete applications correctly and in a timely manner. The newness of the program and changes in law and rules guiding the application process have posed a
challenge for applicants who are navigating the process.  In order to help applicants, the Oregon Liquor Control Commission has produced a business guide for applicants and licensees, and
continues to provide education and information through the website, emails, and frequent communication with license investigators.
An analysis of some of the longer duration applications show applicants that fail to respond extended periods before resuming the application process.  The Commission has implemented a process of
side-tracking applications for applicants who are not actively engaged in the licensing process.
Management Comments
There are many factors affecting the number of days it takes to issue a marijuana license; some internal and some external. Internal factors continue to be identified and streamlined as business
processes are developed, staff hired and technological solutions incorporated. Primary external factors delaying licensing completion of land use compatibility statements, local permitting processes,
and the difficulty many licensees have in completing their license applications.
The license investigation process is largely manual despite the online application system. Applicants struggle to understand and implement regulatory requirements at a variety of levels of
government.  Complicated financial arrangements require investigators to follow-up multiple interested parties for each applicant.  These are time-consuming processes.
In the short-term, the agency does anticipates increasing the availability of additional resources dedicated marijuana license investigations as staffing for the marijuana program stabilizes.
However, license renewals also represent an ongoing workload challenge as licensees alter their businesses and change financial interests, requiring investigation of changes as part of the renewal
process.
The Oregon Liquor Control Commission has implemented a re-organization of licensing staff and business processes to optimize resources to handle the increased licensing responsibilities for both
programs.  Staffing gaps caused by implementing marijuana licensing and increased workload from recreational marijuana in addition to implementing software and business process changes,
combined with increased workloads for our local government partners, have all had an impact on increasing the time to license.
