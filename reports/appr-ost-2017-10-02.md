How Are We Doing
The goal of KPM 1 is to exceed the actuarial discount rate approved by the PERS Board, while applying the investment standards established in ORS 293.721 and 293.726. Net of fees, OPERF’s
average annual investment performance lagged its policy benchmark for the five-year period ended 12/31/16 (9.1% vs. 10.1%). OPERF’s average annual investment performance ranked in the top
quartile (i.e., top 25%) of all large public funds for the five-year period ended 12/31/16, per OIC consultant, Callan Associates.
Factors Affecting Results
Financial market conditions during the five-period ended 12/31/16 were constructive, with equity markets delivering strong performances in three of the five years. Low interest rates and extended
equity market valuations remain potential impediments to higher
ex ante
(i.e., future) return expectations.
The dominant factor influencing OPERF investment performance is prevailing economic and financial conditions as largely determined by monetary, fiscal and regulatory policies on both national
and international levels. Social, political and foreign policy matters also impact investment performance in the form of overall investment sentiment. In addition, the increasingly complex structure of
institutional-quality investment portfolios, particularly the growing deployment of private market investment strategies, has become a primary theme, both in terms of forward-looking, return-seeking
opportunities and also in terms of heightened risk management considerations and perennial resource and personnel constraints. Consistent with its fiduciary duty, the OIC has directed OST
investment staff to diversify OPERF among multiple asset classes, geographies and strategies, including a substantial and peer-leading commitment to private market and other alternative
investments. This deliberate and broad diversification is designed to maximize risk-adjusted, long-term investment returns, and OPERF’s historically strong performance record, which has
significantly reduced contribution obligations for Oregon taxpayers and businesses, is testament to the success of this approach.
Current economic and financial conditions are historically unique and present, relative to the OIC’s mission and fiduciary responsibilities, an unprecedented set of investment and governance
challenges. Global interest rates remain at or near historic lows, while U.S. public equity markets are trading at all-time highs. International public equity markets have recovered from an extended
period of relative underperformance, and stock markets in emerging countries have delivered sharp appreciation over the last twelve months in anticipation of more synchronized global economic
growth. However, liquid, high-quality investments still produce negligible or very low, single-digit returns which in turn continues to encouraged investors of all types to move further out on the risk
spectrum in search of the higher yields needed to meet those investors’ liabilities and cash flow obligations.

How Are We Doing
The goal of KPM 3 is to provide investment returns and banking services that are cost-efficient compared to external sources. The Oregon Investment Council (OIC) determines the OSTF
benchmark. Meeting or beating that benchmark, net of fees, is the fund's performance goal. In 2016, OSTF's investment performance exceeded its benchmark (rolling 91-day Treasury bill returns),
while the fund’s five-year annualized return outperformed that same benchmark by 0.79% for the period ended December 31, 2016.
Factors Affecting Results
Fund investments with original maturities greater than three months are marked-to-market on a monthly basis; therefore, interest rate and credit risks affect OSTF investment performance. Low
short-term interest rates continue to challenge the fund’s return potential. During 2016, the Federal Reserve’s Federal Open Market Committee increased the target Federal Funds rate by 0.25% at
its December 2016 meeting which moved its corresponding target rate to 0.50-0.75%.  This increase benefitted the Oregon Short Term Fund by providing incrementally higher front-end interest
rates enabling the fund to earn a greater return in 2016 than has been achievable in the past several years. According to Federal Open Market Committee member forecasts, continued increases
in the Federal Funds rate are expected in 2017 and 2018 which should increase payments to fund participants in the year ahead.

How Are We Doing
The goal of KPM 6 is to control costs relative to the procurement of banking services. Treasury’s strategy is to aggressively manage banking services contracts to keep costs low for our customers
while helping them identify and implement cost-effective cash management practices. Treasury’s target is to maintain an average cost per transaction that is no more than half (50 percent) of the
average retail cost paid by other customers of our primary banking services provider.  Statistics for this KPM are only updated in even years, and therefore the next update to the table above will be
in 2018.
Factors Affecting Results
This KPM compares the standard (or retail) pricing realized by most of our primary banking services provider’s commercial and government customers.  The State of Oregon continues to enjoy
significant pricing advantages. The savings highlight the significant savings achieved through central banking of state agencies and many public universities. A recent Treasury analysis indicates
that retail banking fees have increased by 4 percent over the last two years, while Treasury’s per transaction banking fees are at their lowest point in the last eight years.
As part of the Cash Management Improvement & Renewal Program (CMIRP), Treasury is in the midst of reevaluating our primary banking and cash management service delivery model and
partnerships. Our goal in this effort is to mitigate risk, and better support contingency planning and readiness, while striving to continue to provide low cost services to customers. As we pursue
changes in this area, impacts to cost per transaction may occur, including potential for some increase overall. Notwithstanding these potential impacts, Treasury expects to meet or beat the target
moving forward.

How Are We Doing
The goal of KPM 10 is to track and report state debt to the Governor and Legislature to assure that it remains at a sustainable levels, which helps maintain Oregon’s high state credit ratings and our
ability to achieve attractive long-term interest rates when borrowing for various capital projects and infrastructure programs.  The amount of state debt issued and outstanding remained fairly level
from 2013 through 2015, with new state debt being issued at roughly the same pace as existing debt was retired.  More recently, overall state debt levels have begun to rise, as the state’s economy
and revenues have improved sufficiently for the state to step up our capital project renewal and replacement efforts while remaining within prudent overall state debt limits.
Factors Affecting Results
State net tax-supported debt increased over the past decade due to the issuance of bonds to fund high-priority capital projects to fund new adult and juvenile correctional facilities; the replacement
of the state mental hospital; building upgrades and expansion for our public university, community college, and K-12 school systems; and congestion management projects and other transportation
improvements throughout Oregon.
