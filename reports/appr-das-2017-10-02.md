How Are We Doing
Actual General Fund revenue collections for the 2015-17 biennium came in above expectations, triggering both of the state’s unique kicker laws. Under Oregon’s kicker, errors larger than 2 percent
are returned to taxpayers (for personal income taxes) or retained in the General Fund for additional education spending (corporate income taxes.) In 2015-17, the corporate kicker amount totaled
$110,529,313, while the personal kicker (technically all revenues excluding corporate) totaled $463,526,040.
The most recent quarter general fund forecast amount-
$18,572,311,000
The close of legislative session 15-17 general fund forecast amount-
$17,998,055,960
Factors Affecting Results
The state’s volatile underlying economy and current tax structure yield larger year-to-year swings than in the typical state. While Oregon comes out ahead of the typical state in both an economic
and revenue sense over the entire business cycle, such variances are more challenging and difficult to accurately forecast.

How Are We Doing
The state’s resignation rate (4a) for FY 16-17 is 5.32%. This is 0.18% lo wer than the benchmark of 5.5% set in 2010. However, it is lower than the Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey
(JOLTS) data reported by the United States Bureau of Labor Statistics for 2016, which was 9.7% for state and local governments. The proposed targets reflect the new JOLTS reports and serve as

employment, and this data does not include turnover from layoffs, involuntary terminations, or retirement therefore it aligns with the JOLTS definition of “quits”.

How Are We Doing
Our view is that this KPM has long outlived its usefulness. Established 15-years ago, in 2002, as part of the Oregon Shines Initiative’s Oregon Benchmarks, led by the now long defunct Oregon
Progress Board, the measure attempts to determine the rate of savings (as compared to surrounding States) derived by Oregon’s Price Agreements. In the current procurement environment, in
most high dollar commodity areas, Oregon’s Price Agreements are part of national cooperative contracts, thus our pricing is essentially the same as other states and there is no discernible savings
to report. In addition, gathering the needed data from other states is quite labor intensive. We believe that continuation of this reporting is of no value to the Enterprise. DAS Procurement Services
is testing a new method for measuring savings and will recommend adoption of the new method when deemed adequate.

Factors Affecting Results
2016-17 saw an increase in number of severe claims along with a decrease in FTE.  The result is a 9% increase in severe claims per 100 FTE over 15/16.  Claims per 100 FTE involving only
