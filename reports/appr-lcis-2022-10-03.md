How Are We Doing
actual target
The Legislative Commission on Indian Services (LCIS) is a three-person legislative agency which strives to provide optimal service to the public, Oregon’s nine tribal governments, multiple executive
and legislative branch offices, government officials, and representatives of business and various organizations. Staff supports the 14-member Commission to work on ways to improve state-tribal
relations for the benefit of all Oregonians. We will continue to maximize resources to educate, advise, and assist across a vast spectrum of areas that touch on Tribes, Indians, and the State of Oregon
and fulfill a function to the best of our ability that does not exist anywhere else in the State.
Factors Affecting Results
Please note – LCIS has recently undergone several transitions in leadership resulting in the hiring of a permanent Executive Director in 2021. Additionally, LCIS has recently hired a physical
anthropologist, which is an entirely new position to the agency and the State. The position of State Physical Anthropologist was created to address specific needs identified by Tribes and State
agencies. Through the hiring of these two positions, LCIS is continuing to make advances in providing excellent service to the public, Oregon’s nine tribal governments, multiple executive and
legislative branch offices, government officials, and representatives of business and various organizations. Concurrently, LCIS achieved significant improvement in the customer satisfaction survey
this year over past years.  Additionally, we continue to receive frequent positive feedback from agency and tribal partners at meetings, events we sponsor, via email and in person. To more accurately
match the LCIS customer satisfaction survey with the somewhat unique services and products we provide to our partners, the LCIS survey varies in format somewhat from the measures in the
recording chart above in order to give us a more complete and useful picture of how we are doing. The LCIS survey is meant to get feedback about the priorities our state and tribal partners want
addressed and to have a guide to assess areas in which we provide the most assistance. Our goal is to provide the highest quality services and to focus on the services most valued by our very
diverse public, private, and tribal partners.
